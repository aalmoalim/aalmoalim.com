That the Travel Be Long Enough to Shorten Prayer

Ibn Rushd, may Allah have mercy on him, said: As for the reasonable meaning of the permissibility of breaking the fast in travel, it is hardship. And when it is not found in all travel, it must be that breaking the fast is permissible in travel in which there is hardship.

And when the companions were as if they agreed on a limit in that, it must be that it is compared to the limit in shortening prayer.(1)

Al-Kasani, may Allah have mercy on him, said: As for travel, what permits from it is absolute travel that is measured. And the basis for it is the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"So whoever among you is ill or on a journey, then [he should fast] a number of other days"
[Al-Baqarah 2:184]

Meaning: So whoever among you is ill, or on a journey and breaks his fast due to the excuse of illness and travel, then [he should fast] a number of other days. It indicates that illness and travel are the two causes of the concession. Then travel and illness - even if their mention is absolute in the verse - what is intended from them is the restricted, because absolute travel is not a cause of the concession, for the reality of travel is departure from the homeland, or appearance, and that occurs by departing to the estate, and the concession is not attached to it. So it is known that what permits is travel that is measured by a known measure, which is departure from the homeland with the intention of a journey of three days or more according to us.(2)

The duration of travel that permits breaking the fast according to the Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis is a journey of two moderate days without a night, or a journey of two nights without a day, or a journey of a day and a night.(3)

This equals 86.4 kilometers, and this has been explained previously in the Book of Prayer in the prayer of the traveler.

(1) "Bidayat al-Mujtahid" (1/408).

(2) "Al-Bada'i'" (2/630).

(3) "Al-Majmu'" (7/439) and "Al-Mughni" (4/138).
