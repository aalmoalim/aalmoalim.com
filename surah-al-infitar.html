Everything You Need to Know About Surah Al-Infitar (The Cleaving)

Introduction: Surah Al-Infitar addresses a dangerous human trait: arrogance toward Allah (الغرور بالله - al-ghurūr billāh). This is when a person commits sins or neglects obligations, and because Allah doesn't immediately punish them, they assume Allah is pleased with them or has forgotten about them. This surah exposes this delusion and warns against it, showing that Allah's delay in punishment is not approval but rather a test or a delay until the Day of Judgment.

The Main Theme: This surah specifically addresses those who become arrogant because of Allah's forbearance and generosity. When they sin and aren't immediately punished, they think Allah is pleased with them. This is a dangerous delusion that the surah seeks to correct.

The Opening: The First Moments of the Day of Judgment

إِذَا السَّمَاءُ انفَطَرَتْ
"When the sky breaks apart"
[Al-Infitar 82:1]

The surah begins with the first moments of the Day of Judgment - the moment of shock for the arrogant person. The word انفَطَرَتْ (infatarat) means the first cracking or splitting - the beginning of the sky breaking apart.

The Meaning of "Infatarat": This is the first stage of splitting - the initial crack. It's like when you say "my heart broke" from extreme grief - it means it was subjected to such intense pressure that it couldn't bear it and broke. What pressure did the vast, strong heavens face that caused them to break?

وَإِذَا الْكَوَاكِبُ انتَثَرَتْ
"And when the stars fall, dispersing"
[Al-Infitar 82:2]

The planets and stars will scatter and fall. Everything will go out of order. Allah, who holds the sky from falling on the earth except by His permission, will release His hold. The entire cosmic system will change.

وَإِذَا الْبِحَارُ فُجِّرَتْ
"And when the seas are burst forth"
[Al-Infitar 82:3]

The seas that bring moisture and life will be burst open and turned into fire. There will be no refuge on that Day.

وَإِذَا الْقُبُورُ بُعْثِرَتْ
"And when the graves are turned inside out"
[Al-Infitar 82:4]

This is the beginning of the reckoning. The graves will be turned inside out, and the dead will emerge. The arrogant person who thought there would be no accountability after death will be shocked when the grave is opened.

The Shock: Imagine a criminal who committed a crime and lived safely in his house, playing and enjoying himself, when suddenly someone knocks on the door. These are moments of terror and panic for every criminal. Similarly, the arrogant person who never thought about the Day of Reckoning will be shocked when the graves are opened.

The Consequence: Every Soul Will Know

عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّا قَدَّمَتْ وَأَخَّرَتْ
"A soul will [then] know what it has put forth and kept back"
[Al-Infitar 82:5]

After these four opening verses describing the shock, Allah states the consequence: every soul will know what it has done and what it has delayed.

Two Types of Deeds:

  1. What it put forth (qaddamat): The sins it committed or good deeds it did
  2. What it delayed (akhkharat): The obligations it delayed, thinking it could do them later

The arrogant person lived a life of carelessness, not thinking about the value of their actions. They didn't consider whether what they did was halal or haram. They didn't think about whether they should perform an act of worship now or delay it. They lived a life of heedlessness, full of play and amusement.

Examples of Delayed Obligations:

  • Waking up at 8 AM and not caring that they missed Fajr prayer
  • Delaying Asr prayer after returning from work
  • Delaying Zakat beyond its time
  • Delaying maintaining family ties
  • Delaying any obligatory act, thinking it can be done later

All of these will be accounted for. The person will be informed of everything they delayed.

Scholars also explain that أَخَّرَتْ (akhkharat) can mean "what bad precedent they set" - when a person establishes a bad practice that others follow, they continue to receive the burden of sins even after death, as people continue to follow their bad example.

The Question: What Made You Arrogant?

يَا أَيُّهَا الْإِنسَانُ مَا غَرَّكَ بِرَبِّكَ الْكَرِيمِ
"O mankind, what has deceived you concerning your Lord, the Generous"
[Al-Infitar 82:6]

Allah addresses the arrogant person before they wake up to the first shock on the Day of Judgment. This question should make a person with a sound heart break, just as the heavens broke.

The Meaning of "Ghurūr" (Arrogance/Deception):

  • From the word غَرَّ (gharra) - to pour continuously, like water flowing
  • The arrogant person pours out in sin continuously, without care
  • Arrogance means believing something is more or less than its true value
  • It's like when Iblis deceived Adam - he made them think the tree was more valuable than it was

The Foolishness of the Arrogant

Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) said when he heard this verse: "What deceived him? By Allah, his foolishness and ignorance!"

The Foolish Thinking: The arrogant person thinks: "When Allah didn't immediately punish me for my sin, He must be pleased with me or will leave me alone."

This is the thinking of the Jews, as Allah mentions in the Quran. They would say: "Why doesn't Allah punish us for what we say?" - thinking that the absence of immediate punishment means approval.

The Story of the People of the Sabbath

There's a famous story about a village by the sea where fish would come abundantly on Saturday (their Sabbath, when fishing was forbidden) but not on other days. One man dared to openly disobey Allah and caught a fish on Saturday. His neighbor warned him to fear Allah, and the man became so afraid of Allah's punishment that he left his house, thinking lightning would strike.

When a week passed and nothing happened, he returned and started fishing with the first man. The sin spread throughout the village because they thought: "Since Allah didn't punish us immediately, He must be pleased with us."

Critical Lesson: The absence of immediate punishment does NOT mean Allah is pleased. Allah says: "Do those who do evil deeds think they can outstrip Us? Evil is what they judge" (Al-Ankabut 29:4).

Allah gives respite but does not forget. The arrogant person who sins continuously, thinking they've escaped punishment, will be exposed on the Day of Judgment if they don't repent.

Allah's Generosity and Forbearance

الَّذِي خَلَقَكَ فَسَوَّاكَ فَعَدَلَكَ
"Who created you, proportioned you, and balanced you"
[Al-Infitar 82:7]

Allah reminds the person of His favors: He created you, formed you in the best form, and balanced you. He gave you all these blessings. Did His generosity make you arrogant?

Analogy: Imagine a father who gives his son money, sends him to the best school, spends on his education, and provides everything. Then the son doesn't study, fails at the end of the year, and the father asks: "Why didn't you study? Didn't I give you money? Didn't I provide everything for you?"

Generosity doesn't justify arrogance - it demands gratitude and obedience.

فِي أَيِّ صُورَةٍ مَّا شَاءَ رَكَّبَكَ
"In whatever form He willed has He assembled you"
[Al-Infitar 82:8]

Allah could have created you in any form He willed - as a donkey, a monkey, or any other creature. He has the power to transform you into any shape. You don't even control your own appearance - how can you control your destiny? How can you not fear your fate when you don't even control your own form?

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "If you see Allah giving a servant from His blessings while he persists in disobedience, know that this is a gradual leading to destruction (istidrāj)."

We seek refuge in Allah from this. Sometimes Allah's blessings while we're in sin are not a sign of approval but a gradual punishment - leading us further into destruction.

Denying the Day of Judgment

كَلَّا بَلْ تُكَذِّبُونَ بِالدِّينِ
"No! But you deny the Day of Recompense"
[Al-Infitar 82:9]

The fundamental problem is that you don't think about the Day of Recompense (الدِّين - ad-dīn). The word "dīn" here means the Day of Submission - the day when every action you did will be returned to you, and you'll be held accountable for it.

وَإِنَّ عَلَيْكُمْ لَحَافِظِينَ
"And indeed, over you are keepers [angels]"
[Al-Infitar 82:10]
كِرَامًا كَاتِبِينَ
"Noble and recording"
[Al-Infitar 82:11]
يَعْلَمُونَ مَا تَفْعَلُونَ
"They know whatever you do"
[Al-Infitar 82:12]

How can you deny the Day of Judgment when you have noble recording angels over you? They never leave you. They record every action you do. They even record your intentions:

  • If you intend a good deed and do it, it's written as ten good deeds
  • If you intend a good deed but don't do it, Allah writes it as one good deed
  • If you intend a bad deed but abandon it, Allah writes it as one good deed

They know everything you do - every hidden glance at what's forbidden, every source of your wealth (whether halal or haram), every action.

The Final Outcome

إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ لَفِي نَعِيمٍ
"Indeed, the righteous will be in pleasure"
[Al-Infitar 82:13]
وَإِنَّ الْفُجَّارَ لَفِي جَحِيمٍ
"And indeed, the wicked will be in Hellfire"
[Al-Infitar 82:14]

The righteous (الْأَبْرَار - al-abrār) will be in bliss - in this world through obedience to Allah, in the grave, and in the Hereafter.

The wicked (الْفُجَّار - al-fujjār) will be in Hellfire. Notice the precision of the Quran's word choice: فُجَّار comes from فَجَر (fajar) - to burst forth, like water bursting from a spring. The wicked person bursts forth in sin because they don't care. They don't care about prayer times, don't care if something is halal or haram, don't care about anything. They're arrogant because of Allah's forbearance with them.

يَصْلَوْنَهَا يَوْمَ الدِّينِ
"They will [enter to] burn therein on the Day of Recompense"
[Al-Infitar 82:15]
وَمَا هُمْ عَنْهَا بِغَائِبِينَ
"And they will not be absent from it"
[Al-Infitar 82:16]

They will burn in it on the Day of Recompense, and they will never be absent from it. Just as they were never absent from gatherings of amusement and were absent from gatherings of knowledge and religion, they will be absent from Paradise and never absent from the Fire.

The Contrast: The arrogant person who never thought about religion, never asked "What do I know about my religion? What do I know about prayer, Zakat, fasting?" - this person will remain in the Fire forever because they never occupied themselves with thinking about the Day of Judgment.

What Do You Know About the Day of Judgment?

وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا يَوْمُ الدِّينِ
"And what can make you know what is the Day of Recompense?"
[Al-Infitar 82:17]
ثُمَّ مَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا يَوْمُ الدِّينِ
"Then, what can make you know what is the Day of Recompense?"
[Al-Infitar 82:18]

Allah repeats this question twice to emphasize its importance. No matter how much you read or hear about the Day of Judgment, you cannot imagine its horror with your limited understanding. You cannot imagine:

  • The sky falling on the earth
  • Fetuses falling from wombs
  • A nursing mother throwing away her infant to save herself
  • A father kicking his son away, saying "Get away from me!"
  • People fleeing from their own brothers, mothers, fathers, wives, and children

The Story of Abdullah ibn Jad'an: A'ishah (may Allah be pleased with her) asked the Prophet (peace be upon him) about Abdullah ibn Jad'an, who used to feed people in the pre-Islamic period but died before accepting Islam. She asked if that would help him on the Day of Judgment. The Prophet said: "No, O A'ishah. He never said once: 'O my Lord, forgive me on the Day of Judgment.'"

He never occupied his mind with the Day of Judgment. He never feared this day. He never thought before sleeping: "What will I do on the Bridge (as-Sirat)?" He never asked himself: "Will Allah hold me accountable for this wealth I took from haram sources?"

Key Lessons and Warnings

1. Don't Be Deceived by Allah's Forbearance

When you sin and aren't immediately punished, this doesn't mean Allah is pleased with you. It could be a test, a delay, or even a gradual leading to destruction (istidrāj).

2. The Day of Judgment Will Come

No matter how much you ignore it or try to forget it, the Day of Judgment will definitely come. Every death you witness, every plant or animal that dies - all remind you that you too will die and face accountability.

3. Everything Is Recorded

Noble recording angels are with you at all times, recording every action, every intention, every glance. Nothing is hidden from Allah.

4. Don't Delay Obligations

Every obligation has its time. Delaying prayers, Zakat, or other obligations is a form of arrogance. You will be asked about everything you delayed.

5. The Two Paths

There are only two outcomes: the righteous in bliss or the wicked in Hellfire. Your actions determine which path you take.

6. The Promise of Security

Allah does not combine two securities or two fears for a servant. If you fear Allah in this world, He will give you security in the Hereafter. If you feel secure from Allah in this world, He will make you fearful in the Hereafter.

Conclusion

Surah Al-Infitar is a powerful warning against arrogance toward Allah. It exposes the dangerous delusion that the absence of immediate punishment means Allah's approval. The surah shows that:

  • Allah's forbearance is not approval
  • The Day of Judgment will definitely come
  • Everything is being recorded
  • Delayed obligations will be accounted for
  • There are only two outcomes: bliss or Hellfire

The surah calls people to wake up from their heedlessness, to think about the Day of Judgment, to hasten to repentance, to fulfill their obligations on time, and to never be deceived by Allah's generosity and forbearance.

We must all examine ourselves: Are we among those who delay obligations? Do we think that because we haven't been punished immediately, Allah is pleased with us? Do we occupy our minds with the Day of Judgment? These are questions we must ask ourselves regularly.

Source: This comprehensive guide is based on the tafsir (exegesis) khutbah (sermon) of Dr. Ahmed Abd al-Moneim from his course "Basa'ir Qur'aniyya" (Quranic Insights), specifically covering Surah Al-Infitar.
