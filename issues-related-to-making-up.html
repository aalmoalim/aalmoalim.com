Issues Related to Making Up

First: One Who Delayed Making Up Ramadan

One who delayed making up Ramadan - and likewise vow and expiation - due to an excuse, by the continuation of one's illness or travel or unconsciousness or her menstruation or postpartum bleeding or her pregnancy or breastfeeding and the like, until death, and one was not able to make it up, there is nothing required upon one, and it is not fasted for one, and feeding is not done for one. This is by agreement of the four schools, because it is an obligation that one was not able to do until death, so its ruling is dropped, like Hajj.

In one narration according to the Hanbalis: Feeding is done for one, like the decrepit old man.

Abu al-Khattab, may Allah have mercy on him, said: It is possible that fasting is required for one or expiation, but the school is the first.(1)

Second: If One Broke One's Fast with an Excuse

If one broke one's fast with an excuse, and the excuse was removed, and one was able to make it up, and one did not make it up until one died, the jurists differed: Is feeding done for one, and it is not permissible to fast for one, or is fasting for one permissible? With detail in that:

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Malikis, Shafi'is in the correct view of the school, and Hanbalis in their school held that feeding a poor person for each day is required in one's estate, and fasting by one's guardian for one is not valid, because fasting is obligatory by the origin of the law, and it is not made up for one, because representation does not enter it in life, so likewise after death, like prayer.

Al-Shafi'i in the old view - and it is the choice of Al-Nawawi, and it is the view of Abu al-Khattab from the Hanbalis - held that it is permissible for one's guardian to fast for one, and that is valid, and it counts for one instead of feeding, and one's responsibility is cleared by it. However, fasting is not required upon the guardian. Rather, it is to his choice, due to the hadith of 'Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "Whoever dies and has fasting required upon one, one's guardian fasts for one."(2)

The Hanbalis said: Whoever dies and has a vowed fast required upon one, one's guardian fasts for one on the correct view of the school, and it is the choice of Al-Nawawi from the Shafi'is.

It was said in "Al-Insaf": It is recommended for the guardian to do it. And know that if one has an estate, doing it is required, so it is recommended for the guardian to fast, and one has the right to give to one who fasts for one from one's estate, for each day a poor person. If one does not have an estate, nothing is required upon one.(3)

The Hanbalis and Al-Nawawi used as evidence the following:

1. The hadith of Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: A man came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, my mother died and has fasting a month required upon her [and in one narration: 'fasting a vow'] shall I make it up for her?" He said: "Yes." He said: "The debt of Allah is more entitled to be paid."(4)

2. The hadith of Buraydah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: While I was sitting with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, a woman came to him and said: I gave charity to my mother with a slave girl, and she died. He said: So he said: "Your reward has become due, and inheritance has returned it to you." She said: O Messenger of Allah, she had fasting a month required upon her. Shall I fast for her? He said: "Fast for her..." the hadith.(5)

3. The hadith of Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both:

"A woman rode the sea and vowed: If Allah saves her, she will fast a month. So Allah saved her, and she did not fast until she died. So her daughter or her sister came to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and he commanded her to fast for her."(6)

Imam Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Issue: He said: (And whoever vows to fast and dies before coming to it, one's heirs from one's relatives fast for one, and likewise everything that was from a vow of obedience.

Meaning: Whoever vows Hajj or fasting or charity or freeing a slave or i'tikaf or prayer or other than it from acts of obedience, and dies before doing it, the guardian does it for one. And from Ahmad regarding prayer: Prayer is not performed for the dead, because it has no substitute in any case. As for the rest of the actions, it is permissible for the guardian to represent one in them, and it is not required upon one. However, it is recommended for one to do that, on the way of maintaining ties with one and doing good. And Ibn 'Abbas issued a fatwa regarding that in a woman who vowed to walk to Quba', and she died and did not make it up, that her daughter walk for her. And Sa'id reported from Sufyan from 'Abd al-Karim ibn Abi Umayyah "that he asked Ibn 'Abbas about a vow that was upon his mother from i'tikaf. He said: Fast for her and do i'tikaf for her."

And he said: Abu al-Ahwas reported to us from Ibrahim ibn Muhajir from 'Amir ibn Shu'ayb "that 'Aishah did i'tikaf for her brother 'Abd al-Rahman after he died."

And Malik said: No one walks for anyone, and no one prays or fasts for one, and likewise the rest of bodily actions, by analogy to prayer.

And Al-Shafi'i said: One makes up Hajj for one, and one does not make up prayer, one statement. And one does not make up fasting in one of the two views, and one feeds for one for each day a poor person, because Ibn 'Umar said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'Whoever dies and has fasting a month required upon one, let feeding be done for one in place of each day a poor person.'" Ibn Majah narrated it.

And the people of Zahir said: Making up is required upon one's guardian, by the apparent of the reports that came in it.

And the majority of the people of knowledge are on that that is not required upon the guardian, except if it is a right in wealth, and the dead person has an estate, and the command of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in this is carried on recommendation and preference, by the evidence of indicators in the report:

From them: That the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, compared it to debt, and making up debt for the dead person is not required upon the heir as long as one does not leave an estate to make it up with.

And from them: That the questioner asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, whether one does that or not, and his answer differs by the difference of what one's question requires. If its requirement is the question about permissibility, the command in its answer requires permissibility. And if the question is about validity, one's command requires validity, like their statement: "Do we pray in sheep pens?" He said: "Pray in sheep pens." And if their question was about obligation, one's command requires obligation, like their statement: "Do we perform ablution from camel meat?" He said: "Perform ablution from camel meat." And the questioner's question in our issue was about validity, so the Prophet's, peace and blessings be upon him, command to do it requires it, nothing else.

And our evidence for the permissibility of fasting for the dead person: What 'Aishah narrated that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever dies and has fasting required upon one, one's guardian fasts for one."

And from Ibn 'Abbas, who said: A man came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: O Messenger of Allah, my mother died and has fasting a month required upon her. Shall I fast for her? He said: "What do you think if your mother had a debt, would you pay it?" He said: Yes. He said: "The debt of Allah is more entitled to be paid." And in one narration he said: A woman came to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: O Messenger of Allah, my mother died and has fasting required upon her. Shall I fast for her? He said: "What do you think if your mother had a debt and you paid it, would that pay it for her?" She said: Yes. He said: "Fast for your mother." Agreed upon.

And from Ibn 'Abbas: "That Sa'd ibn 'Ubadah al-Ansari sought a legal opinion from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, regarding a vow that was upon his mother, and she died before making it up, and he gave him a legal opinion that he make it up, and it became a Sunnah after that."

And from him: "A man came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: My sister vowed to perform Hajj, and she died. So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: If she had a debt, would you pay it? He said: Yes. He said: Pay Allah, for He is more entitled to payment." Al-Bukhari narrated both of them. And this is explicit in fasting and Hajj, and absolute in vow, and what is other than what is mentioned in the hadith is analogized to it. And the hadith of Ibn 'Umar is in obligatory fasting by the origin of the law, and it is determined to carry it on that, combining between the two hadiths. And if contradiction is assumed, our hadiths are more authentic and more numerous and more entitled to precedence.

If this is established, then it is better that one's heir make up the vow for one. If one's heir makes it up, it counts for one, just as if one makes up one's debt for one, for the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, compared it to debt and analogized it to it. And because what the heir makes up is only a gift from one, and other than one is like one in the gift. And if the vow is in wealth, it is attached to one's estate.(7)
