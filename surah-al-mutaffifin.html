Everything You Need to Know About Surah Al-Mutaffifin (Those Who Give Less)

Introduction: Surah Al-Mutaffifin holds special importance as it was revealed during the transition from Makkah to Madinah - either at the end of Makkah or the beginning of Madinah. This timing is significant because it marks the transition from a small group of believers to building an Islamic state. The surah addresses economic justice and establishes that Islam will govern all aspects of life, including economics, politics, and social affairs. It rejects secularization and makes clear that Islam is not confined to the mosque but extends to all areas of human life.

Historical Context: The surah comes between Surah Al-Infitar and Surah Al-Inshiqaq - both of which describe the Day of Judgment in vivid detail. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever wishes to look at the Day of Judgment as if seeing it with their own eyes, let them read: 'When the sun is wrapped up' (At-Takwir), 'When the sky is split open' (Al-Infitar), and 'When the sky is cleft asunder' (Al-Inshiqaq)."

The placement of Al-Mutaffifin between these two surahs indicates that the issue it addresses - economic justice and giving people their due rights - cannot be fully resolved except through the Day of Judgment and the fear of accountability.

The Opening: Woe to Those Who Give Less

وَيْلٌ لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ
"Woe to those who give less [than due]"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:1]

The surah opens with a severe warning. The word وَيْل (wayl) is a form of threat and warning. Notice that it says لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ (al-mutaffifīn) - using the noun form (ism) rather than the verb form (fi'l). This indicates that giving less than due has become a permanent characteristic, not just an occasional act. It has become part of their nature.

The Meaning of "Mutaffifin":

  • From the word طَفِيف (ṭafīf) - meaning something small or slight
  • They take small amounts from people, thinking it won't be noticed
  • It's a form of theft, oppression, and lowliness
  • Even small amounts are significant because the principle is that people's wealth is sacred

Even a small amount taken unjustly is haram. The word "ṭafīf" (small) is contrasted with "aẓīm" (great) - what they consider small and insignificant is actually great in the sight of Allah.

The Practice of Giving Less

الَّذِينَ إِذَا اكْتَالُوا عَلَى النَّاسِ يَسْتَوْفُونَ
"Who, when they take a measure from people, take in full"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:2]
وَإِذَا كَالُوهُمْ أَو وَّزَنُوهُمْ يُخْسِرُونَ
"But when they give by measure or by weight to [others], they give less than due"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:3]

These people have two scales - a common expression now: "measuring with two scales." When they take their right from people, they use the full measure to take everything they're owed (or even more). But when they give to people, they use a smaller measure to give less than due.

Notice the Preposition: The verse says عَلَى النَّاس (alā an-nās - "on people") rather than مِنَ النَّاس (min an-nās - "from people"). The preposition عَلَى (alā) indicates:

  • They see themselves as above people - a higher class
  • There's an element of oppression and force, even when taking their legitimate right
  • They don't recognize that others have rights - notice the second verse doesn't say "gave to them" but "measured/weighed them" - as if they can't admit the other person has a right

The Solution: Remembering the Day of Judgment

أَلَا يَظُنُّ أُولَٰئِكَ أَنَّهُم مَّبْعُوثُونَ
"Do those not think that they will be resurrected"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:4]
لِيَوْمٍ عَظِيمٍ
"For a Great Day"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:5]
يَوْمَ يَقُومُ النَّاسُ لِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ
"A Day when [all] mankind will stand before the Lord of the worlds"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:6]

The solution to this problem is to remember the Day of Judgment. If they truly believed they would be resurrected for a Great Day, they would never cheat people. The word يَظُنُّ (yaẓunnu - "think") is used - even just thinking about it should be enough to prevent this behavior.

Key Point: The verse says "A Day when mankind will stand before the Lord of the worlds" - not "before each other." Allah Himself will take the rights of the oppressed. In a hadith qudsi, Allah says: "Three I will be their opponent on the Day of Judgment: a man who made a covenant in My name then broke it, a man who sold a free person and consumed the price, and a man who hired a worker but didn't give him his due."

Allah Himself will be the opponent of those who cheat others. What they considered small and insignificant will be dealt with on a Great Day.

The Four Groups in Society

The surah describes four groups of people, which scholars have identified as the main groups that will oppose the establishment of an Islamic state:

1. Al-Mutaffifin (Those Who Give Less)

Those who control the economy through cheating and fraud. They have corrupted scales and measures designed for cheating.

2. Al-Fujjār (The Wicked)

Those who indulge in desires and spread immorality in society.

كَلَّا ۖ إِنَّ كِتَابَ الْفُجَّارِ لَفِي سِجِّينٍ
"No! Indeed, the record of the wicked is in sijjīn"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:7]
وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا سِجِّينٌ
"And what can make you know what is sijjīn?"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:8]
كِتَابٌ مَّرْقُومٌ
"It is a register inscribed"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:9]

سِجِّين (sijjīn) comes from the root meaning prison, narrowness, and lowliness. The record of the wicked is in sijjīn - a place of constriction and punishment. The word مَّرْقُوم (marqūm) means inscribed or sealed - it cannot be increased or decreased, unlike their cheating scales.

وَيْلٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ لِّلْمُكَذِّبِينَ
"Woe, that Day, to the deniers"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:10]
الَّذِينَ يُكَذِّبُونَ بِيَوْمِ الدِّينِ
"Who deny the Day of Recompense"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:11]
وَمَا يُكَذِّبُ بِهِ إِلَّا كُلُّ مُعْتَدٍ أَثِيمٍ
"And none deny it except every sinful transgressor"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:12]

Those who deny the Day of Judgment are described as:

  • Mu'tadin (Transgressor): One who transgresses against the rights of people
  • Athīm (Sinful): One who commits sins against Allah - the word أَثِيم (athīm) is in the form that indicates it has become a permanent characteristic
إِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِ آيَاتُنَا قَالَ أَسَاطِيرُ الْأَوَّلِينَ
"When Our verses are recited to him, he says, 'Legends of the former peoples'"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:13]

When the believers come to them and recite the Quran, they dismiss it as "legends of former peoples." This shows the believers' duty to go to people and recite the Quran to them, even to the wicked.

كَلَّا ۖ بَلْ ۜ رَانَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِم مَّا كَانُوا يَكْسِبُونَ
"No! Rather, the stain has covered their hearts of that which they were earning"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:14]

The word رَان (rān) means a covering or seal that prevents understanding. Their hearts are covered because of what they earned through their sins. Each sin adds a black spot to the heart until it becomes completely sealed.

كَلَّا إِنَّهُمْ عَن رَّبِّهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَّمَحْجُوبُونَ
"No! Indeed, they will be veiled from their Lord that Day"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:15]
ثُمَّ إِنَّهُمْ لَصَالُو الْجَحِيمِ
"Then indeed, they will be thrown into Hellfire"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:16]

Just as the covering (رَان) prevented them from hearing the word of Allah in this world, it will prevent them from seeing the face of Allah in the Hereafter. The distance from hearing Allah's word equals the distance from seeing Allah's face. Then they will be thrown into Hellfire.

ثُمَّ يُقَالُ هَٰذَا الَّذِي كُنتُم بِهِ تُكَذِّبُونَ
"Then it will be said [to them], 'This is what you used to deny'"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:17]

This is the punishment of rebuke - being shown what they denied and being told: "This is what you used to deny."

3. Al-Abrār (The Righteous)

كَلَّا إِنَّ كِتَابَ الْأَبْرَارِ لَفِي عِلِّيِّينَ
"No! Indeed, the record of the righteous is in 'illiyyīn'"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:18]
وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا عِلِّيُّونَ
"And what can make you know what is 'illiyyūn?"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:19]
كِتَابٌ مَّرْقُومٌ
"It is a register inscribed"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:20]
يَشْهَدُهُ الْمُقَرَّبُونَ
"Which is witnessed by those brought near [to Allah]"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:21]

The record of the righteous is in عِلِّيِّين ('illiyyīn) - a place of elevation and honor, the opposite of sijjīn. It is witnessed by the angels brought near to Allah. This is a form of honor and pride for the righteous - the noble angels witness their record.

The Angels' Testimony: When Allah told the angels He would create a vicegerent on earth, they said: "Will You place upon it one who causes corruption therein and sheds blood?" They mentioned the conflict and bloodshed that would occur. But when they see the righteous who protected people's rights, didn't shed blood, and gave their wealth to others, Allah shows them off to the angels, saying: "Look at these - they struggled against themselves, gave to others, and gave up their rights for people. These are truly the righteous."

إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ لَفِي نَعِيمٍ
"Indeed, the righteous will be in pleasure"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:22]
عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ يَنظُرُونَ
"On adorned couches, observing"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:23]

The righteous will be in bliss - in this world through obedience, in the grave, and in the Hereafter. They will be on adorned couches, looking at the face of Allah or at all the blessings around them.

تَعْرِفُ فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ نَضْرَةَ النَّعِيمِ
"You will recognize in their faces the radiance of pleasure"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:24]

You will recognize in their faces the radiance of pleasure - just one glance at the face of Allah transforms their faces. The word تَعْرِف (ta'rif) means you'll know immediately with just a glance - you don't need to look closely. Their faces are different because they looked at the face of Allah.

يُسْقَوْنَ مِن رَّحِيقٍ مَّخْتُومٍ
"They will be given to drink [pure] wine [which was] sealed"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:25]
خِتَامُهُ مِسْكٌ ۚ وَفِي ذَٰلِكَ فَلْيَتَنَافَسِ الْمُتَنَافِسُونَ
"Its seal [will be] musk. And for that let the competitors compete"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:26]

They will be given pure wine sealed with musk. When they open it, they smell musk. Some say the musk is at the beginning (making them eager to drink) and at the end (making them want more). This is what deserves competition - not cheating people or competing in worldly gains, but competing in good deeds and righteousness.

وَمِزَاجُهُ مِن تَسْنِيمٍ
"And its mixture is of Tasnīm"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:27]
عَيْنًا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا الْمُقَرَّبُونَ
"A spring from which those near [to Allah] drink"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:28]

تَسْنِيم (Tasnīm) comes from the root meaning elevation. It's a spring in the highest place in Paradise. Ibn Abbas and Ibn Mas'ud said: "Tasnīm is a spring from which the righteous drink mixed, and from which those brought near drink pure."

The Levels of the Righteous:

  • Al-Abrār (The Righteous): They drink from Tasnīm mixed - a small amount is added to their drink
  • Al-Muqarrabūn (Those Brought Near): They drink from Tasnīm pure - they live near this spring and drink directly from it

This shows the precise levels even among the righteous. As you purify your deeds and make your heart purely for Allah, you rise in levels. The principle: "Whoever purifies, it is purified for them."

4. Al-Mujrimūn (The Criminals - The Mockers)

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ أَجْرَمُوا كَانُوا مِنَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا يَضْحَكُونَ
"Indeed, those who committed crimes used to laugh at those who believed"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:29]
وَإِذَا مَرُّوا بِهِمْ يَتَغَامَزُونَ
"And when they passed by them, they would exchange derisive glances"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:30]
وَإِذَا انقَلَبُوا إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهِمُ انقَلَبُوا فَكِهِينَ
"And when they returned to their people, they would return jesting"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:31]

This is a complete class of people whose entire role is to mock the believers. They laugh at them, exchange derisive glances when they pass by, and return to their families continuing the mockery. They want to spread this mockery to their homes so their wives and children also mock the believers.

The Weapon of Mockery: The people of falsehood fight the people of faith with two weapons:

  1. Spreading Desires: To attract people to falsehood
  2. Mockery: To make people fear being ridiculed if they follow the truth

This mockery is not a small matter - billions are spent on media programs, films, and shows whose main purpose is to mock the believers and turn people away from faith.

وَإِذَا رَأَوْهُمْ قَالُوا إِنَّ هَٰؤُلَاءِ لَضَالُّونَ
"And when they saw them, they would say, 'Indeed, those are lost'"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:32]

The mockery escalates from laughter to derisive glances to direct accusations: "These are lost." They accuse the believers of being misguided while they themselves are on falsehood.

وَمَا أُرْسِلُوا عَلَيْهِمْ حَافِظِينَ
"But they had not been sent as guardians over them"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:33]

They weren't sent to watch over or judge the believers. Their role is not to mock and ridicule.

فَالْيَوْمَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنَ الْكُفَّارِ يَضْحَكُونَ
"So today those who believed are laughing at the disbelievers"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:34]
عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ يَنظُرُونَ
"On adorned couches, observing"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:35]

On the Day of Judgment, the tables will be turned. The believers will be in Paradise on adorned couches, looking at the disbelievers being punished. Windows will be opened for them to see the punishment of those who mocked them, and their hearts will be healed.

هَلْ ثُوِّبَ الْكُفَّارُ مَا كَانُوا يَفْعَلُونَ
"Have the disbelievers [not] been rewarded [this Day] for what they used to do?"
[Al-Mutaffifin 83:36]

Yes, they will receive their punishment for what they did. They will realize that all their efforts were in vain and that the believers were on the truth all along.

Key Lessons and Principles

1. Islam Governs All Aspects of Life

From the first moment in Madinah, Islam made clear that it would govern all aspects of life - economics, politics, social affairs. It rejects secularization and refuses to be confined to the mosque.

2. Economic Justice is Fundamental

Strong states are built on strong economies. Islam must establish just economic systems with fixed standards and measures. The people of faith must intervene to fix economic corruption.

3. Fixed Standards and Measures

There must be fixed, unchanging standards for evaluation - both in religious and worldly matters. People of faith must establish these standards, or people will turn to others who give them their rights.

4. The Fear of the Hereafter is Essential

No matter how many laws, cameras, or monitoring systems you put in place, real change will only come through the fear of the Hereafter. People will always find ways around laws, but the fear of standing before Allah prevents cheating.

5. The Four Groups That Oppose Islamic State

When building an Islamic state, you will face four groups:

  1. Those who control corrupt economies - They fear Islamic rules will affect their fraudulent systems
  2. Those who spread immorality - They fear Islamic rules will restrict their desires
  3. The righteous - Those who work for reform
  4. The mockers - Those who use media and mockery to turn people away from faith

6. The Duty to Go to People

The believers must go to people, enter the markets, and recite the Quran to them. Real change in society will only come when the righteous go to people with the verses of Allah.

7. The Levels of Treatment

Scholars mention four levels of treatment:

  1. Best: Give people more than their due, take less than your due
  2. Good: One scale - give full rights, take full rights (justice)
  3. Acceptable: Take your full right, give their full right
  4. Forbidden: Take more than your due, give less than their due (the mutaffifin)

Conclusion

Surah Al-Mutaffifin is crucial for understanding the economic and social principles of Islam, especially in the context of building an Islamic state. It establishes that:

  • Islam governs all aspects of life, including economics
  • Economic justice requires fixed standards and measures
  • The fear of the Hereafter is essential for real change
  • People of faith must actively work to fix corruption in society
  • There are specific groups that will oppose Islamic governance
  • The believers must go to people and call them with the Quran

The surah shows that what people consider small and insignificant (ṭafīf) is actually great (aẓīm) in the sight of Allah, and will be dealt with on a Great Day when all people stand before the Lord of the worlds.
