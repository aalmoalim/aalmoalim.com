Chewing Gum

Ibn Hubayrah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: They have unanimously agreed that chewing gum that increases in strength by chewing is disliked in fasting.(1)

It came in "Al-Mudawwanah al-Kubra": Regarding tasting food and chewing gum and something entering the throat of the fasting person.

(I said): Did Malik dislike that the fasting person taste something like honey and salt and what is similar to it while fasting and not enter it into his stomach? (He said): Yes, he does not taste anything. (He said): And I asked him about a man who has a cavity in his mouth, so he treats it in Ramadan and expels the medicine. (He said): He does not do that. And Malik disliked for one who makes strings, strings of the heel, that he pass that in his mouth, chewing it or smoothing it with his mouth. (Ibn al-Qasim said): And Malik disliked for the fasting person chewing gum and chewing food for the child. (I said): What do you think about the fasting person if a fly enters his throat, or something between his teeth, a piece of a grain or the like, and he swallows it with his saliva? (Malik said): There is nothing upon him. (Malik said): And likewise if it was in prayer, it would not cut his prayer either. (Ibn Wahb) from Yunus ibn Yazid from Ibn Shihab that he disliked for the fasting person chewing gum, and 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah disliked that.(2)

Imam Al-Tahawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Our companions said: Chewing gum is disliked for the fasting person, and chewing food, except if there is no alternative for the child. And likewise Malik and al-Thawri without exception.

Al-Shafi'i said: I dislike gum, because it brings saliva, and if one chews it, it does not break one's fast.(3)

Al-Mawsili, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Chewing gum is disliked for the fasting person... due to what is in it of exposing one's fast to invalidation. And this is regarding gum that sticks together. As for if it is not sticking together, then it breaks one's fast, because it does not stick together except by the separation of parts that break off from it, and that invalidates fasting.(4)

Imam Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Al-Shafi'i said: And gum (al-'ilk) with the kasrah on the 'ayn is this known thing, and it is permissible to open the 'ayn and it would mean the action, which is chewing gum, and turning it...

Al-Shafi'i and the companions said: Gum is disliked for the fasting person, because it collects saliva and causes thirst and vomiting. Al-Bayhaqi narrated with his chain from Umm Habibah, the wife of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that she said: The fasting person does not chew gum. And the wording of Al-Shafi'i in "Mukhtasar al-Muzni": (And I dislike gum, because it milks the mouth). The author of "Al-Hawi" said: This wording was narrated with the letter jim and with the letter ha'. So whoever said it with jim, its meaning is: It collects saliva, so perhaps one swallows it, and that invalidates fasting in one of the two views and is disliked in the other. He said: And it has been said: Its meaning is: It sweetens the mouth and removes the bad breath. He said: And whoever said it with ha', its meaning is: It sucks saliva and exhausts the fasting person, so it causes thirst.

Our companions said: One does not break one's fast by mere gum, nor by the descent of saliva from it to one's stomach. If it crumbles and something from its substance reaches one's stomach intentionally, one breaks one's fast. And if one doubts that, one does not break one's fast. And if its taste descends into one's stomach or its smell, without its substance, one does not break one's fast, because that taste is by the proximity of saliva to it. This is the school, and with this the majority have decided. Al-Darimi reported a view from Ibn al-Qattan that if one swallows saliva containing its taste, one breaks one's fast, and it is nothing.(5)

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Chewing strong gum from which nothing dissolves is disliked. As for what dissolves from it, parts whose taste one finds in one's throat, chewing it is not permissible except if one does not swallow one's saliva. If one swallows it and finds its taste in one's throat, it breaks one's fast. And if one finds the taste of what nothing dissolves from it in one's throat, there are two views:

One of them: It breaks one's fast, like kohl.

The second: It does not break one's fast, because mere taste does not break one's fast, like one who smears the inside of his feet with colocynth and finds its bitterness in his throat, it does not break his fast.(6)

(1) "Al-Ifsah" (1/421), and see: "Al-Bada'i'" (2/663), "Al-Taj wa-l-Iklil" (3/205), "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/447), "Al-Umm" (2/133), "Al-Majmu'" (7/590), and "Al-Mughni" (4/166).

(2) "Al-Mudawwanah al-Kubra" (1/199).

(3) "Mukhtasar Ikhtilaf al-'Ulama'" (2/40).

(4) "Al-Ikhtiyar" (1/173).

(5) "Al-Majmu'" (6/369).

(6) "Al-Kafi" (1/361).
