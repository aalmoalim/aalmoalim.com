Expiation for Intentional Eating or Drinking

The jurists differed regarding if a fasting person eats or drinks intentionally: is expiation and making up obligatory upon him, or is only making up obligatory upon him, and that expiation is only required due to intercourse?

The Hanafis and Malikis held that whoever breaks the fast intentionally by eating or drinking, making up and expiation are obligatory upon him, because fasting legally is abstention from food and intercourse. So if something is established in one aspect of that, it is established in its equivalent. What unites them is violating the sanctity of the month by what invalidates fasting intentionally.

However, Abu Hanifah required in the obligation of expiation that what is consumed be something that is nourishing or used for treatment. As for if one swallows a pebble or seed, expiation is not obligatory upon him. From Malik regarding one who swallows a pebble or the like, there are two narrations.

And the expiation is like the expiation for zihar.(1)

The Shafi'is and Hanbalis held that whoever eats or drinks intentionally, only making up is obligatory upon him, without expiation.

Ibn Rushd, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The reason for their disagreement is their disagreement regarding the permissibility of analogizing one who breaks the fast by eating and drinking to one who breaks the fast by intercourse.

Whoever saw that their similarity in it is one, which is violating the sanctity of fasting, made their ruling one.

Whoever saw that expiation - even though it is a punishment for violating the sanctity - is more appropriate for intercourse than for other than it, and that is because the purpose of punishment is deterrence, and the greater punishment may be placed for what the soul is more inclined to, and it is more dominant for it than other crimes, even if the crimes are similar, since the purpose of that is people's adherence to the laws, and that they be good and just, as the Exalted said:

"Fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may become righteous"
[Al-Baqarah 2:183]

He said: This severe expiation is specific to intercourse. This is if one sees analogy.

As for whoever does not see analogy, his matter is clear that it does not extend the ruling of intercourse to eating and drinking.

As for what Malik narrated in "Al-Muwatta'": that a man broke the fast in Ramadan, so the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, commanded him with the mentioned expiation, it is not a proof, because the narrator's statement "he broke the fast" is general, and the general does not have generality, so it is not taken from it. However, this is a statement that the narrator saw that the expiation was for the place of breaking the fast, and were it not for that, he would not have expressed it with this wording, and he would have mentioned the type of breaking the fast by which he broke the fast.(2)

However, the Hanafis said: Expiation is only obligatory upon him if he intended to fast then broke the fast. As for one who woke up in Ramadan not intending to fast, then ate or drank or had intercourse, making up that day is obligatory upon him and there is no expiation upon him.(3)

Imam al-Sarakhsi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: (And likewise, if one eats or drinks intentionally, making up and expiation are obligatory upon him according to us), and according to al-Shafi'i, may Allah have mercy on him: There is no expiation upon him, because the cause of the obligation of expiation by the text is intercourse that invalidates fasting. So if it were made obligatory for eating, it would be by analogy to intercourse, and there is no place for analogy in expiation. Do you not see that the causes of intercourse are not analogized to intercourse in it? And because sanctity is sometimes for the sake of worship, and sometimes for the absence of ownership. Then what relates to eating does not relate to intercourse when sanctity is for the absence of ownership, so likewise worship. And he argued with Hajj, for what relates to intercourse in it, which is the invalidation of the rite, does not relate to other prohibitions, so likewise fasting. What unites them is that this is an act of worship, and the greatest expiation in it is specific to intercourse.

(For us) is the hadith of Abu Hurayrah that a man said: O Messenger of Allah, I broke the fast in Ramadan. He said: "Without illness or travel?" He said: Yes. He said: "Free a slave." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, understood from his question breaking the fast by what necessitates it, like illness and travel. Abu Dawud mentioned that the man said: I drank in Ramadan. And 'Ali, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "Expiation is only in eating, drinking, and intercourse." And because breaking the fast includes violating the sanctity of the text, so it is like breaking the fast by intercourse.

Its explanation is that the text of prohibition in the month includes what the text of permissibility in the nights includes, and violating the sanctity of the text is a complete crime. Then we do not make expiation obligatory by analogy, rather we make it obligatory by inference from the text, because the questioner mentioned intercourse and specified it, not as a crime, rather it is an action in an owned place. The crime is the breaking of the fast, so it became clear that what makes expiation obligatory is breaking the fast that is a crime. Do you not see that expiation is attributed to breaking the fast, and obligations are attributed to their causes? The evidence for it is that it is not obligatory upon the forgetful person due to the absence of breaking the fast. And breaking the fast that is a complete crime occurs by eating just as it occurs by intercourse, and because it is a means to it. The attachment of the ruling is to the cause, not to the means. Then making it obligatory for eating is more appropriate, because expiation was made obligatory as a deterrent, and the call of nature at the time of fasting to eating is more than to intercourse, and patience from it is more difficult, so making expiation obligatory in it is more appropriate, just as the prohibition of insulting entails the prohibition of abuse by way of greater priority. Then for the sake of worship, the sanctity of intercourse and the sanctity of eating are equal, unlike the state of absence of ownership, for the sanctity of intercourse is more severe, so the sanctity of intercourse increases over the sanctity of eating. And unlike Hajj, for the sanctity of intercourse in it is stronger, so it does not become permissible by shaving.

The evidence for equality here is the exception of the forgetful person, for we made the text that came regarding eating in the state of forgetfulness like that which came regarding intercourse, so likewise it makes the text that came regarding making expiation obligatory by intercourse like that which came regarding eating. And the causes are subordinate, so the crime is not completed by them.

Then the conclusion of the madhab according to us is that breaking the fast, whenever it occurs by what is nourishing or used for treatment, expiation is attached to it as a deterrent, for natures call to nourishment and likewise to medicine for preserving health or restoring it.

As for if one consumes what is not nourishing, like dust and pebbles, his fast becomes invalid except according to the view of some whose view is not relied upon, for he says: The occurrence of breaking the fast is by what causes the desire.

But we say: The pillar of fasting is abstaining from delivering something to its interior, and that is absent by consuming a pebble. Then there is no expiation upon him except according to the view of Malik, may Allah have mercy on him, the Exalted, for he said: He is one who breaks the fast without excuse. He said: And his crime here is more apparent, for he has no purpose in this action except the crime against fasting, unlike what is nourishing.

But we say: The absence of the call of nature to it suffices from making expiation obligatory in it as a deterrent, just as we did not make the hadd obligatory for drinking blood and urine, unlike wine. Then the completion of the crime is by the absence of the pillar of fasting in form and meaning. The absence of the meaning of what causes the desire, if it is absent, the crime is not completed. And in deficiency there is the semblance of absence, and expiation falls due to semblance.(4)
