Rinsing Mouth or Sniffing Water

The jurists of the four schools have agreed that the fasting person does not break his fast by rinsing the mouth, whether it is in purity or other than it. And it was narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that 'Umar asked him about kissing for the fasting person. So he, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"What do you think if you rinsed your mouth with water while you are fasting?" I said: "There is no harm in it." He said: "So what about it?"(1)

And because the mouth is in the ruling of the exterior, and reaching it does not invalidate the fast, like the nose and the eye.

However, they differed regarding if one rinses the mouth or sniffs water and water enters his throat: does he break his fast by that or not?

This is not free from two cases, as follows:

First Case: That One Exaggerates in Rinsing the Mouth and Sniffing

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis in one view held that if the fasting person exaggerates in rinsing the mouth and sniffing, and something enters his throat, he breaks his fast by that, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to Laqit ibn Sabrah:

"Exaggerate in sniffing, except if you are fasting."(2)

So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, forbade exaggeration to preserve the fast, so that indicates that it breaks the fast, because if the reaching of water in exaggeration were not forbidden in fasting, there would be no meaning to the prohibition of exaggeration. And because exaggeration is forbidden in fasting, and what is produced from a forbidden cause is like direct action, just as if a person is wounded and dies, it is made as if he directly killed him. And because it reached by a forbidden action, so it resembles intention, so it is known that this is prevented in fasting as a precaution and to avoid water reaching the throat. So that indicates that whenever it occurs, breaking the fast happens by it. And because he delivered water to his stomach while remembering his fast, so he broke his fast, just as if he drank intentionally.

The Hanbalis in their school held that if one exaggerates in rinsing the mouth or sniffing, and water enters his throat, he does not break his fast by that, because it reached without intention, so it resembles the dust of flour if one sifts it.

Second Case: That One Does Not Exaggerate in Rinsing the Mouth and Sniffing

The jurists differed regarding the fasting person if he does not exaggerate in rinsing the mouth or sniffing, except that water reaches his throat: does he break his fast by that or not?

The Hanafis, Malikis, and Shafi'is in one view held that he breaks his fast by that and his fast becomes invalid, due to the previous hadith of 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, so he compared kissing to rinsing the mouth. So if one kisses and ejaculates, his fast becomes invalid, so likewise if one rinses the mouth and water reaches his stomach, his fast must become invalid. And due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to Laqit ibn Sabrah:

"Exaggerate in sniffing, except if you are fasting."(3)

So it is known that he prevented that in fasting as a precaution and to avoid water reaching the throat, so that indicates that whenever it occurs, breaking the fast happens by it, and considering it if it was from exaggeration. And because he delivered water to his stomach while remembering his fast, so he broke his fast, just as if he drank intentionally.

The Shafi'is in their school and the Hanbalis held that his fast does not become invalid by that, because it reached his stomach without his choice, so his fast does not become invalid, like the dust of flour and sifting flour. And because it reached his throat without excess or intention, so it resembles if a fly flew into his throat.(4)

(1) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2385).

(2) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2366), Al-Tirmidhi (788), and Ibn Majah (407).

(3) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2366), Al-Tirmidhi (788), and Ibn Majah (407).

(4) "Tuhfat al-Fuqaha'" (1/354), "Mukhtasar al-Quduri" (63), "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (80), "Al-Taj wa al-Iklil" (2/426), "Al-Ishraf" (203), "Al-Majmu'" (7/540, 542), "Hilyat al-'Ulama'" (3/195), "Al-Ifsah" (1/397), "Al-Mughni" (4/164, 165), "Al-Insaf" (3/309), and "Al-Mubdi'" (3/29).
