Fifth: Severe Hunger and Thirst

The jurists held that whoever is overcome by hunger and thirst, so he fears destruction, then he breaks his fast and makes it up.

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Whoever is overcome by hunger and thirst, so he fears destruction, breaking the fast is required upon him, even if he is healthy and resident, due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And do not kill yourselves. Indeed, Allah is to you ever Merciful"
[Al-Nisa' 4:29]

And His statement, the Exalted:

"And do not throw [yourselves] with your [own] hands into destruction"
[Al-Baqarah 2:195]

And making up is required upon him, like the sick person. And Allah, Glorified and Exalted, knows best.(1)

The Malikis said: If he fears for himself, fasting is forbidden for him, and that is because preserving the soul and benefits is obligatory.(2)

Al-Kasani al-Hanafi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: As for severe hunger and thirst from which destruction is feared: it is absolutely permitting, in the position of illness from which destruction is feared due to fasting.(3)

However, the Hanafis restricted it to two matters:

First: That He Fears Destruction for Himself

By preponderance of opinion, not by mere imagination, or he fears the loss of reason, or the loss of some senses, like the pregnant woman and the nursing woman if they fear destruction for themselves or for their two children.

Second: That It Not Be by Exhausting Himself

For if it is by that, expiation is required upon him. And it was said: No.(4)

Some jurists equated it with illness. So al-Hisni said: Know that being overcome by hunger and thirst is like illness.(5)

Al-Qalyubi said: And like illness is being overcome by hunger and thirst, not like headache, and earache and light toothache.

And they gave as examples for it those with difficult professions, like the reaper, the builder, and the guard. However, they said: He must intend to fast at night. So if hardship befalls him, he breaks his fast.(6)

As for the Hanafis, it came in "Al-Durr al-Mukhtar" his statement: [Branches] It is not permissible to do work that reaches weakness, so he bakes half the day and rests the remainder. If he says: It is not enough for me, he is contradicted by the shortest days of winter. If a free person exhausts himself by work until he becomes ill, then breaks his fast, there are two views regarding his expiation. Qunya. And in "Al-Bazzaziyyah": If he fasts and is unable to stand, he fasts and prays sitting, combining the two acts of worship.

Ibn 'Abidin said: (His statement: It is not permissible, etc.) He attributed it in "Al-Bahr" to Qunya. And he said in "Al-Tatarkhaniyyah": And in "Al-Fatawa", 'Ali ibn Ahmad was asked about the professional if he knows that if he engages in his profession, illness that permits breaking the fast will befall him, and he is in need of expenses. Is eating permitted for him before he becomes ill? So he prohibited that with the strongest prohibition. And likewise he reported it from his teacher al-Wabri. And in it: I asked Abu Hamid about a baker who becomes weak at the end of the day. Does he have the right to do this work? He said: No, but he bakes half the day and rests in the remainder. If he says: It is not enough for me, he is contradicted by the days of winter, for they are shorter, so what he does today. End, summarized.

Al-Ramli said: And in "Jami' al-Fatawa": If one becomes weak for fasting due to engaging in livelihood, he may break his fast and feed for each day half a sa'. End.

Meaning: If he does not catch a number of other days in which fasting is possible for him, otherwise making up is required upon him. And on this is the reaper if he is unable to do it with fasting and the crop will be destroyed by delay, then there is no doubt in the permissibility of breaking the fast and making up. And likewise the baker.

And his statement: (He is contradicted, etc.) There is consideration in it, for the length of the day and its shortness have no connection to sufficiency. So his truthfulness may appear in his statement: It is not enough for me, so it is left to him, assuming his state is sound. So consider. End, the statement of al-Ramli: Meaning, because need differs in summer and winter, and in high prices and low prices, and in few dependents and their opposite. However, what he reported from "Jami' al-Fatawa" was portrayed in "Nur al-Idah" and others as one who vows to fast forever, and it is supported by the absolute statement: He breaks his fast and feeds. And our discussion is regarding fasting Ramadan.

What should be said in the issue of the professional, where the apparent is that what passed from the juristic considerations of the shaykhs is not from what is transmitted of the school, is to say: If he has what is sufficient for him and his dependents, breaking the fast is not permissible for him, because asking people is forbidden for him, so breaking the fast is more appropriate. Otherwise, he has the right to work to the extent that is sufficient for him, even if it leads him to breaking the fast. It is permissible for him if work in other than that which does not lead him to breaking the fast is not possible for him. And likewise if he fears the destruction of his crop or its theft, and he does not find one who will work for him for the wage of the like, and he is able to pay it, because he has the right to cut prayer for less than that. However, if his wage for himself in work for a known period came, and Ramadan came, the apparent is that breaking the fast is permitted for him, even if he has what is sufficient for him, if the employer does not agree to canceling the contract, as in the wet nurse, for she must breastfeed by contract, and breaking the fast is permissible for her if she fears for the child. So his fear for himself is more appropriate. So consider.

This is what appeared to me, and Allah, the Exalted, knows best.

(His statement: If a free person exhausts, etc.) He said in "Al-Wahbaniyyah":

If a person exhausts himself by work... Then breaks his fast, two views were written regarding expiation.

Al-Shurunbulali said: Its form: A fasting person exhausts himself in work until thirst exhausts him, then he breaks his fast. Expiation is required upon him. And it was said: No. And with this al-Baqali issued a fatwa. And this is contrary to the female slave if she exhausts herself, because she is excused under the coercion of the master, and she has the right to refrain from that. And likewise the male slave.

And its apparent, which is what is in "Al-Shurunbulaliyyah" from "Al-Muntaqa", is preferring the requirement of expiation.

I said: The requirement of his statement: And she has the right to refrain, is the requirement of expiation upon her also, if she does it voluntarily. What is before it is carried on what is if it was without her choice, by the evidence of the explanation. And Allah, Glorified and Exalted, knows best.(7)

Al-Hattab said in "Mawahib al-Jalil": The question occurs in our time if fasting occurs in the time of summer. Is it permissible for the hired worker to go out for reaping with the necessity of breaking the fast or not? And the fatwa with us was: If he is in need of his craft for his livelihood, and he has no alternative to it, then that is permitted for him. Otherwise, it is disliked. As for the owner of the crop, there is no disagreement in the permissibility of gathering his crop, even if it leads to his breaking the fast, otherwise he falls into the prohibition of wasting property.

And likewise the spinning of women of flax and thinning the thread with their mouths. If the flax is Egyptian, it is absolutely permissible. And if it is Damani and has a taste that dissolves, then she is like those with crafts. If she is weak, that is permitted for her. And if she is not in need, it is disliked for her in the day of Ramadan.(8)

Abu Bakr al-Ajuri from the Hanbalis said: Whoever's craft is difficult, if he fears destruction by fasting, he breaks his fast and makes it up, if leaving the craft harms him. If leaving it does not harm him - meaning leaving the craft - he sins by breaking the fast and leaves it. And if harm is not removed by leaving it, there is no sin upon him for breaking the fast due to the excuse. He said: This is the statement of the jurists, may Allah have mercy on them.(9)
