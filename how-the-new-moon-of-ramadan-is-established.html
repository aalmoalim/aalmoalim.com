How the New Moon of Ramadan is Established

The jurists differed regarding how the new moon of Ramadan is established - does it require one witness or must there be two witnesses like other months?

The Hanafis, Shafi'is in the school, and Hanbalis in the school held that it is established with the testimony of one just person, due to the hadith of Ibn 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said:

"The people looked for the new moon, and I informed the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, that I had seen it, so he fasted it and ordered the people to fast it."(1)

And from Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: A Bedouin came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said:

"I saw the new moon," meaning Ramadan. He said: "Do you testify that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah?" He said: "Yes." He said: "Do you testify that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah?" He said: "Yes." He said: "O Bilal, call the people, let them fast tomorrow."(2)

And what Fatimah bint al-Husayn ibn 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, may Allah be pleased with them, narrated:

"That a man testified before 'Ali, may Allah be pleased with him, about seeing the new moon of Ramadan, so he fasted, and I think he said: and he ordered the people to fast. And he said: 'I would prefer to fast a day from Sha'ban rather than break my fast a day from Ramadan.'"(3)

They said: And because reporting the sighting of the new moon is from narration, and not from testimony; because it obligates the reporter to fast, and the content of testimony does not obligate the witness with anything. And number is not a condition in narration, so it is possible to accept the report of one person regarding the sighting of the new moon with the conditions that must be present in the narrator for a religious report, which are: Islam, sanity, puberty, and justice.(4)

The Malikis, Shafi'is in one opinion, and Ahmad in one narration held that it is required for the sighting of the new moon of Ramadan to have the testimony of two just persons, due to what Al-Nasa'i narrated from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd ibn al-Khattab, that he addressed the people on the day in which there was doubt, and said:

"Indeed, I sat with the companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and asked them, and they informed me that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'Fast when you see it and break your fast when you see it and perform the sacrifice for it. If it is obscured from you, then complete thirty days. If two witnesses testify, then fast and break your fast.'"(5)

And by analogy to Shawwal and other months.(6)

(1) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2340).

(2) Narrated by Abu Dawud (1993), and Al-Albani weakened it in "Da'if Abu Dawud" (507), and Al-Nawawi authenticated it in "Al-Majmu'" (7/471).

(3) Narrated by Al-Shafi'i in "Al-Musnad" (721), and in "Al-Umm" (2/94), and from his chain Al-Bayhaqi (4/213), and Al-Nawawi authenticated it in "Al-Majmu'" (7/472).

(4) "Al-Bada'i'" (2/593).

(5) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Al-Nasa'i (2116).

(6) "Al-Dhakhirah" (2/488, 489), "Mawahib al-Jalil" (2/386), "Al-Mudawwanah" (1/174), "Bidayat al-Mujtahid" (1/347), "Kashshaf al-Qina'" (2/305), and "Al-Mughni" (4/124).
