Conditions for the Obligation of Fasting

(Meaning: the liability being charged with the obligation) They are:

a - Islam:

It is a general condition for being addressed by the branches of the Shari'ah. As for the disbeliever: if he is an original disbeliever, he is not required to do it in the state of disbelief because it is not valid from him. If he becomes Muslim, making up (qada') is not obligatory upon him, due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted: "Say to those who have disbelieved: if they desist, what has passed will be forgiven for them" [Al-Anfal 8:38], and because requiring the making up of what was missed in the state of disbelief would repel from Islam.

Similarly, the apostate, if he becomes Muslim, making it up is not required of him, and he is not required to perform it during his apostasy according to Abu Hanifah, Malik, and Ahmad.

Al-Shafi'i said: Making it up is required of him if he becomes Muslim.(1)

b - Sanity:

Sanity is required for the obligation of fasting, meaning the fasting person must be sane, for there is no benefit in directing the address without it. Fasting is not obligatory upon the insane person, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"The pen has been lifted from three... and from the insane person until he regains consciousness."(2)

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The insane person is not required to fast in the present state by consensus, due to the hadith and consensus.(3)

c - Puberty:

The jurists have agreed that the child is not required to fast Ramadan, and making up what was missed before puberty is not required of him, due to what 'Ali, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said:

"The pen has been lifted from three: from the insane person who has lost his mind until he regains consciousness, from the sleeper until he awakens, and from the child until he reaches puberty."(4)

And because the purpose of legal obligation is compliance, and that is through understanding and the ability to act, and childhood and infancy are incapacity.

The jurists have explicitly stated that if the discerning boy or girl is able to fast, then the guardian must command them to do it at seven and strike them for abandoning it at ten, by analogy to prayer.

The Hanafis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis said: It is obligatory upon their guardian to command them to fast if they are able, and to strike them at that time if they abandon it so that they become accustomed to it, like prayer, except that fasting is more difficult, so ability is considered for it, because one who is able to pray may not be able to fast.

The Malikis said: Fasting is disliked for them, and fasting is not like prayer.(5)

(1) "Al-Majmu'" (7/414), "Al-Bada'i'" (2/600), "Ahkam al-Qur'an" by Ibn al-'Arabi (2/399), "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (1/18), and "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/440). The details of this issue will come, if Allah wills, in the Book of Hajj.

(2) Sahih hadith: Its citation will come later.

(3) "Al-Majmu'" (7/416) and "Al-Ifsah" (1/381).

(4) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (399), Al-Tirmidhi (1423), Al-Nasa'i (6/1156), Ibn Majah (2041), and Ahmad (6/100).

(5) "Al-Majmu'" (7/416) and "Al-Ifsah" (1/381).
