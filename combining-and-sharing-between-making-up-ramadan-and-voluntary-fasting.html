Combining and Sharing Between Making Up Ramadan and Voluntary Fasting

The jurists differed regarding the ruling of sharing in intention in fasting, by intending to fast, for example, six from Shawwal, with the intention of making up what is required upon one. Is it valid or not? And does it count for both of them, or does it count as voluntary, or does it not count at all for one of them?

The Hanafis said: If one intends in fasting making up and voluntary acts, it becomes voluntary, and it does not count for making up according to Imam Muhammad ibn al-Hasan.

Imam Abu Yusuf said: It counts for making up, and voluntary acts are invalidated. And al-Hasan reported it from Abu Hanifah, because the obligation is stronger, so the intention of the lower is repelled, like one who intends the Hajj of Islam and voluntary acts.(1)

The Malikis and Shafi'is held that sharing and combining between the intention of making up and the intention of voluntary acts is permissible, like fasting the six with the intention of making up the debt and achieving the reward of voluntary acts.

Imam Al-Zarqani, may Allah have mercy on him, said: (Benefit) Al-Badr said: Consider: If one fasts the day of 'Arafah for making up required upon one, and intends by it making up and 'Arafah together, the apparent is that it counts for both of them together, by analogy to one who intends by his ritual bath major ritual impurity and Friday, for it counts for both of them together, and by analogy to one who prayed the obligation and intended the greeting. And consider the report in the issue. And likewise it is said in 'Ashura' and the ninth and the like. Consider.(2)

Al-Dusuqi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And know that it is taken from this issue the validity of the intention of fasting 'Ashura' for virtue and making up, and Ibn 'Arafah leaned to it. And it is also taken from it that if one says takbir once, intending by it the opening and bowing, it counts for him. And if one says taslim once, intending by it the obligation and the response, it counts for him. And with this Ibn Rushd said.(3)

As for the Shafi'is, Imam Al-Ramli, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And if one fasts in Shawwal for making up or vow or other than them, or in the like of the day of 'Ashura', one attains the reward of its voluntary act, as the father, may Allah have mercy on him, issued a fatwa, following al-Barizi, al-Asfuniy, al-Nashiri, and the jurist 'Ali ibn Salih al-Hadrami and others. However, one does not attain the complete reward arranged for what is required, especially one who missed Ramadan and fasted for it in Shawwal, because the previously mentioned meaning is not true of him.(4)

Shihab al-Din al-Ramli, may Allah have mercy on him, was asked about a person who has fasting from Ramadan required upon one, and making up in Shawwal. Does one attain making up Ramadan and the reward of six days from Shawwal? And is there a report in that?

(He answered) that one attains by one's fasting making up Ramadan, even if one intends by it other than it, and one attains the reward of six from Shawwal. And a group of later scholars mentioned the issue.(5)

And it was said in "Hashiyat al-Sharqawi 'ala Sharh al-Tahrir": "And if one fasts in it (meaning in the month of Shawwal) for making up from Ramadan or other than it, vow or another voluntary act, one attains the reward of its voluntary act, for the consideration is the existence of fasting in six days from Shawwal... However, one does not attain the complete reward arranged for what is required except by the intention of fasting it specifically for the six from Shawwal, especially one who missed Ramadan and fasted for it in Shawwal, because it is not true that one fasted Ramadan and followed it with six from Shawwal."(6)

The Hanbalis in their school (and Ibn Hazm, and it is what he reported from the predecessors of the scholars)(7) held that sharing in intention in fasting between an obligatory fast and a recommended one is not valid, by fasting making up Ramadan, for example, in the ten of Dhul-Hijjah, and attaining the reward of making up and voluntary acts.

Ibn Muflih, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Making up Ramadan is permissible in the ten of Dhul-Hijjah, and from him it is disliked.

The second narration: It is disliked, and it was explained that making up in it causes missing the virtue of fasting it voluntarily. And with this Imam Ahmad and others explained it. Ibn Rajab mentioned it in "Al-Lata'if" and said: And it was said: That one attains by it the virtue of fasting voluntary acts also.(8)

The explicit statement of Ibn Rajab, may Allah have mercy on him: And it was said: That one attains by it the virtue of fasting voluntary acts also. And this is on the view of whoever says: If one vows fasting a month and fasts Ramadan, it counts for one's obligation and vow. It is directed.(9)

So he explicitly stated that it was said: That one attains by it the virtue of fasting voluntary acts also. So the origin is that it is not permissible, and in one view it is said that one attains by it the reward, so sharing is permissible on this view. As for on the school, it is not permissible.

And on this is the disagreement in most of the rulings, like one who gives zakat, intending by it the obligation and voluntary acts. Al-Kasani, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And if one gives charity with five, intending by all of it zakat and voluntary acts, it is from zakat in the view of Abu Yusuf. And Muhammad said: It is from voluntary acts. The aspect of Muhammad's view is that the two intentions contradicted, so specification was not valid due to the contradiction, so it attached to absence, so the charity remained with an absolute intention, so it occurs for voluntary acts, because it is lower, and the lower is certain with it. The aspect of Abu Yusuf's view is that when the two directions contradict, one acts with the stronger, which is the obligation, as when two evidences contradict, one acts with the stronger of them. And because specification is considered in zakat, not in voluntary acts, for voluntary acts do not need specification. Do you not see that the absolute charity occurs for it? So he nullified its specification, and zakat remained specified, so it occurs for zakat. "Bada'i' al-Sana'i'" (2/40), published by Dar al-Kitab al-'Arabi.

The proof of that is the statement of Allah, the Exalted: "And they were not commanded except to worship Allah, [being] sincere to Him in religion" [Al-Bayyinah 5], and sincerity is that one makes the commanded action sincere for the face for which Allah, the Exalted, commanded one in it only. And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever does an action that is not upon our command, it is rejected." So whoever mixes one action with another has done an action that is not upon the command of Allah, the Exalted, or the command of His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, so it is invalid and rejected. And with Allah, the Exalted, is success. And it is the view of Malik, Al-Shafi'i, Abu Sulayman, and their companions. And Abu Yusuf said: Whoever prays while traveling two rak'ahs, intending by them Dhuhr and voluntary acts together, or fasts a day from making up Ramadan, intending by it making up what is required upon one and voluntary acts together, or gives what is required upon one in the zakat of one's wealth, intending by it zakat and voluntary acts together, or makes ihram for the Hajj of Islam, intending by it the obligation and voluntary acts together, then all of that counts for one from the prayer of the obligation, the fast of the obligation, the zakat of the obligation, and the Hajj of the obligation, and voluntary acts are invalidated in all of that. And Muhammad ibn al-Hasan said: As for prayer, it is invalidated, and it does not count for one, neither for obligation nor for voluntary acts. As for zakat and fasting, one's action in that becomes voluntary in both of them, and the obligation is invalidated. As for Hajj, it counts for one for the obligation, and voluntary acts are invalidated. So have you heard of statements more dropped than these? And we do not know from whom is the wonder: from whoever let loose his tongue with the like of them in the religion of Allah, the Exalted, erasing what He wills and establishing with confusion, and specifying what He wills and invalidating with mixing, or from whoever followed the one who said them and spent his life in studying them and supporting them, being religious with them. And we seek refuge with Allah from abandonment, and we ask Him for the continuation of safety and protection, and we praise Him for His blessings to us with that greatly. And we have reported from Mujahid that he said regarding one who made upon one fasting two consecutive months, if one wishes, one fasts Sha'ban and Ramadan, and it counts for one, meaning from one's obligation and vow. Mujahid said: And whoever has making up Ramadan required upon one and fasts voluntarily, it is one's making up, even if one did not intend it. "Al-Muhalla" (6/174).
