One Who Eats or Drinks Forgetfully

The jurists differed regarding one who eats or drinks forgetfully during the day of Ramadan: is his fast valid or does his fast become invalid and making up is obligatory upon him?

The majority of jurists - the Hanafis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis - held that his fast is valid and making up is not obligatory upon him, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Whoever forgets while fasting and eats or drinks, let him complete his fast, for indeed Allah fed him and gave him drink."(1)

In another wording:

"Whoever eats or drinks forgetfully does not break his fast, for indeed it is provision that Allah provided for him."(2)

From Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"Whoever breaks the fast in the month of Ramadan forgetfully, there is no making up upon him and no expiation."(3)

And other hadiths, and because it is an act of worship that has permissibility and prohibition, so in its prohibitions there is what differs between its intentional and forgetful [performance], like prayer and Hajj.(4)

The Malikis held that whoever eats or drinks forgetfully, his fast becomes invalid, and making up is obligatory upon him, due to the generality of the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And eat and drink until the white thread becomes distinct to you from the black thread of dawn, then complete the fast until night"
[Al-Baqarah 2:187]

And this one did not complete it. And He prohibited abstention, so it resembles the intentional [one].

And because every action with which fasting is not valid with something of its kind intentionally in one way, it is not valid with its forgetfulness, like abandoning the intention.

As for the obligation of making up upon him: because he is legally responsible, and eating occurred from him in Ramadan, like the intentional [one]. And because he ate in an obligatory fast, it does not fall due to illness, like the sick person.

And because making up, if it is obligatory upon the sick person with him being more excused than the forgetful person, then it is more appropriate that it be obligatory upon the forgetful person.

All of this is in the obligation. As for if one eats or drinks forgetfully in voluntary [fasting], his fast is valid, because eating in voluntary [fasting] does not invalidate it.

Imam Malik, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Whoever eats or drinks absentmindedly or forgetfully in voluntary fasting, there is no making up upon him, and let him complete his day in which he ate or drank while performing voluntary [fasting], and let him not break his fast.(5)

Al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Ibn al-'Arabi said: All the jurists of the cities adhered to the apparent meaning of this hadith - meaning the hadith "for indeed Allah fed him and gave him drink" - and Malik looked at the issue from its way, so he overlooked it, because breaking the fast is the opposite of fasting, and abstention is a pillar of fasting, so it resembles if one forgets a rak'ah from prayer. He said: And al-Daraqutni narrated in it: "There is no making up upon you." Our scholars interpreted it to mean: There is no making up upon you now.

Al-Hafiz said: This is forced. Rather I say: Would that it were authentic so we would follow it and say it, except on Malik's principle that the narration of one [person], if it comes contradicting the principles, is not acted upon. So when the first hadith came, agreeing with the principle in lifting the sin, we acted upon it. As for the second, it does not agree with it, so we did not act upon it.

Al-Qurtubi said: Those who dropped making up argued with it, and it was answered that it did not address making up in it, so it is understood as the dropping of accountability, because what is required is fasting a day without defect in it. But al-Daraqutni narrated in it the dropping of making up, and it is a text that does not accept probability. But the issue is in its authenticity. If it is authentic, it must be taken and making up is dropped.

Some Malikis answered by understanding the hadith as referring to voluntary fasting, as Ibn al-Tin reported from Ibn Sha'ban. And likewise Ibn al-Qassar said and reasoned that Ramadan was not specified in the hadith, so it is understood as voluntary [fasting]. And al-Muhallab and others said: He did not mention in the hadith the affirmation of making up, so it is understood as the dropping of expiation from him, and the affirmation of his excuse and lifting the sin from him, and the continuation of his intention that he made at night.

The answer to all of that is with what Ibn Khuzaymah, Ibn Hibban, al-Hakim, and al-Daraqutni narrated from the chain of Muhammad ibn 'Abd Allah al-Ansari from Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu Salamah from Abu Hurayrah with the wording: "Whoever breaks the fast in the month of Ramadan forgetfully, there is no making up upon him and no expiation" - so he specified Ramadan and explicitly stated the dropping of making up. Al-Daraqutni said: Muhammad ibn Marzuq alone narrated it from al-Ansari. It was objected to that Ibn Khuzaymah also narrated it from Ibrahim ibn Muhammad al-Bahili, and that al-Hakim narrated it from the chain of Abu Hatim al-Razi, both from al-Ansari, so he is the one who alone narrated it, as al-Bayhaqi said, and he is trustworthy. The intended meaning is that he alone narrated the mention of dropping making up only, not the specification of Ramadan, for al-Nasa'i narrated the hadith from the chain of 'Ali ibn Bakkar from Muhammad ibn 'Amr, and its wording is: "Regarding a man who eats in the month of Ramadan forgetfully, he said: Allah fed him and gave him drink." And the dropping of making up came from another chain from Abu Hurayrah, which al-Daraqutni narrated from the narration of Muhammad ibn 'Isa ibn al-Tabba' from Ibn 'Ulayyah from Hisham from Ibn Sirin, and its wording is: "For indeed it is provision that Allah provided for him, and there is no making up upon him." And he said after its citation: This chain is authentic, and all of them are trustworthy. I say: But the hadith in Muslim and others from the chain of Ibn 'Ulayyah does not have this addition. And al-Daraqutni also narrated the dropping of making up from the narration of Abu Rafi', Abu Sa'id al-Maqburi, al-Walid ibn 'Abd al-Rahman, and 'Ata' ibn Yasar, all from Abu Hurayrah. And he also narrated from the hadith of Abu Sa'id, raised to the Prophet: "Whoever eats in the month of Ramadan forgetfully, there is no making up upon him." And its chain, even if it is weak, is suitable for support. So the least degree of the hadith with this addition is that it be hasan, so it is suitable for arguing with it. And arguing has occurred in many issues with what is less than it in strength. And it is also supported by the fact that a group of the Companions issued fatwas with it without disagreement from them - as Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Hazm, and others said - 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, Zayd ibn Thabit, Abu Hurayrah, and Ibn 'Umar. Then it agrees with His statement, the Exalted: "But He will call you to account for what your hearts have earned" - so forgetfulness is not from what the heart earns. And it agrees with the analogy in invalidating prayer by intentional eating, not by forgetfulness, so likewise fasting. As for the analogy that Ibn al-'Arabi mentioned, it is in opposition to the text, so it is not accepted. And his rejection of the hadith despite its authenticity because it is a narration of one [person] that contradicts the principle is not conceded, because it is a principle independent regarding fasting. So whoever opposes it with analogy to prayer has introduced one principle into another. And if the door of rejecting authentic hadiths with the like of this were opened, little would remain of hadith.

In the hadith is the gentleness of Allah, Glorified and Exalted, with His servants, and making things easy for them, and lifting difficulty and hardship from them. And Ahmad narrated a reason for this hadith, so he narrated from the chain of Umm Hakim bint Dinar from her mistress Umm Ishaq that she was with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, then a bowl of tharid was brought, and she ate with him. Then she remembered that she was fasting. So Dhu al-Yadayn said to her: Now after you have become full? So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to her: "Complete your fast, for indeed it is provision that Allah provided for you." And in this is a refutation of whoever distinguished between little eating and much eating.

Among the rare narrations is what 'Abd al-Razzaq narrated from Ibn Jurayj from 'Amr ibn Dinar that a person came to Abu Hurayrah and said: I woke up fasting then forgot and ate. He said: No problem. He said: Then I entered upon a person and forgot and ate and drank. He said: No problem, Allah fed you and gave you drink. Then he said: I entered upon another and forgot and ate. So Abu Hurayrah said: You are a person who has not become accustomed to fasting.(6)

(1) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1933) and Muslim (809).

(2) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Al-Tirmidhi (721) and Al-Daraqutni (2/180).

(3) Hasan hadith: Narrated by Ibn Khuzaymah in "Sahihuh" (3/239) and Ibn Hibban in "Sahihuh" (8/287).

(4) "Al-Ikhtiyar" (1/268), "Sharh Fath al-Qadir" (2/327), "Mukhtasar al-Quduri" (62), "Al-Majmu'" (7/536), "Al-Mughni" (4/171), and "Al-Ifsah" (1/396).

(5) "Al-Muwatta'" (1/306), "Al-Ishraf" (202), "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/457), "Sharh al-Zarqani" (2/253), "Al-Mughni" (4/171), and "Al-Ifsah" (1/396).

(6) "Fath al-Bari" (4/156, 157).
