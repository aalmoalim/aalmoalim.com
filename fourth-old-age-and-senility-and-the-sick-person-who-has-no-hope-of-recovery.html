Fourth: Old Age and Senility, and the Sick Person Who Has No Hope of Recovery

The jurists have agreed that the old man and the old woman who are unable to fast may break their fast, and the sick person who has no hope of recovery enters into their ruling.

Ibn al-Mundhir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: They have unanimously agreed that the old man and the old woman who are unable to fast may break their fast.(1)

Al-Buhuti, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Whoever is unable to fast due to old age or illness with no hope of recovery breaks his fast, meaning: that is permitted for him by consensus, due to the absence of its obligation - meaning fasting - upon him, because he is unable to do it, so he is not charged with it.(2)

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The old man whom fasting causes hardship, meaning: it causes him severe difficulty, and the sick person who has no hope of recovery, there is no fasting upon them without disagreement.(3)

Due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And He has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty"
[Al-Hajj 22:78]

And His statement, the Mighty and Majestic:

"Allah does not charge a soul except [with that within] its capacity"
[Al-Baqarah 2:286]

And due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And upon those who are able to bear it, a ransom of feeding a poor person"
[Al-Baqarah 2:184]

It has been said in some aspects of interpretation: That "no" is implied in the verse, and the meaning: And upon those who are not able to bear it, as in His statement, the Exalted: "Allah makes clear to you lest you go astray" [Al-Nisa' 4:176], meaning: lest you go astray.(4)

From 'Ata' that he heard Ibn 'Abbas reciting this verse, so Ibn 'Abbas said:

"It is not abrogated. It is for the old man and the old woman who are unable to fast, so they feed in place of each day a poor person."(5)

The verse is in the place of evidence, even on the view of its abrogation, as the majority held, because if it came regarding the decrepit old man, as some of the predecessors held, then it is clear. And if it came for choice, then likewise, because abrogation is only established regarding one who is able to fast, so the decrepit old man remains in his state as it was.(6)

However, the Hanafis restricted the inability of old age and senility to be continuous. So if one is unable to fast due to severe heat, for example, they may break their fast and make it up in winter.(7)

However, they differed regarding what is required upon them if they break their fast:

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Shafi'is in their school, and Hanbalis held that ransom is required upon him, which is a mudd of food for each day, due to the previously mentioned statement of Ibn 'Abbas.

And due to what was narrated that Anas, may Allah be pleased with him:

"Became weak for fasting a year before his death, so he broke his fast and fed for each day a poor person."(8)

And because the performance of fasting is obligatory, so it is permissible that it falls to expiation.

Al-Kasani, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And because when fasting is missed, the need for compensation is touched, and compensation by fasting is impossible, so it is compensated by ransom. And ransom is made equivalent to fasting legally in this case due to necessity, like value in the guarantee of destroyed property.(9)

The Malikis and Shafi'is in one view held that ransom is not required, because he breaks his fast due to an excuse, so feeding is not required upon him, like the sick person and the coerced. And because he breaks his fast and making up is not required upon him, so feeding is not required upon him, like the child. And because if making up is not possible due to the continuation of the excuse, feeding is not required upon its being missed, like the sick person if illness continues with him until he dies. And because feeding in the principles is required in fasting due to the delay of fasting or making up. As for its complete dropping, there is nothing in the principles, like the child.(10)

(4) "Al-Bada'i'" (2/638).
