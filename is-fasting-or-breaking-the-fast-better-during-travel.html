Is Fasting or Breaking the Fast Better During Travel?

The jurists of the four schools differed regarding: Which is better for the traveler, fasting or breaking the fast?

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis in one view held that fasting is better than breaking the fast, if fasting does not exhaust him and does not weaken him, due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And that you fast is better for you"
[Al-Baqarah 2:184]

And due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Whoever has a mount that provides satiation, let him fast Ramadan wherever it reaches him."(1)

And because fasting in Ramadan is the better of the two times, and fasting in the better of the two times of fasting is better than fasting at other times.

And due to the hadith of Hamzah ibn 'Amr al-Aslami mentioned previously, in which: "If you wish, fast, and if you wish, break your fast." So whoever is given a choice between fasting and breaking the fast, fasting is better for him, like voluntary acts.

And because if he breaks his fast, he has delayed fasting from its time. And because performing obligations in their times is better. And because he is safe from missing it, and he attains the reward, and his obligation is fulfilled. And the verses have indicated that fasting is an obligation, and breaking the fast is a concession, and the obligation is better than the concession as long as it does not return with harm.

Ibn Rushd, may Allah have mercy on him, said: What is a concession, it is better to abandon the concession.(2)

The Hanafis also qualified the preference of fasting by that if most of his companions are not breaking their fast, or they share in the expense. If they are so, then breaking the fast is better, in agreement with the group.(3)

The Hanbalis in their school, Ibn Habib, 'Abd al-Malik ibn al-Majishun, and Ibn 'Abd al-Barr from the Malikis held that breaking the fast is better for the traveler, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Fasting while traveling is not from righteousness."

And in another narration:

"Take the concession of Allah that He has given you."(4)

And from 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"Allah loves that His concessions be taken, just as He dislikes that His disobedience be committed."(5)

And from Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, went out in the year of the conquest to Makkah in Ramadan, and he fasted until he reached Kuray' al-Ghamim, and the people fasted. Then he called for a cup of water and raised it until the people looked at it, then he drank. Then it was said to him after that: Some of the people have fasted. So he said: 'Those are the disobedient, those are the disobedient.'"(6)

Ibn Habib said: And because it is the later of the two matters from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him.(7)

However, al-Nawawi and al-Kamal ibn al-Humam, may Allah have mercy on them both, said: The hadiths that indicate the preference of breaking the fast are interpreted as referring to one who is harmed by fasting, and in some of them there is explicit mention of that. And this interpretation is necessary to reconcile between the hadiths, and that is better than neglecting some of them, or claiming abrogation without decisive evidence.(8)

(1) Weak hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2410).

(2) "Bidayat al-Mujtahid" (1/407). See: "Al-Bada'i'" (2/634), "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/464), "Al-Hidayah" (2/273), "Al-Durr al-Mukhtar" (2/117), "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (81), "Al-Majmu'" (7/431), and "Kashaf al-Qina'" (2/312).

(3) "Hashiyat al-Tahtawi" (452) and "Nur al-Idah" (1/111).

(4) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1844) and Muslim (1115), and the addition is his.

(5) Sahih hadith: Previously mentioned.

(6) Narrated by Muslim (1114).

(7) "Al-Tamhid" (22/53), "Al-Ifsah" (1/413), "Al-Mughni" (4/214), "Kashaf al-Qina'" (2/312), "Al-Insaf" (3/287), and "Al-Majmu'" (7/440).

(8) "Al-Majmu'" (7/442) and "Fath al-Qadir" (2/273, 274).
