Hearing the Call to Prayer While Eating or Drinking

The majority of jurists - the Shafi'is and Hanbalis - have explicitly stated, and al-Bayhaqi and al-Nawawi reported it from the general people of knowledge, that if a person hears the second call to prayer or the time of true dawn enters while he is eating, let him spit out what is in his mouth and not swallow it. And likewise if the vessel is on his mouth, he should remove it from his mouth when he hears the call to prayer and let him complete his fast.

As for the hadith that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"If one of you hears the call to prayer and the vessel is in his hand, let him not put it down until he fulfills his need from it."(1)

In another narration:

"And the mu'adhdhin used to call the adhan when dawn appeared."(2)

The intended meaning by "call to prayer" is Bilal's first adhan, due to his statement, peace and blessings be upon him: "Indeed Bilal calls the adhan at night, so eat and drink until Ibn Umm Maktum calls the adhan," and "the vessel" is raised as a subject with its predicate being what comes after it.

Or the intended meaning from it is that the prohibition of eating is related to dawn, not to the adhan, for the mu'adhdhin may hasten with the adhan before dawn, so there is no consideration for the adhan if one does not know of the appearance of dawn. This ruling is for those who know dawn. As for the common people who do not know, they should be cautious.

Imam al-Bayhaqi, may Allah have mercy on him, said after citing the hadith: If this is authentic, it is understood according to the general people of knowledge that he, peace and blessings be upon him, knew that the caller was calling before the appearance of dawn such that his drinking would occur just before the appearance of dawn. And the narrator's statement "And the mu'adhdhins used to call the adhan when dawn appeared" may be a disconnected report from someone below Abu Hurayrah, or it may be a report about the second adhan. And the Prophet's statement, peace and blessings be upon him, "If one of you hears the call to prayer and the vessel is in his hand," is a report about the first call to prayer.(3)

Imam al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Branch: We mentioned that whoever has dawn appear while food is in his mouth, let him spit it out and complete his fast. If he swallows it after knowing of dawn, his fast becomes invalid. There is no disagreement on this. Its evidence is the hadith of Ibn 'Umar and 'A'ishah, may Allah be pleased with them, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Indeed Bilal calls the adhan at night, so eat and drink until Ibn Umm Maktum calls the adhan." Narrated by Al-Bukhari and Muslim. And in the Sahih there are hadiths with its meaning.

As for the hadith of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said: "If one of you hears the call to prayer and the vessel is in his hand, let him not put it down until he fulfills his need from it," and in another narration "And the mu'adhdhin used to call the adhan when dawn appeared" - al-Hakim Abu 'Abd Allah narrated the first narration and said: This is authentic according to the condition of Muslim.

Al-Bayhaqi narrated both of them, then said: If this is authentic, it is understood according to the general people of knowledge that he, peace and blessings be upon him, knew that it was being called before the appearance of dawn such that his drinking would occur just before the appearance of dawn. He said: And his statement "when it appeared" may be from the speech of someone below Abu Hurayrah, or it may be a report about the second adhan. And the Prophet's statement, peace and blessings be upon him, "If one of you hears the call to prayer and the vessel is in his hand" is a report about the first call to prayer, so that it agrees with the hadith of Ibn 'Umar and 'A'ishah, may Allah be pleased with them. He said: And on this the reports agree, and success is from Allah. And Allah, Glorified and Exalted, knows best.(4)

Imam 'Ali al-Qari, may Allah have mercy on him, said: (From Abu Hurayrah who said: The Messenger of Allah said: "If one hears the call to prayer") meaning: the adhan of Fajr ("one of you and the vessel") meaning: that from which one eats ("in his hand") a circumstantial phrase ("let him not put it down") meaning: the vessel ("until he fulfills his need from it") meaning: by eating and drinking. This is if it is known or presumed that dawn has not appeared. And Ibn al-Malik said: This is if one does not know of the appearance of Fajr. As for if one knows that it has appeared or doubts it, then no. And al-Khattabi said: This is built upon his statement, peace and blessings be upon him: "Indeed Bilal calls the adhan at night, so eat and drink until Ibn Umm Maktum calls the adhan." And in it is that the benefit of the restriction does not appear at that time.

He said: Or its meaning may be that one hears the call to prayer while doubting Fajr, due to cloudiness of the air, for example, so he does not know from its adhan that dawn has appeared, because he knows that the signs of dawn are absent, and if they had appeared to the mu'adhdhin, they would have appeared to him as well. As for if one knows of its appearance, there is no need for the caller's adhan, for he is commanded to abstain when the white thread becomes distinct to him from the black thread. And al-Tayyibi said: The indication of the address suggests that he did not break his fast if the vessel was not in his hand. And it has preceded that hastening iftar is a sunnah, but this is from the understood meaning of the title, so it is not acted upon. And Ibn Hajar objected to it in the chapter of correctness that it is not from the understood meaning of the title, and the restriction with the circumstantial phrase has an understood meaning by agreement.

That is according to the Shafi'is. Otherwise, according to the Hanafis, there is no consideration for the understood meaning except in the issue, not in the evidences. And Ibn Hajar said - following al-Tayyibi by implication -: It is valid that the intended meaning from the hadith is the request to hasten iftar, meaning: if one of you hears the call to prayer of Maghrib and it coincides that the vessel is in his hand for another reason, let him hasten to break the fast from it, and let him not delay until putting it down. And with this the statement of the commentator is refuted. And the way of its refutation is that his statement "and the vessel is in his hand" is not for restriction, rather for emphasis on speed.

This is extremely far-fetched, and his statement "for another reason" is refuted by the explicit hadith "until he fulfills his need from it."

So the correct view is that it is a restrictive condition at the time of Fajr, suggesting that it is possible to quickly eat and drink, due to the proximity of its time, and making up his need, and his soul's eagerness and strength of his appetite and his desire's orientation with all his determination from what one almost fears for him, that if he were prevented from it, he would not abstain. So the Lawgiver permitted it as mercy upon him and gradually for him, by proceeding and moving toward it. Perhaps this was in the beginning of the matter. And what occurred of disagreement in the Fajr intended in fasting points to it. Al-Shamani mentioned that what is considered is the first appearance of Fajr according to the majority of scholars, and before its illumination. And it is reported from 'Uthman, Hudhayfah, Ibn 'Abbas, Talaq ibn 'Ali, 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, and al-A'mash. Masruq said: They did not count Fajr as your Fajr. Rather, they counted Fajr as that which fills the houses. Shams al-A'immah al-Halawani said: The first is more cautious, and the second is more lenient.

Perhaps this hadith is built upon leniency, and Allah, the Exalted, knows best. And the wording of "becomes distinct" in the verse supports it. Ibn Hajar said: As for what was reported from the majority of the Companions that the intended meaning by Fajr in the verse is illumination, it is something that consensus almost agreed upon in opposition to it.(5)

(1) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2350), Ahmad (10637), Al-Hakim in "Al-Mustadrak" (792), and Al-Bayhaqi (7809).

(2) Narrated by Ahmad (10637) and Al-Bayhaqi (7810).

(3) "Sunan al-Bayhaqi" (4/218).

(4) "Al-Majmu'" (6/319). See also: "Ma'alim al-Sunan" (2/106), "Al-Furu'" (3/52), and "Hashiyat al-Rawd al-Murbi'" (3/431).

(5) "Mirqat al-Mafatih" (4/421, 422).
