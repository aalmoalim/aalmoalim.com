Time of Permissibility to Break the Fast for the Traveler

Regarding the time of permissibility of breaking the fast for the traveler, there are three cases:

First Case: That One Begins Travel Before Dawn, or Dawn Appears While One Is Traveling and One Intends to Break the Fast

Breaking the fast is permissible for him by consensus, because he began travel in a time in which breaking the fast is permissible for him. And because he is described as traveling at the time of the existence of the cause of obligation.(1)

Second Case: That One Begins Travel After Dawn

That dawn appears while one is resident in one's city, then one travels after the appearance of dawn or during the day. The jurists differed: Is breaking the fast permissible for him in this case, or is it not permissible and completing the fast is required upon him?

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Malikis, Shafi'is, and Ahmad in one narration held that breaking the fast is not permissible for him, and completing the fast is required upon him, because it is an act of worship that differs by travel and residence. So it is required that if one begins it while resident, then travel occurs to him, the ruling of residence is given precedence, like prayer and wiping over socks. And because he has mixed permissibility with prohibition, and it is necessary to give precedence to one of them in the ruling, so giving precedence to residence is more appropriate.

Despite that, if he breaks his fast, there is no expiation upon him according to the Hanafis and Malikis in the well-known view, due to doubt at the end of the time. And because when he traveled after dawn, he became from the people of breaking the fast, so expiation was dropped from him.

And the correct view according to the Shafi'is is that breaking the fast is forbidden for him, so even if he breaks his fast by intercourse, expiation is required upon him.(2)

The Hanbalis in their school, al-Muzani, and others from the Shafi'is, and Ibn Hubayrah reported it from the people of Madinah from the companions of Malik, held that whoever intends to fast while resident, then travels during the day, voluntarily or involuntarily, breaking the fast is permissible for him on that day after his departure and separation from the inhabited houses of his village, and his exit from between their buildings, due to the generality of His statement, the Exalted:

"So whoever among you is ill or on a journey, then [he should fast] a number of other days"
[Al-Baqarah 2:185]

And due to the hadith of Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, went out to Makkah in the year of the conquest and fasted until he reached Kuray' al-Ghamim, and the people fasted with him. Then it was said to him: The people have found fasting difficult, and the people are looking at what you have done. So he called for a cup of water after 'Asr and drank, and the people were looking at him. So some of them broke their fast and some of them fasted. Then it reached him that some people had fasted. So he said: 'Those are the disobedient, those are the disobedient.'"(3)

And the hadith of Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, went out in the year of the conquest to Makkah in the month of Ramadan and fasted until he passed by a pool on the road, and that was at the time of noon. He said: The people became thirsty and began to stretch their necks, and their souls yearned for it. He said: So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, called for a cup containing water and held it in his hand until the people saw it, then he drank, and the people drank."(4)

And likewise the hadith of 'Ubayd ibn Jubayr, who said:

"I rode with Abu Basrah al-Ghifari, the companion of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, in a ship from Fustat in Ramadan. So he set out, then brought his meal near, then said: Come near. So I said: Are you not between the houses? So Abu Basrah said: Do you dislike the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him?"(5)

And likewise the hadith of Muhammad ibn Ka'b, who said:

"I came to Anas ibn Malik in Ramadan and he intended to travel, and his mount had been prepared for him, and he had put on his travel clothes, then he called for food and ate. So I said to him: Is it Sunnah? He said: It is Sunnah, then he mounted."(6)

Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, said after mentioning this report: And in it is evidence for whoever permits breaking the fast for the traveler on a day in which he travels during it. This is one of the two narrations from Imam Ahmad, and the view of 'Amr ibn Shurahbil, al-Sha'bi, and Ishaq. And it was reported from Anas. And it is the view of Dawud and Ibn al-Mundhir.

And Malik, al-Shafi'i, and Abu Hanifah said: He does not break his fast.

And it is the view of al-Zuhri, al-Awza'i, and Makhul.(7)

They said: And because travel is a meaning that if it existed at night and continued in the day, it would permit breaking the fast. So if it exists during it, it permits it, like illness. And because it is one of the two matters explicitly mentioned in the permissibility of breaking the fast by them, so its permissibility during the day is like the other.(8)

And those who permitted it from the Shafi'is said: It is giving precedence to the ruling of travel.(9)

The Hanbalis explicitly stated that it is better for whoever travels during a day in which he intended to fast to complete the fast of that day, avoiding the difference of whoever did not permit breaking the fast for him, as it is the view of the majority, giving precedence to the ruling of residence, like prayer.(10)

Third Case: That One Breaks the Fast Before Leaving One's City

The jurists of the four schools have agreed that it is not permissible for one who intends to travel to break his fast before leaving the place of his residence, because the concession of travel is not realized without it, just as it does not remain without it. And when travel is not yet realized, rather he is resident and present. And Allah, the Exalted, said:

"So whoever among you witnesses the month, let him fast it"
[Al-Baqarah 2:185]

And this one is present, and he is not described as being a traveler until he exits from the city. And as long as he is in the city, he has the rulings of the residents. Therefore, he does not shorten prayer.(11) And this has been explained previously.
