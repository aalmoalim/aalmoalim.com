Purity from Menstruation and Postpartum Bleeding

The jurists have unanimously agreed that it is not permissible for the menstruating woman and the woman in postpartum bleeding to fast. Rather, they break their fast and then make it up, and if they fast, it is not valid from them, whether it is obligatory or voluntary, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

“Is it not the case that when she menstruates she does not pray and does not fast?”(1)

Thus, if a woman sees blood for even an hour of the day, her fast for that day is invalidated. Al-Nawawi, Ibn Jarir, Ibn Qudamah, Ibn Hubayrah, and others have transmitted consensus on this.

Ibn Battal, may Allah have mercy on him, said: His statement, peace and blessings be upon him, “Is it not the case that when she menstruates she does not pray and does not fast?” is an explicit text that the obligation of prayer drops from the menstruating woman, and that it is not permissible for her to fast during her days of menstruation. The Ummah is agreed on that, and they have unanimously agreed that making up the fasts she missed is obligatory upon her, and that there is no making up of the prayers upon her, except for a faction of the Khawarij who hold that she must make up the prayer; the scholars of the Ummah, from the predecessors and successors, are in opposition to them on this.(2)

Imam Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The people of knowledge have unanimously agreed that it is not permissible for the menstruating woman and the woman in postpartum bleeding to fast, and that they break their fast in Ramadan and then make it up, and that if they fast, their fast does not suffice them. ‘A’ishah, may Allah be pleased with her, said: “We used to menstruate at the time of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and we were commanded to make up the fast but we were not commanded to make up the prayer.” Agreed upon. And the command is only from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. And Abu Sa‘id said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Is it not the case that when one of you menstruates she does not pray and does not fast? That is from the deficiency of her religion.” Narrated by Al-Bukhari.

The menstruating woman and the woman in postpartum bleeding are the same in ruling, because postpartum blood is the blood of menstruation, and its ruling is the ruling of menstruation. Whenever menstruation occurs in any part of the day, the fast of that day is invalidated, whether it occurs at the beginning or at the end of the day. And if the menstruating woman intends to fast and refrains (from food and drink) while knowing the prohibition of that, she sins and it does not suffice her.(3)

Imam al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The Ummah has unanimously agreed on the prohibition of fasting upon the menstruating woman and the woman in postpartum bleeding, and that their fasting is not valid, as we have mentioned previously. Ibn Jarir transmitted this consensus, and others have transmitted it as well.

Imam al-Haramayn said: The fact that fasting is not valid from her is not something whose meaning is fully comprehended, for purity is not a condition in fasting.(4)

Imam Ibn Rajab, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The Ummah has unanimously agreed that the menstruating woman does not fast during her days of menstruation, that her fast is not valid and not regarded, and that it is obligatory upon her to make up the fast when she becomes pure.(5)

They have also agreed on the obligation of making up Ramadan upon her, due to the hadith of ‘A’ishah, may Allah be pleased with her, when Mu‘adhah asked her: “What is the matter that the menstruating woman makes up the fast but does not make up the prayer?” She said: “Are you a Haruriyyah?” I said: “I am not a Haruriyyah, but I am asking.” She said: “This used to happen to us, and we were commanded to make up the fast but we were not commanded to make up the prayer.”(6) So the command to make up (qada') is a branch of the obligation of performance (ada').

Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Jarir, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nawawi, Ibn Hubayrah, and others have transmitted consensus on this.

Imam al-Qurtubi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The scholars have unanimously agreed that the menstruating woman makes up the fast but does not make up the prayer, due to the hadith of Mu‘adhah.(7)

Imam al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, also said: The Ummah has unanimously agreed on the obligation of making up the fast of Ramadan upon her as well; al-Tirmidhi, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Jarir, our companions, and others have transmitted consensus on this.(8)

He also said: The fast of the menstruating woman and the woman in postpartum bleeding is not valid, it is not obligatory upon them, it is forbidden for them to fast, and making it up is obligatory upon them. All of this is unanimously agreed upon.(9)

They have also agreed that menstruation does not break the consecutiveness in fasting kaffarat (expiatory fasts), because it contradicts fasting, and the woman who menstruates will usually not be free of it in a given month, and delaying (the fast) until the age of menopause is dangerous. The Hanafis excluded from this the expiation for oaths and the like.(10)
