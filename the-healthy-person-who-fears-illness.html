The Healthy Person Who Fears Illness

It has preceded that the severity of illness permits breaking the fast for the sick person. However, the jurists differed regarding the healthy person if he fears severity or exhaustion: is breaking the fast permitted for him or not?

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Malikis in one view, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis in general according to them held that if a healthy person fears illness with preponderant probability by a sign and experience, or by the report of a doctor, breaking the fast is permitted for him.

The Hanafis said: If a healthy person fears illness with preponderant probability by a sign and experience, or by a report from a skilled, Muslim, trustworthy doctor, breaking the fast is permitted for him. If he fears it by mere suspicion, breaking the fast is not permitted for him.(1)

The Shafi'is said: Whoever is overcome by hunger and thirst and fears destruction, breaking the fast is required upon him, even if he is healthy and resident, due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"Between you and them is a covenant, a ransom delivered to his family and freeing a believing slave"
[Al-Nisa' 4:92]

And His statement, the Exalted:

"And do not throw yourselves into destruction"
[Al-Baqarah 2:195]

And making up is required upon him, like the sick person.

And if the sick person transgresses by doing what made him ill, breaking the fast is permitted for him if he finds severe harm by it. However, they made the condition for the permissibility of breaking his fast the intention of concession, as al-Ramli said and relied upon.

And they distinguished between continuous illness and intermittent illness: If the illness is continuous, he may abandon the intention at night. And if it comes and goes, it is considered: If one is feverish at the time of beginning the fast, he may abandon the intention. Otherwise, he must intend from the night. Then if he needs to break the fast, he breaks it.

And the like of that: the harvester, the builder, and the guard - even if volunteering - so intention is required upon them at night. Then if hardship befalls them, they break their fast.(2)

As for the Hanbalis, Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And the healthy person who fears illness by fasting is like the sick person who fears an increase in it in the permissibility of breaking the fast, because the sick person is only permitted to break his fast out of fear of what is renewed by his fasting from an increase in illness and its prolongation. So fear of the renewal of illness is in its meaning.

Ahmad said regarding one who has overwhelming desire for intercourse and fears that his testicles will split: Breaking the fast is permitted for him. And he said regarding a young girl who fasts when she menstruates: If fasting exhausts her, let her break the fast and make it up. He means: If she menstruates while she is young and has not reached fifteen years of age.

Al-Qadi said: This is if she fears illness by fasting, breaking the fast is permitted for her. Otherwise, it is not.

Section: And whoever is permitted to break the fast due to the severity of his desire, if it is possible for him to repel the desire by other than intercourse, like masturbation with his hand or with the hand of his wife or his slave girl, intercourse is not permissible for him, because it is breaking the fast due to necessity, so what is beyond what repels the necessity is not permitted for him, like eating carrion in necessity. And if he has intercourse, expiation is required upon him. And likewise, if it is possible for him to repel it by what does not invalidate the fast of another, like having intercourse with his wife or his young slave girl or the People of the Book, or direct contact with a mature Muslim woman less than the private parts, or masturbation with her hand or with his hand, invalidating the fast of another is not permitted for him, because if necessity is repelled, what is beyond it is not permitted, like satiation from carrion if necessity is repelled by satisfying hunger. And if necessity is not repelled except by invalidating the fast of another, that is permitted, because it is from what necessity calls to, so it is permitted like breaking his fast, and like the pregnant woman and the nursing woman break their fast out of fear for their two children.

If he has two wives, one menstruating and one pure and fasting, and necessity calls him to have intercourse with one of them, there are two views. One of them: Having intercourse with the fasting one is more appropriate, because Allah, the Exalted, explicitly mentioned the prohibition of having intercourse with the menstruating woman in His Book. And because having intercourse with her includes harm that does not disappear with the need for intercourse.

The second: He may choose, because having intercourse with the fasting woman invalidates her fast, so the two invalidations conflict and are equal.(3)

The Malikis in the well-known view held that if a healthy person fears severity or exhaustion, breaking the fast is not permissible for him if mere severe exhaustion occurs to him by fasting, even if it is said that breaking his fast is permissible.

Al-Dardir said in "Al-Sharh al-Kabir": And breaking the fast is permissible (by illness if one fears) meaning: thinks, by the statement of a knowledgeable doctor, or experience, or what agrees with the temperament (an increase in it or its continuation), meaning that recovery is delayed. And likewise if severe exhaustion occurs to the sick person by fasting, contrary to the healthy person.

Al-Dusuqi said: His statement: (contrary to the healthy person) meaning: Breaking the fast is not permissible for him if mere severe exhaustion occurs to him by fasting. And this is the well-known view. And there will come from the commentator another statement that breaking his fast is permissible. And likewise, if a healthy person fears the occurrence of the origin of illness by his fasting, breaking the fast is not permissible for him on the well-known view, for perhaps illness will not befall him if he fasts. And it is said: Breaking the fast is permissible for him.

Al-Dardir also said: And breaking the fast is required for a sick person and a healthy person if one fears destruction of oneself by fasting, or severe harm like the disabling of a benefit from hearing or sight or other than them, due to the obligation of preserving the soul. As for severe hardship, it permits breaking the fast for the sick person, and it is said: And the healthy person as well.(4)

Ibn Juzayy from the Malikis summarized the states of the sick person regarding fasting, and said: The sick person has states:

The first: That one is unable to fast, or fears destruction from illness or weakness if one fasts, so breaking the fast is required upon him.

The second: That one is able to fast with hardship, so breaking the fast is permissible for him. And Ibn al-'Arabi said: It is recommended.

The third: That one is able with hardship, and fears an increase in illness, so there are two views regarding the requirement of breaking his fast.

The fourth: That it is not difficult for him, and he does not fear an increase in illness, so he does not break his fast according to the majority, contrary to Ibn Sirin.(5)

(1) "Al-Durr al-Mukhtar wa Hashiyat Ibn 'Abidin 'alayh" (2/422).

(2) "Al-Majmu'" (7/423), "Hashiyat Qalyubi" (2/81), and "Rawdat al-Talibin" (2/369).

(3) "Al-Mughni" (4/211, 212), "Al-Furu'" (3/20), "Al-Insaf" (3/286), and "Kashaf al-Qina'" (2/310).

(4) "Al-Sharh al-Kabir ma'a Hashiyat al-Dusuqi" (1/535) and "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/465).

(5) "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (82).
