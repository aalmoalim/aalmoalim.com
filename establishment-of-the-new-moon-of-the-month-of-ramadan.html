Establishment of the New Moon of the Month of Ramadan

The jurists have agreed that fasting the month of Ramadan becomes obligatory by sighting the new moon or by completing thirty days of Sha'ban when there is no sighting, and when the horizon is clear of any obstruction that would prevent sighting,

due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Fast when you see it (the new moon) and break your fast when you see it. If it is obscured from you, then complete the number of Sha'ban as thirty days."(1)

Then they differed regarding when clouds or dust(2) obstruct the sighting of the new moon on the thirtieth night of Sha'ban:

The majority of jurists from the Hanafi, Maliki, and Shafi'i schools, and Ahmad in one narration, held that fasting is not obligatory, and it is obligatory to complete Sha'ban as thirty days, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "Fast when you see it and break your fast when you see it. If it is obscured from you, then complete the number of Sha'ban as thirty days."(1) And because this day is a day of doubt, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, prohibited fasting on the day of doubt - as in the two Sahih collections. And because the principle is the continuation of the month, so it is not transferred from it except with evidence, and none was found.

Imam Ahmad, in another narration, held that the people follow the Imam: if he fasts, they fast, and if he breaks his fast, they break their fast, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Fasting is on the day you fast, breaking the fast is on the day you break your fast, and the sacrifice is on the day you sacrifice."(3)

It was said: Its meaning is that fasting and breaking the fast are with the community and the majority of the people.

(3) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Al-Tirmidhi (697) and Ibn Majah (1660).

Imam Ahmad, in the third narration from him (which is the well-known one), held that fasting that day is obligatory, due to what Nafi' narrated from Ibn 'Umar, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"The month is twenty-nine days, so do not fast until you see the new moon, and do not break your fast until you see it. If it is obscured from you, then estimate it."(4)

Nafi' said: "When twenty-nine days of Sha'ban had passed, Ibn 'Umar would send someone to look for the new moon for him. If it was seen, that was it. If it was not seen and there was no cloud or dust obstructing the view, he would wake up breaking his fast. But if clouds or dust obstructed the view, he would wake up fasting."(5)

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The meaning of "estimate it" (iqdiru lahu) is: make the number tight for it, from the statement of Allah, the Exalted: "and whoever's provision is restricted" [At-Talaq 65:7], meaning it is made tight for him, and His statement, Glorified and Exalted: "He extends provision for whom He wills and restricts it" [Ar-Ra'd 13:26]. Making it tight means making Sha'ban twenty-nine days. Ibn 'Umar explained it by his action, and he is its narrator and most knowledgeable of its meaning, so it is necessary to refer to his explanation, just as reference was made to him in explaining separation in the option of the two parties to a sale. It was narrated from 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said to a man:

"Did you fast anything from the end of Sha'ban?" He said: "No." In another wording: "Did you fast anything from the end of this month?" He said: "No." He said: "Then when you break your fast, fast two days."(6)

The end of the month (sarr al-shahr): its last nights, in which the new moon is concealed and does not appear. And because there is doubt about one of the two ends of the month, it did not become clear that it was not from Ramadan, so fasting became obligatory, like the final circumambulation.

(4) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1801) and Muslim (1080).

(5) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2320).

(6) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1983) and Muslim (818).

'Ali, Abu Hurayrah, and 'A'ishah, may Allah be pleased with them, said: "I would prefer to fast a day from Sha'ban rather than break my fast a day from Ramadan." And because fasting is to be taken with caution, and for that reason fasting became obligatory with the testimony of one person, and breaking the fast only with the testimony of two.(7)

(1) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1810). The meaning of "obscured" (ghubbi) is: clouds or the like came between you and it, derived from ghabawah (obscurity) and lack of clarity, used metaphorically for the concealment of the new moon. This was stated by Al-Shawkani.

(2) Qatar (dust): Al-qatar is dust (darkness) - "Tartib al-Qamus" (3/558).

(3) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Al-Tirmidhi (697) and Ibn Majah (1660).

(4) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1801) and Muslim (1080).

(5) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2320).

(6) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1983) and Muslim (818).

(7) "Al-Mughni" (4/122, 124), "Fath al-Qadir" (2/313), "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (77), "Al-Majmu'" (7/445) and what follows, and "Al-Ifsah" (1/386).
