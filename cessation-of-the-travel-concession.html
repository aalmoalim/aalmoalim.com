Cessation of the Travel Concession

The concession of travel is dropped by two matters by agreement:

First: If the Traveler Returns to His City

If the traveler returns to his city and enters his homeland, which is the place of his residence, even if his entry is due to something he forgot, fasting is required upon him, just as if he arrived at night, or he arrived before noon according to the Hanafis.(1)

As for if he arrives during the day, and did not intend to fast at night, or he arrives after noon according to the Hanafis, and he did not intend to fast before, he abstains for the rest of the day due to the sanctity of the time, and that is not required according to the Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis in one view, because he broke his fast due to an excuse.

And abstaining is required upon him according to the Hanafis and Hanbalis in their school.(2)

Second: If the Traveler Intends Residence

If the traveler intends residence absolutely, or for a period of residence - which is four days and nights according to the Malikis and Shafi'is, more than four days according to the Hanbalis, and half a month or fifteen days according to the Hanafis - in one place, and the place is suitable for residence, not like a ship, the desert, and the land of war, then he becomes a resident by that. So he completes prayer and fasts, and does not break his fast in Ramadan, due to the cessation of the ruling of travel.(3)

The Shafi'is explicitly stated on the correct view that breaking the fast is forbidden for him if he intends residence in travel, and he had intended to fast from the night before consuming anything that breaks the fast, due to the absence of what permits.

And in one view: Breaking the fast is permissible for him, considering the beginning of the day.(4)

Ibn Juzayy, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Travel does not permit shortening except by intention and action, contrary to residence, for it requires fasting and completion by intention without action.(5)

If one does not intend residence, but he stays to fulfill a need of his, without the intention of residence, and he does not know when it will end, or he expects it to end at any time, then breaking the fast is permissible for him, just as he shortens for prayer.

The Hanafis said: Even if he remains on that for years.

If he thinks that it will not end except after more than four days according to the majority, or fifteen days according to the Hanafis, then he is considered a resident, so he does not break his fast and does not shorten, except if the obligation is fighting - as al-Ghazali says - then he takes the concession on the more apparent of the two views. Or the Muslims entered the land of war, or they besieged a fortress in it, or the siege was for a city on the surface of the sea, for the surface of the sea has the ruling of the land of war.(6)

The evidence for that is what Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, narrated:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, fought the battle of the conquest in Ramadan and fasted until when he reached al-Kadid, the water that is between Qudayd and 'Usfan, he broke his fast. So he did not cease breaking his fast until the month ended."(7)

And it is known that the conquest was for ten remaining days of Ramadan, so it is known that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, broke his fast in Makkah for ten days or eleven, according to different narrations. And there is no doubt that his breaking the fast in this period does not negate breaking the fast in what is more than them. "And because he, peace and blessings be upon him, stayed in Tabuk for twenty days shortening prayer."(8)

And we find that breaking the fast is like shortening, which they explicitly stated regarding the prayer of the traveler from the aspect of concession, for the traveler has all the concessions of travel.(9)
