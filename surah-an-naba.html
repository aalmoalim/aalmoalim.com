Everything You Need to Know About Surah An-Naba (The Great News)

Introduction: Surah An-Naba is the opening surah of Juz Amma (the 30th part of the Quran). It is a Makkan surah that serves as a powerful introduction to the themes that dominate this section of the Quran. The surah addresses the fundamental questions about resurrection, the Day of Judgment, and the Hereafter that were being debated in Makkan society.

The Opening: About What Are They Questioning?

عَمَّ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ
"About what are they questioning?"
[An-Naba 78:1]

The surah opens with a powerful rhetorical question that immediately draws attention to the discussions happening in Makkan society. The scene is set: groups of disbelievers in Makkah are gathered, whispering among themselves, questioning and mocking the message of Islam.

The Scene: Imagine groups of people in Makkah sitting together, discussing and questioning. They are not genuinely seeking truth, but rather engaging in mockery, denial, and creating doubts. The Quran captures this moment and addresses it directly.

The word يَتَسَاءَلُونَ (yatasā'alūn - they are questioning) uses the present continuous tense, indicating that this questioning was an ongoing, daily activity. It wasn't a one-time event but a persistent pattern of behavior. The form of the verb also suggests mutual questioning - they ask each other, creating an echo chamber of doubt and denial.

The Great News (An-Naba' al-Azeem)

عَنِ النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ
"About the Great News"
[An-Naba 78:2]

Allah reveals what they were questioning about: النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ (An-Naba' al-Azeem) - The Great News. This is not just any news, but news of immense importance that requires immediate attention and action.

What is "An-Naba'"? The word نَبَأ (naba') differs from خَبَر (khabar - news) in that:

  • Naba' refers to news of great importance that requires a response and action
  • Khabar is simply information that may or may not require action
  • Naba' always implies truth and significance
  • Naba' demands a reaction - it cannot be ignored

The "Great News" encompasses three fundamental truths that shook Makkan society:

  1. Tawheed (Monotheism): That there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah, and all the idols they worshipped were false
  2. Resurrection (Al-Ba'th): That all people will be raised after death and held accountable for their deeds
  3. The Quran and the Prophet: That the Quran is the word of Allah and Muhammad (peace be upon him) is His final messenger

They Are in Disagreement

الَّذِي هُمْ فِيهِ مُخْتَلِفُونَ
"About which they are in disagreement"
[An-Naba 78:3]

The Quran uses powerful language here: هُمْ فِيهِ (hum fīhi - they are in it). This suggests that they have become completely absorbed in this disagreement. It's not just that they disagree about the topic, but they have become defined by their disagreement. Their entire existence has become consumed by questioning and denying the Great News.

Lesson for Da'wah: When the message of Islam creates such a strong reaction that people become completely absorbed in discussing it (even if negatively), it indicates the power and impact of the message. A weak message doesn't generate this level of engagement.

Allah's Response: "Nay, They Will Come to Know"

كَلَّا سَيَعْلَمُونَ
"Nay, they will come to know"
[An-Naba 78:4]
ثُمَّ كَلَّا سَيَعْلَمُونَ
"Then, nay, they will come to know"
[An-Naba 78:5]

Allah responds to their questioning with كَلَّا (kallā) - a strong negation meaning "Nay" or "Stop!" This is not just a denial of their questions, but a command to stop this line of questioning. The repetition emphasizes the certainty of what will happen.

The phrase is repeated twice, which scholars explain in different ways:

  • First occurrence: They will see the reality with their own eyes
  • Second occurrence: They will experience it themselves (enter into it)
  • Or it could mean: First: The disbelievers will know they were wrong, Second: The believers will know they were right

This response bypasses their questions entirely. Instead of engaging with their mockery, Allah declares that the reality will be made clear to them - they will witness it themselves.

Cosmic Signs as Evidence for Resurrection

Before describing the Day of Judgment, Allah presents a series of cosmic signs as evidence of His power and wisdom, which in turn proves the possibility and reality of resurrection.

The Earth and Mountains

أَلَمْ نَجْعَلِ الْأَرْضَ مِهَادًا
"Have We not made the earth a resting place?"
[An-Naba 78:6]
وَالْجِبَالَ أَوْتَادًا
"And the mountains as pegs?"
[An-Naba 78:7]

The word مِهَادًا (mihādan) means a bed or resting place, like a cradle for a baby. This emphasizes Allah's mercy and care in preparing the earth for human habitation. The earth wasn't just created randomly - it was carefully prepared and made suitable for life.

The mountains are described as أَوْتَادًا (awtādan - pegs), like the pegs that hold down a tent. This refers to their role in stabilizing the earth's crust and maintaining geological balance.

Creation in Pairs

وَخَلَقْنَاكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا
"And created you in pairs"
[An-Naba 78:8]

Allah created humans as pairs (male and female), and this pairing extends throughout creation. This serves multiple purposes:

  • It demonstrates the wisdom and purpose in creation
  • It shows the interconnectedness of all things
  • It points to the Oneness of the Creator - one God created all these pairs
  • It serves as evidence for resurrection, as the One who created from nothing can recreate

Sleep as Rest

وَجَعَلْنَا نَوْمَكُمْ سُبَاتًا
"And made your sleep [a means for] rest"
[An-Naba 78:9]

The word سُبَاتًا (subātan) means a cutting off or cessation. Sleep is a mercy from Allah - a necessary break that humans cannot avoid. Even if someone tries to stay awake, eventually sleep overcomes them. This demonstrates Allah's control over human needs and His wisdom in providing rest.

Night and Day

وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا
"And made the night as clothing"
[An-Naba 78:10]
وَجَعَلْنَا النَّهَارَ مَعَاشًا
"And made the day for livelihood"
[An-Naba 78:11]

Night is described as a covering (لِبَاسًا - libāsan), providing protection and rest. Day is for earning a living (مَعَاشًا - ma'āshan), for activity and work. This perfect alternation demonstrates Allah's wisdom and care for His creation.

The Heavens and the Sun

وَبَنَيْنَا فَوْقَكُمْ سَبْعًا شِدَادًا
"And built above you seven strong [heavens]"
[An-Naba 78:12]
وَجَعَلْنَا سِرَاجًا وَهَّاجًا
"And made [therein] a burning lamp"
[An-Naba 78:13]

The seven heavens are described as شِدَادًا (shidādan - strong, firm), indicating their stability and perfection. The sun is called سِرَاجًا وَهَّاجًا (sirājan wahhājan - a blazing lamp), providing both light and heat in perfect balance.

Rain and Vegetation

وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ الْمُعْصِرَاتِ مَاءً ثَجَّاجًا
"And sent down from the rain clouds water in abundance"
[An-Naba 78:14]
لِّنُخْرِجَ بِهِ حَبًّا وَنَبَاتًا
"That We may bring forth thereby grain and vegetation"
[An-Naba 78:15]
وَجَنَّاتٍ أَلْفَافًا
"And gardens of luxurious growth"
[An-Naba 78:16]

The word الْمُعْصِرَاتِ (al-mu'sirāt) refers to clouds that are ready to release their water - clouds that have reached the right moment to rain. The water is described as ثَجَّاجًا (thajjājan - pouring abundantly), indicating Allah's generosity.

From this water, Allah brings forth grain, vegetation, and gardens. The word أَلْفَافًا (alfāfan) means dense, interwoven gardens - gardens so lush that their branches and leaves are intertwined.

Purposes of These Cosmic Signs:

  1. Proof of Absolute Power: The One who created all this can certainly resurrect the dead
  2. Proof of Wisdom: There is purpose in creation, so there must be purpose in resurrection
  3. Proof from Similar Phenomena: Just as dead earth brings forth life, so can dead bodies be brought back to life
  4. Proof of Interconnectedness: All creation works together in harmony, pointing to one Creator
  5. Proof of Human Weakness: Humans are compelled by needs (sleep, food, etc.), showing their dependence on Allah

The Day of Separation

إِنَّ يَوْمَ الْفَصْلِ كَانَ مِيقَاتًا
"Indeed, the Day of Separation is an appointed time"
[An-Naba 78:17]

After presenting the evidence, Allah now declares the reality: يَوْمَ الْفَصْلِ (Yawm al-Fasl - The Day of Separation). This is the Day of Judgment, when truth will be separated from falsehood, and people will be separated according to their deeds.

The word مِيقَاتًا (mīqātan) means an appointed time - a time that has been fixed and determined. The use of كَانَ (kāna - was) in the past tense indicates that this appointment was made long ago - it's a done deal, not something that might or might not happen.

Important Lesson: The Quran doesn't just prove that the Day of Judgment will happen - it declares it as an established fact. This is the proper approach in da'wah: present the Hereafter as a certainty, not as a possibility to be debated.

The Trumpet Will Be Blown

يَوْمَ يُنفَخُ فِي الصُّورِ فَتَأْتُونَ أَفْوَاجًا
"The Day when the Horn will be blown and you will come forth in multitudes"
[An-Naba 78:18]

On that day, a single blast of the Horn (الصُّور - as-Sūr) will be sounded, and all people will come forth in groups (أَفْوَاجًا - afwājan). These are the same groups that were questioning and mocking - now they will be brought together to face the reality they denied.

The word أَفْوَاجًا (afwājan) means groups or waves. People will come in different groups:

  • Each group according to their deeds
  • The arrogant together, the humble together
  • Those who committed the same sins together

The Heavens and Mountains

وَفُتِحَتِ السَّمَاءُ فَكَانَتْ أَبْوَابًا
"And the heaven will be opened and become gateways"
[An-Naba 78:19]
وَسُيِّرَتِ الْجِبَالُ فَكَانَتْ سَرَابًا
"And the mountains will be removed and become mirage"
[An-Naba 78:20]

The seven strong heavens that were described earlier will be opened like gates. The mighty mountains that were like pegs will be set in motion and become like a mirage - appearing to be there but having no substance.

This demonstrates Allah's absolute power: what seemed permanent and unshakeable will be transformed in an instant. If Allah can do this to the heavens and mountains, how can anyone deny His ability to resurrect humans?

Hellfire: The Abode of the Transgressors

إِنَّ جَهَنَّمَ كَانَتْ مِرْصَادًا
"Indeed, Hell has been lying in wait"
[An-Naba 78:21]
لِّلطَّاغِينَ مَآبًا
"For the transgressors, a place of return"
[An-Naba 78:22]

The word مِرْصَادًا (mirṣādan) means a watch-post or ambush - like a predator waiting for its prey. Hellfire has been prepared and is waiting, ready to receive those who deserve it.

It is described as مَآبًا (ma'āban - a place of return) for الطَّاغِينَ (at-tāghīn - the transgressors). The transgressors are those who exceeded all limits, who rejected boundaries and constraints, who wanted to live without accountability.

Who are the Transgressors (At-Tāghīn)? They are those who:

  • Rejected the limits set by Allah
  • Refused to accept accountability
  • Wanted to live without restrictions
  • Oppressed others and exceeded boundaries
  • Rejected the Day of Separation because they didn't want to be held accountable

The Duration of Punishment

لَّابِثِينَ فِيهَا أَحْقَابًا
"They will remain therein for ages"
[An-Naba 78:23]

The word أَحْقَابًا (ahqāban) means ages or long periods. Scholars have explained this in different ways:

  • Psychological Torment: Each time a period (like 80 years) passes, they are told another period awaits, creating endless psychological suffering
  • Different Types of Punishment: Each period brings a different type of punishment, increasing in severity
  • Eternal Duration: The periods continue without end for the disbelievers

No Relief from the Punishment

لَّا يَذُوقُونَ فِيهَا بَرْدًا وَلَا شَرَابًا
"They will not taste therein [any] coolness or drink"
[An-Naba 78:24]
إِلَّا حَمِيمًا وَغَسَّاقًا
"Except scalding water and [foul] discharge"
[An-Naba 78:25]

There will be no relief - no coolness to ease the burning, no drink to quench the thirst. Instead, they will have:

  • Hamīm: Scalding, boiling water that burns
  • Ghassāq: Foul discharge, pus, or extremely cold water (depending on interpretation) that causes additional suffering

Some scholars explain that غَسَّاقًا (ghassāq) refers to the pus and discharge that flows from the wounds of the people of Hell, which they are forced to drink due to their extreme thirst.

Increasing Punishment

جَزَاءً وِفَاقًا
"An appropriate recompense"
[An-Naba 78:26]
إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا لَا يَرْجُونَ حِسَابًا
"Indeed, they were not expecting an account"
[An-Naba 78:27]
وَكَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا كِذَّابًا
"And denied Our signs with [total] denial"
[An-Naba 78:28]

The punishment is وِفَاقًا (wifāqan - appropriate, fitting). It matches their crimes. They denied accountability, so they face eternal accountability. They denied the signs, so they face the consequences of that denial.

The word كِذَّابًا (kiddhāban) is an intensive form, meaning complete and total denial. They didn't just have doubts - they actively and completely rejected every sign and evidence presented to them.

وَكُلَّ شَيْءٍ أَحْصَيْنَاهُ كِتَابًا
"And all things We have enumerated in writing"
[An-Naba 78:29]

Everything has been recorded in detail. The word أَحْصَيْنَاهُ (ahṣaynāhu) means not just counted, but fully recorded with all details - every action, intention, consequence, and impact.

فَذُوقُوا فَلَن نَّزِيدَكُمْ إِلَّا عَذَابًا
"So taste [the penalty], and never will We increase you except in torment"
[An-Naba 78:30]

This is one of the most severe verses in the Quran regarding punishment. Not only will they taste the punishment, but it will only increase - never decrease, never end. This creates psychological torment: knowing that things will only get worse, never better.

Paradise: The Reward for the Righteous

إِنَّ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ مَفَازًا
"Indeed, for the righteous is attainment"
[An-Naba 78:31]

After describing the punishment, Allah now describes the reward. The word مَفَازًا (mafāzan) means success, escape, or attainment. For the مُتَّقِينَ (muttaqīn - the God-conscious, the righteous), there is success - primarily the success of being saved from Hellfire.

Who are the Muttaqīn (God-conscious)? They are those who:

  • Are conscious of Allah in all their actions
  • Fear Allah's punishment
  • Observe the limits set by Allah
  • Are careful not to transgress boundaries
  • Are the opposite of the transgressors (at-tāghīn)

The Gardens of Paradise

حَدَائِقَ وَأَعْنَابًا
"Gardens and grapevines"
[An-Naba 78:32]
وَكَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًا
"And full-breasted [companions] of equal age"
[An-Naba 78:33]
وَكَأْسًا دِهَاقًا
"And a full cup"
[An-Naba 78:34]

Paradise contains:

  • Gardens and grapevines: Beautiful, lush gardens with all kinds of fruits
  • Companions of equal age: Pure spouses who are always young and beautiful
  • Full cups: Cups that are always full, never empty, of the finest drinks

The word دِهَاقًا (dihāqan) means full and overflowing. The cups in Paradise are always full, continuously replenished, never running out.

No Idle Talk or Falsehood

لَّا يَسْمَعُونَ فِيهَا لَغْوًا وَلَا كِذَّابًا
"No ill speech will they hear therein or any falsehood"
[An-Naba 78:35]

In Paradise, there will be no:

  • Laghw: Idle talk, meaningless speech, or harmful words
  • Kiddhāb: Lies or falsehood

Beautiful Connection: The surah began with people engaging in لَغْو (idle talk) and كِذَّاب (falsehood/denial) about the Great News. In Paradise, the reward is complete freedom from such things - the opposite of what they were doing in this world.

Reward from Their Lord

جَزَاءً مِّن رَّبِّكَ عَطَاءً حِسَابًا
"A reward from your Lord, [a generous] gift [made due by] account"
[An-Naba 78:36]

This reward is:

  • From their Lord: Coming directly from Allah, not earned by right but given as a gift
  • Atā'an: A generous gift, beyond what they deserve
  • Hisāban: Fully accounted for - every good deed is multiplied and recorded

Scholars explain that حِسَابًا (hisāban) means that their good deeds are fully calculated and multiplied. A single good deed can be worth ten, a hundred, or even seven hundred times its value. This is the generosity of Allah - He gives far more than what is due.

The Day When No One Speaks Except by Permission

رَّبِّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا الرَّحْمَٰنِ ۖ لَا يَمْلِكُونَ مِنْهُ خِطَابًا
"[From] the Lord of the heavens and the earth and whatever is between them, the Most Merciful. They possess not from Him [authority for] speech"
[An-Naba 78:37]
يَوْمَ يَقُومُ الرُّوحُ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ صَفًّا ۖ لَّا يَتَكَلَّمُونَ إِلَّا مَنْ أَذِنَ لَهُ الرَّحْمَٰنُ وَقَالَ صَوَابًا
"The Day that the Spirit and the angels will stand in rows, they will not speak except for one whom the Most Merciful permits, and he will say what is correct"
[An-Naba 78:38]

On that Day, even the angels and the Spirit (Jibrīl) will stand in rows, unable to speak. No one can intercede, no one can argue, no one can change what has been decreed - except by the permission of the Most Merciful.

This verse emphasizes:

  • The absolute authority of Allah: No one has any power except what Allah grants
  • The end of all false deities: Those who were worshipped besides Allah have no power
  • The reality of intercession: It only happens with Allah's permission and for those who said what is correct (like "There is no deity except Allah")

Who is "the Spirit" (Ar-Rūh)? Most scholars identify this as Jibrīl (Gabriel), the angel who brought revelation. On the Day of Judgment, even he - who delivered the message - will stand in submission, unable to speak except by permission.

That is the True Day

ذَٰلِكَ الْيَوْمُ الْحَقُّ ۖ فَمَن شَاءَ اتَّخَذَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِ مَآبًا
"That is the True Day; so he who wills may take to his Lord a [way of] return"
[An-Naba 78:39]

This is الْيَوْمُ الْحَقُّ (al-yawm al-haqq - the True Day). Everything before it was temporary and will pass away. This Day is the reality, the truth, the permanent state.

Allah calls people to take a path of return to their Lord. The word مَآبًا (ma'āban) means a place of return. In this world, people can choose to make Allah their destination, their place of return. If they do, they will return to Him in the Hereafter for reward, not punishment.

Beautiful Contrast: Earlier in the surah, Hellfire was described as مَآبًا (ma'āban - a place of return) for the transgressors. Here, Allah offers people the chance to make إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِ مَآبًا (ilā rabbihi ma'āban - a return to their Lord) - a return to Allah Himself, which is Paradise.

The Warning and the Regret

إِنَّا أَنذَرْنَاكُمْ عَذَابًا قَرِيبًا
"Indeed, We have warned you of a punishment near"
[An-Naba 78:40]

Allah has warned of a punishment that is near. The word قَرِيبًا (qarīban) means near in time - it could come at any moment. Death could come today, and with it, the beginning of the Hereafter.

يَوْمَ يَنظُرُ الْمَرْءُ مَا قَدَّمَتْ يَدَاهُ
"The Day when a person will observe what his hands have put forth"
[An-Naba 78:40]
وَيَقُولُ الْكَافِرُ يَا لَيْتَنِي كُنتُ تُرَابًا
"And the disbeliever will say, 'Oh, I wish I were dust'"
[An-Naba 78:40]

On that Day, people will see all that they did in their lives. The disbeliever will see the consequences of their denial and will wish they had never been created - they will wish they were just dust, with no accountability, no consciousness, no existence.

The Ultimate Regret: The surah began with people questioning and mocking. It ends with the ultimate regret - wishing they had never existed. This is the journey from denial to despair.

Key Lessons and Insights

1. The Importance of "The Great News" in Da'wah

The surah teaches that effective da'wah (calling to Islam) must begin with presenting "The Great News" - the fundamental truths about:

  • The reality of the Hereafter
  • The Day of Accountability
  • The Oneness of Allah
  • The truth of the Quran and the Prophet

This should come before discussing details of law or practice. The foundation must be established first.

2. The Power of Cosmic Signs

The surah demonstrates how the signs in creation serve as evidence for:

  • Allah's absolute power (proving He can resurrect)
  • Allah's wisdom (proving there is purpose in resurrection)
  • The interconnectedness of creation (pointing to one Creator)
  • Similar phenomena (dead earth bringing forth life, proving dead bodies can be revived)

3. The Contrast Between Transgressors and the God-Conscious

The surah presents a clear contrast:

  • At-Tāghīn (Transgressors): Those who reject limits, want no accountability, exceed boundaries → Destination: Hellfire
  • Al-Muttaqīn (God-Conscious): Those who observe limits, accept accountability, fear Allah → Destination: Paradise

4. The Certainty of the Day of Judgment

The Quran doesn't present the Day of Judgment as a possibility to be debated, but as a certainty that has already been appointed. This is the proper approach - present it as reality, not as a topic for discussion.

5. The Psychological Impact of Punishment

The description of punishment includes not just physical suffering, but psychological torment:

  • Knowing that punishment will only increase, never decrease
  • Being told of new periods of punishment as each one ends
  • Experiencing different types of punishment in sequence
  • The ultimate regret of wishing one had never existed

6. The Generosity of Allah's Reward

Paradise is described as:

  • A gift from Allah, not something earned by right
  • Fully accounted - every good deed multiplied
  • Complete - lacking nothing, including freedom from idle talk and falsehood
  • Eternal - never ending, never decreasing

Conclusion

Surah An-Naba serves as a powerful introduction to Juz Amma, establishing the fundamental themes that will be developed throughout this section of the Quran. It addresses the core questions about life, death, accountability, and the Hereafter that every human being must confront.

The surah moves from the questioning and denial of the disbelievers, through the evidence of Allah's power in creation, to the reality of the Day of Judgment, and finally to the ultimate outcomes: Paradise for the righteous and Hellfire for the transgressors.

It teaches us that the message of Islam is not just about rules and regulations, but about the "Great News" - the fundamental truths that give meaning and purpose to our existence and determine our ultimate destiny.
