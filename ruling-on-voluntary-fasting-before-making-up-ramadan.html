Ruling on Voluntary Fasting Before Making Up Ramadan

The jurists of the four schools differed regarding the ruling of one who has days required upon him to make up from the month of Ramadan: Is it permissible for him to do voluntary fasting before making up what is required upon him or not?

The Hanafis, the Malikis in one view, and the Hanbalis in one narration held that voluntary fasting before making up Ramadan is permissible without dislike, due to making up not being required immediately, because it is an act of worship attached to an extended time, so voluntary acts are permissible in its time before doing it, like prayer, one does voluntary acts at the beginning of its time.

Imam Al-Kasani, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Our companions said: It is not disliked for one who has making up Ramadan required upon him to do voluntary acts. And if requirement were immediately, voluntary acts would be disliked for him before making up, because that would be delaying the obligatory from its restricted time, and that is disliked.(1)

The Malikis in what is preponderant according to them, the Shafi'is, and the Hanbalis in one view held that voluntary fasting before making up what is required upon one from Ramadan is disliked.

Ibn Rushd, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Issue: Malik was asked about a man who has making up Ramadan required upon him. Does he fast the day of 'Ashura' before making up Ramadan? He said: That does not please me, and perhaps it is light. It was said to him: Does he fast it in making up Ramadan? He said: There is no harm in it.

Muhammad ibn Rushd said: His statement "That does not please me, and perhaps it is light" means: I dislike that with a light dislike. And his dislike for it is that he fast it voluntarily, which implies that what is recommended according to him is that he fast it in making up what is required upon him from Ramadan. And his statement after that "There is no harm in fasting it in making up Ramadan" contradicts that, for it is not said regarding something whose doing is recommended: "There is no harm in doing it." Rather, that is said regarding the permissible, whose doing and leaving are equal. And in the hearing of Ibn Wahb, it was said to him: Does he fast it in making up Ramadan? He said: No.

Meaning: It is not appropriate for him to do it, and fasting it voluntarily is better. So these are three views:

One of them: That the choice is to fast it for making up Ramadan.

The second: That the choice is to fast it voluntarily and delay making up Ramadan.

The third: That the two matters are equal. The doer does whichever he wishes. And this difference is only conceivable on the view that making up Ramadan is at leisure, by the evidence of the statement of 'Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her: "Fasting would be required upon me from Ramadan, and I would not be able to fast it until Sha'ban comes, due to being busy with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him." For if making up were immediately, that busyness would not have prevented her from it. And the obligatory at leisure, hastening it is better. So when if one fasts the day of 'Ashura' voluntarily and delays making up, one attains the virtue of the day, and hastening making up is missed. And if one fasts it for making up, one attains hastening making up, and the virtue of fasting the day is missed. So the difference occurred.

The aspect of the view that fasting it voluntarily is better is that the virtue of fasting it has come in reports from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with explicit mention of it and its amount. And the virtue of delaying making up is only known by consideration and analogy, so that is absent in it. And also, it was narrated that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, prayed the two rak'ahs of Fajr after the rising of the sun, before the Fajr prayer. And making up prayer is immediately, so how about making up fasting, which is at leisure? And the aspect of the view that fasting it for making up is better is that if one fasts it voluntarily, one does not feel secure that death will take one before making up, so voluntary acts are not accepted from one, on the apparent of what came from Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, may Allah be pleased with him: "That voluntary acts are not accepted from anyone who has an obligation until he performs it." And the aspect of the view in giving the doer choice in whichever he wishes of that is that the evidences are equal according to him, and their equality is evidence of choice. This is like his statement regarding camels if they increase over twenty and one hundred by one, he made the collector have a choice, when the evidences were equal according to him in the amount of the increase mentioned in the hadith, if what is intended by it is what was from little or much, or increase without the obligation.

The apparent of what is in the book of fasting from "Al-Mudawwanah" is that making up Ramadan is immediately, because he said regarding one who broke his fast in Ramadan in travel or illness, then arrived and stayed a month, or recovered a month, then died, and bequeathed that feeding be done for him, that is in his third, initially. And likewise on his school, in what if one was ill for all of Sha'ban after recovering a month, feeding would be required upon one. So on this, it is not permissible for one to fast the day of 'Ashura' if one has making up Ramadan required upon one. So four views come in the issue.(2)

Al-Hattab, may Allah have mercy on him, said: (And voluntary before vow or making up), meaning that voluntary fasting is disliked for one who has a vow of fasting or has making up Ramadan required upon one. And this is in the guaranteed vow. As for the specified vow, if its time comes, it is not permissible for one to do voluntary acts in it. If one does it, one sins and making up is required upon one. He said it in "Jami' al-Ummahat" by al-Tha'alibi, reporting it from "Al-Muntaqa".

And it is understood from it that voluntary fasting before a specified vow, if its time has not come, is not disliked, and that is apparent.

Notes: The first: The apparent is that every obligatory fast is in the meaning of vow, as is understood from the statement of al-Lakhmi and from the statement of the author of "Al-Tiraz" that follows.

The second: It was said in "Al-Tiraz": If one does voluntary acts, one's fast is valid.

Ibn Naf'i said in "Al-Majmu'ah": One's voluntary act is completed, then one makes up what is required upon one, and one has erred in doing voluntary acts before it. And this is clear, for the time is suitable for voluntary acts and other than them, so whichever occurs is valid. And making up was only more obligatory, because the third said in "Al-Tawdih" and they differed in the emphasized voluntary fast, like 'Ashura', is what is recommended to fast it for making up Ramadan, and it is disliked to fast it voluntarily, and this is his statement in the hearing of Ibn Wahb. Or is one given a choice? Three views he reported in "Al-Bayan". As for less than that, whoever does voluntary fasting, the explicit statement is dislike of doing it before making up.

I said: And the issue is in the text of the forbidden from the hearing of Ibn al-Qasim from the book of fasting. And Ibn Rushd lengthened the discussion in it, and said: All of this is on the view that making up Ramadan is at leisure. As for on the view that it is immediately, which is the apparent of "Al-Mudawwanah" in the book of fasting, it is not permissible for one to fast the day of 'Ashura' if one has making up Ramadan required upon one.

He said: So four views come in the issue.

And it was said in "Al-Mudawwanah": It is permissible to make up Ramadan in the first ten of Dhul-Hijjah.

Abu al-Hasan said: 'Umar ibn al-Khattab recommended making up Ramadan in the ten of Dhul-Hijjah.

And Ibn al-Qasim and Salim said it. He said: And one makes up on the day of 'Ashura'.

Ibn Yunus said: They only recommended that due to their virtue. So if voluntary acts are not done, one makes up the obligation in them.(3)

Imam Al-Khirshi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And voluntary before vow or making up, meaning: And from what is disliked is doing voluntary acts of an act of worship from fasting or prayer or other than them before clearing one's responsibility from an obligation required upon one from that act of worship, from a non-specified vow or making up for what is required upon one from it, due to the responsibility being bound by that, so one strives in clearing it. If one does it, one's voluntary act is valid, due to the absence of specification of time for anything from them. Then one comes with what is required upon one. And the specified is excluded by "other than specified," so it is not permissible in its time other than it. If one does it, making up is required upon one. And consider: Is one's voluntary act valid or not, due to the specification of time for other than it? And there is no dislike in voluntary acts before it, due to the absence of the responsibility being occupied by it before its time.(4)

Al-Dusuqi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: His statement: (And voluntary fasting is disliked), its summary is that voluntary fasting is disliked for one who has an obligatory fast required upon one, like the vowed and making up and expiation, and that is due to what follows from delaying the obligation and the absence of its immediacy. And this is contrary to prayer, for it is forbidden, as was mentioned previously. And the apparent of the author is dislike absolutely, whether the voluntary fast that one gives precedence over the obligatory fast is not emphasized or is emphasized, like 'Ashura', the ninth of Dhul-Hijjah. And it is thus on what is preponderant. In Ibn 'Arafah, Ibn Rushd in preferring fasting the day of 'Arafah for making up or voluntarily, the third is: They are equal. The most preponderant is the first, meaning that they differed in fasting the day of 'Arafah for one who has making up required upon one. So it was said: Fasting it for making up is more preponderant and better than fasting it voluntarily, and fasting it voluntarily is disliked. And it was said: The opposite. And it was said: They are equal, there is no preponderance of one over the other. And the most preponderant is the first statement, and it is the first hearing of Ibn al-Qasim, and Sahnon chose it. And the second statement is the hearing of Ibn Wahb. And the third statement is the last hearing of Ibn al-Qasim.

And know that one who has making up from two Ramadans begins with the first of them, and the opposite is valid. Thus in "Al-Mawaqit".(5)

Al-Khatib al-Shirbini, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Whoever missed something from Ramadan, it is recommended that he make it up consecutively, and it is disliked for one who has making up Ramadan required upon one to do voluntary fasting. Al-Jurjani said it.(6)

Imam Al-Ramli, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The implication of the statement of al-Mahamili, following his shaykh al-Jurjani: It is disliked for one who has making up Ramadan required upon one to do voluntary fasting, like the dislike of fasting it for one who broke his fast with an excuse, contradicts what passed, except that it is combined by saying it has two aspects, or that is carried on one who has no making up required upon one, like a child who reached puberty and a disbeliever who became Muslim. And this is on one who has making up required upon one. And if one leaves it in Shawwal for that or other than it, it is Sunnah to make it up from what is after it, and the Sunnah is achieved by fasting it separately. However, their consecutiveness and connection to the day of Eid is better, hastening to worship, and due to what is in delay from harms. And if one fasts in Shawwal for making up or vow or other than them, or in the like of the day of 'Ashura', one attains the reward of its voluntary act, as the father, may Allah have mercy on him, issued a fatwa, following al-Barizi, al-Asfuniy, al-Nashiri, and the jurist 'Ali ibn Salih al-Hadrami and others. However, one does not attain the complete reward arranged for what is required, especially one who missed Ramadan and fasted for it in Shawwal, because the previously mentioned meaning is not true of him.(7)

The Hanbalis in their school held that voluntary acts before making up what is required upon one from an obligatory fast, from making up Ramadan or vow, are forbidden, and one's voluntary fasting is not valid before making up what is required upon one from Ramadan. The explicit statement from the narration of Hanbal is that it is not permissible. Rather, one begins with the obligation until one makes it up, even if one has a vow required upon one, one fasts it, meaning after the obligation, due to what was narrated from Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, raised: "Whoever reaches Ramadan and has something from Ramadan required upon him that he did not make up, it is not accepted from him. And whoever fasts voluntarily and has something from Ramadan required upon him that he did not make up, it is not accepted from him until he fasts it."(8)

And because it is an act of worship in which money enters into its compensation, so voluntary acts are not valid in it before performing its obligation, like Hajj. And because it is an act of worship whose delay is permitted as a lightening, so if one does not perform it, the original is required upon one.(9)

Imam Al-Mardawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And from them: Is it permissible for one who has an obligatory fast required upon one to do voluntary fasting before it? There are two narrations in it, and they were stated absolutely in "Al-Hidayah", "Al-Mughni", "Al-Majd" in its commentary, "Al-Sharh", "Al-Furu'", and "Al-Fa'iq".

One of them: It is not permissible, and it is not valid. And this is the school. The explicit statement in the narration of Hanbal is that it is not permissible. And it was said in "Al-Hawiyayn" it is not valid in the more correct of the two narrations. Ibn 'Abdus chose it in "Al-Tadhkirah" and decided it in "Al-Madhhab", "Masbuk al-Dhahab", "Al-Ifadat", "Al-Manwar", and he gave it precedence in "Al-Mustaw'ib", "Al-Khulasah", "Al-Muharrar", "Al-Ri'ayatayn", and Ibn Razin in its commentary. And it is from the unique matters of the school.

The second narration: It is permissible, and it is valid. He gave it precedence in "Al-Nazm". It was said in "Al-Qa'idah al-Hadiyah 'Asharah": It is permissible on the more correct. I said: And it is the correct view.

So on the school, which is the absence of permissibility, is making up disliked in the ten of Dhul-Hijjah or not disliked in it? There are two narrations, and they were stated absolutely in "Al-Mughni", "Al-Sharh", "Sharh al-Majd", "Al-Fa'iq", and "Al-Furu'".

I said: The correct view is the absence of dislike.

And this method is the correct one, and it is the method of al-Majd in his commentary, and he was followed in "Al-Furu'". And he said: This method is the correct one. The author said in "Al-Mughni", and this is stronger according to me. It was said in "Al-Furu'": Because if we forbid voluntary acts before the obligation, that is more effective than dislike, so it is not valid as a branch upon it.

And we have another method, which some of the companions said, and it is: If we say absence of permissibility of voluntary acts before the fast of the obligation, making up is not disliked in the ten of Dhul-Hijjah. Rather, it is recommended, so that it is not empty of worship completely. And if we say permissibility, making up is disliked in them, to preserve them for voluntary acts, to clarify their virtue in them, with the virtue of making up. It was said in "Al-Mughni": Some of our companions said it.

And it was said in "Al-Ri'ayatayn" and "Al-Hawiyayn": Making up Ramadan is permissible in the ten of Dhul-Hijjah, and from him it is disliked. And it was said in "Al-Kubra" also: Voluntary fast is forbidden before making up one's obligation, due to its sanctity. The explicit statement is that. And from him it is permissible.

Benefit: If what is obligated legally and a vow meet, one begins with what is obligated legally if one does not fear missing the vowed. And if missing it is feared, one begins with it. And one also begins with making up if the vow is absolute.(10)
