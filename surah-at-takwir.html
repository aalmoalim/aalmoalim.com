Everything You Need to Know About Surah At-Takwir (The Overthrowing)

Introduction: Surah At-Takwir is one of the most powerful surahs describing the Day of Judgment. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever wishes to look at the Day of Judgment as if seeing it with their own eyes, let them read: 'When the sun is wrapped up' (At-Takwir), 'When the sky is split open' (Al-Infitar), and 'When the sky is cleft asunder' (Al-Inshiqaq)." This surah presents twelve scenes using conditional statements ("When...") that describe the end of this world and the beginning of the Hereafter with such vividness that the reader feels they are witnessing these events.

The Structure: Twelve Scenes

The surah consists of twelve conditional statements, each beginning with إِذَا (idhā - "when"). These are not mere possibilities but certainties - events that will definitely occur. The first six verses describe the last moments of this world, and the next six describe the first moments of the Hereafter.

The Last Moments of This World

Scene 1: The Sun is Wrapped Up

إِذَا الشَّمْسُ كُوِّرَتْ
"When the sun is wrapped up [and darkened]"
[At-Takwir 81:1]

The surah opens with the most shocking scene: the sun that we know, that rises on us daily, will be wrapped up (كُوِّرَتْ - kuwwirat). Its light will be extinguished, and it will be thrown into Hellfire.

Visualize the Scene: Imagine people living their lives, and suddenly they look up at the sun - it's gone. Complete darkness. But there's still some light remaining - the stars. But then...

Scene 2: The Stars Fall

وَإِذَا النُّجُومُ انكَدَرَتْ
"And when the stars fall, dispersing"
[At-Takwir 81:2]

The stars, which were the remaining source of light and guidance, will fall and scatter. The word انكَدَرَتْ (inkadarat) means they will either fall and scatter or change color, becoming dark.

Notice that the verses don't mention who is doing these actions. This is because no one except Allah can do this. The verses emphasize the absolute power of Allah - these are not natural phenomena but divine acts.

Scene 3: The Mountains are Set in Motion

وَإِذَا الْجِبَالُ سُيِّرَتْ
"And when the mountains are removed"
[At-Takwir 81:3]

The mighty mountains that provide stability to the earth will be set in motion. Allah willed to stabilize the earth with mountains, so He stabilized them. Now He wills to move them, so they will move. Everything is easy for Allah.

The Three Foundations That Will Change:

  1. Light and Guidance: The sun and stars - gone
  2. Stability: The mountains - moved
  3. Emotions: Human feelings toward worldly things - will change

Scene 4: The Pregnant Camels are Abandoned

وَإِذَا الْعِشَارُ عُطِّلَتْ
"And when the pregnant camels are abandoned"
[At-Takwir 81:4]

الْعِشَار (al-'ishār) refers to camels in their tenth month of pregnancy - the most valuable possessions of the Arabs. These were like the most expensive cars or gold in modern terms. People would prioritize these camels over their own wives and children, feeding them before feeding their families.

On that Day, people will pass by these valuable possessions without even looking at them. They will lose all interest in them. The word عُطِّلَتْ (uṭṭilat) means they become idle - not because they're broken, but because people have lost interest in them.

The Reality of Worldly Wealth: The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The earth will vomit out pieces of its liver like columns of gold and silver. The murderer will pass by and say: 'For this I killed.' The thief will pass by and say: 'For this I stole.' The one who cut family ties will pass by and say: 'For this I cut my family ties.' Then they will leave it and go."

On that Day, all the treasures of the world will be exposed, but no one will care about them. They will have lost all value.

Scene 5: The Wild Beasts are Gathered

وَإِذَا الْوُحُوشُ حُشِرَتْ
"And when the wild beasts are gathered"
[At-Takwir 81:5]

Wild beasts will be gathered together with humans. People will pass by lions and other dangerous animals without fear. All emotions have changed. In the previous surah (Abasa), we learned that people will flee from their own brothers, mothers, fathers, wives, and children. Here, they won't even fear wild beasts.

Everything is reversed. What was loved is now abandoned. What was feared is now ignored. All human emotions toward worldly things have been transformed.

Scene 6: The Seas are Set Ablaze

وَإِذَا الْبِحَارُ سُجِّرَتْ
"And when the seas are set on fire"
[At-Takwir 81:6]

The word سُجِّرَتْ (sujjirat) is incredibly comprehensive. It means the seas will fill up, overflow, connect with each other, and then explode into fire like volcanoes.

Visualize the Complete Scene:

  • No light - the sun is gone, stars have fallen
  • No stability - the earth is shaking, mountains are moving
  • No interest in wealth - people pass by their most valuable possessions
  • No fear of danger - wild beasts are present but ignored
  • Water becomes fire - the seas explode into flames

The scene began with the extinguishing of light (the sun) and ends with the explosion of fire (the seas). This is the end of this world.

The First Moments of the Hereafter

Scene 7: The Souls are Paired

وَإِذَا النُّفُوسُ زُوِّجَتْ
"And when the souls are paired"
[At-Takwir 81:7]

After the explosion, the first scene of resurrection: souls return to their bodies. The word زُوِّجَتْ (zuwijat) means paired - souls are reunited with their bodies, just as the soul was first breathed into the body at creation.

Scholars also explain that people will be gathered in groups - the righteous with the righteous, the wicked with the wicked, each group with their like.

Scene 8: The Buried Infant Girl is Asked

وَإِذَا الْمَوْءُودَةُ سُئِلَتْ
"And when the girl [who was] buried alive is asked"
[At-Takwir 81:8]
بِأَيِّ ذَنبٍ قُتِلَتْ
"For what sin she was killed"
[At-Takwir 81:9]

The first sound heard after resurrection: a question. Allah Himself asks a small buried infant girl: "For what sin were you killed?"

The Significance: If a small, innocent child who was killed will be asked and will speak to defend her rights, imagine what will happen with:

  • Those who were oppressed
  • Those whose wealth was stolen
  • Those whose inheritance was taken
  • Every part of the body that will testify

This scene establishes that justice will be absolute and complete.

Scene 9: The Records are Spread Out

وَإِذَا الصُّحُفُ نُشِرَتْ
"And when the pages are spread open"
[At-Takwir 81:10]

There's no need to review your deeds - they're all recorded. There's no need to remember what you did - it's all written. The records of deeds will be spread out for all to see.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "A banner will be raised for every traitor on the Day of Judgment, placed at his rear end, written on it: 'This is the treachery of so-and-so.'"

Every deed, every word, every thought - all will be exposed.

Scene 10: The Sky is Stripped Away

وَإِذَا السَّمَاءُ كُشِطَتْ
"And when the sky is stripped away"
[At-Takwir 81:11]

When a person looks up at the sky, it will be stripped away. There will be no place to hide, no refuge above.

Scene 11: Hellfire is Set Blazing

وَإِذَا الْجَحِيمُ سُعِّرَتْ
"And when Hellfire is set ablaze"
[At-Takwir 81:12]

When a person looks to the right, they see Hellfire blazing with increased intensity.

Scene 12: Paradise is Brought Near

وَإِذَا الْجَنَّةُ أُزْلِفَتْ
"And when Paradise is brought near"
[At-Takwir 81:13]

When a person looks to the left, they see Paradise brought near, with its bliss increased.

The Complete Scene: A person stands with their record in hand, looks up - the sky is stripped away. Looks right - Hellfire is blazing. Looks left - Paradise is near. Where can they go? What can they do?

The Conclusion: Every Soul Will Know

عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّا أَحْضَرَتْ
"A soul will [then] know what it has put forth"
[At-Takwir 81:14]

After all these scenes, every soul will know what it brought forward. Scholars explain this in different ways:

  • Every soul will know the value of what it brought: It will realize the true worth of its deeds - a word it said, an action it did, a glance it took
  • Every soul will remember what it brought: It will recall every deed it had forgotten, every moment it thought was private
  • Deeds will be embodied: Good deeds will appear in beautiful forms, evil deeds in ugly forms

Key Point: In all twelve scenes, everything changes except one thing: your deeds. The sun will be extinguished, stars will fall, mountains will move, wealth will lose value, seas will become fire - everything will be transformed. But your deeds will come with you and never leave you.

The Oath: The Quran is the Word of a Noble Messenger

فَلَا أُقْسِمُ بِالْخُنَّسِ
"So I swear by the retreating stars"
[At-Takwir 81:15]
الْجَوَارِي الْكُنَّسِ
"Those that run [their courses] and disappear"
[At-Takwir 81:16]
وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا عَسْعَسَ
"And by the night when it departs"
[At-Takwir 81:17]
وَالصُّبْحِ إِذَا تَنَفَّسَ
"And by the dawn when it breathes [new life]"
[At-Takwir 81:18]

After describing the changing universe, Allah now swears by the stable, constant universe - the stars that run their courses and disappear (like a gazelle that runs and hides), the night that approaches and increases in darkness, and the dawn that breathes new life.

These are the constant, stable phenomena of the universe - unlike the changing phenomena described earlier. This contrast emphasizes that while the universe will change, the Quran remains constant and true.

إِنَّهُ لَقَوْلُ رَسُولٍ كَرِيمٍ
"Indeed, it is the word of a noble messenger"
[At-Takwir 81:19]

This Quran is the word of a noble messenger - Jibril (Gabriel, peace be upon him). The word كَرِيمٍ (karīm) means noble, generous - not stingy with the message, giving it completely.

The Qualities of Jibril (Gabriel)

ذِي قُوَّةٍ عِندَ ذِي الْعَرْشِ مَكِينٍ
"Possessor of power and with the Owner of the Throne, secure [in position]"
[At-Takwir 81:20]
مُّطَاعٍ ثَمَّ أَمِينٍ
"Obeyed there [in the heavens] and trustworthy"
[At-Takwir 81:21]

Jibril is described with five qualities:

  1. Karīm (Noble): Not stingy, gives the complete message
  2. Dhi Quwwah (Possessor of Power): So powerful that no one can take anything from him - he could lift an entire city with the tip of his wing
  3. Makīn (Secure in Position): Has a high position with Allah
  4. Muṭā' (Obeyed): The angels obey him
  5. Amīn (Trustworthy): Never changes or omits even a single word - completely trustworthy

Application to Those Who Carry the Quran: These qualities should also be present in those who carry the Quran on earth:

  • Noble: Generous with knowledge, not stingy
  • Possessor of Power: Strong in faith and knowledge, cannot be swayed
  • Secure Position: Respected and honored by Allah and people
  • Obeyed: People should follow their guidance
  • Trustworthy: Never changes or distorts the message

The Prophet Muhammad (Peace Be Upon Him)

وَمَا صَاحِبُكُم بِمَجْنُونٍ
"And your companion is not [at all] mad"
[At-Takwir 81:22]
وَلَقَدْ رَآهُ بِالْأُفُقِ الْمُبِينِ
"And he has already seen Gabriel in the clear horizon"
[At-Takwir 81:23]

The Prophet (peace be upon him) is described as:

  • Not mad: He is not insane or possessed
  • Not stingy with the unseen: He doesn't withhold knowledge of the unseen - he is eager to guide people
  • Saw Jibril clearly: He saw Jibril in the clear horizon with his own eyes - this establishes the chain of transmission (isnād) of the Quran
وَمَا هُوَ عَلَى الْغَيْبِ بِضَنِينٍ
"And he is not a withholder of [knowledge of] the unseen"
[At-Takwir 81:24]
وَمَا هُوَ بِقَوْلِ شَيْطَانٍ رَّجِيمٍ
"And it [the Quran] is not the word of a devil, expelled [from the heavens]"
[At-Takwir 81:25]

The Quran is not the word of an accursed devil. Allah challenges: "If all of mankind and jinn were to gather to produce the like of this Quran, they could not produce its like, even if they were to help one another" (Al-Isra 17:88).

The Final Question: So Where Are You Going?

فَأَيْنَ تَذْهَبُونَ
"So where are you going?"
[At-Takwir 81:26]

After all the descriptions of the Day of Judgment and the proof that the Quran is from Allah, Allah asks: "So where are you going?" Where will you turn? What will you do?

إِنَّهُ لَا يَذْكَرُ لِلْعَالَمِينَ
"Indeed, it [the Quran] is not except a reminder to the worlds"
[At-Takwir 81:27]
لِمَن شَاءَ مِنكُمْ أَن يَسْتَقِيمَ
"For whoever wills among you to take a straight course"
[At-Takwir 81:28]
وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَن يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ
"And you do not will except that Allah wills - Lord of the worlds"
[At-Takwir 81:29]

The Quran is a reminder for all the worlds, but it benefits those who want to be straight. However, even the will to be straight comes from Allah. One must turn to Allah and ask for guidance.

Critical Point: There is no straightness (istiqāmah) without the Quran. It is impossible for a person to be on the straight path without the Quran. Every day, Muslims pray: "Guide us to the straight path" - this guidance comes through the Quran.

Key Lessons and Insights

1. The Reality of the Day of Judgment

This surah makes the Day of Judgment so vivid that it's as if you're seeing it with your own eyes. The twelve scenes paint a complete picture of the end of this world and the beginning of the Hereafter.

2. Everything Changes Except Deeds

The most important lesson: In all the cosmic changes, everything transforms except your deeds. They will come with you and never leave you. This emphasizes the importance of preparing good deeds.

3. The Quran is Eternal

While the sun will be extinguished, stars will fall, and the universe will change, the Quran remains constant. It will accompany you to the Hereafter and intercede for you.

4. The Chain of Transmission

The surah establishes the isnād (chain of transmission) of the Quran: Allah → Jibril → Muhammad (peace be upon him). This proves the Quran's authenticity.

5. The Need for Reflection

These verses require deep reflection and living the scenes, not just reading. One should read them in seclusion, away from distractions, to truly experience the Day of Judgment.

6. The Promise of Security

Allah does not combine two securities or two fears for a servant. If you fear Allah in this world, He will give you security in the Hereafter. If you feel secure from Allah in this world, He will make you fearful in the Hereafter.

Conclusion

Surah At-Takwir is one of the most powerful descriptions of the Day of Judgment in the Quran. It takes the reader through twelve vivid scenes that make the Hereafter feel immediate and real. The surah emphasizes that while everything in the universe will change, your deeds will remain with you.

It also establishes the authenticity of the Quran as divine revelation, coming through the noble messenger Jibril to the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), and calls people to reflect: "So where are you going?"

The surah should be read with deep reflection and contemplation, allowing the scenes to transform one's perspective on life and the Hereafter. As the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, reading these surahs is like seeing the Day of Judgment with your own eyes.

Source: This comprehensive guide is based on the tafsir (exegesis) khutbah (sermon) of Dr. Ahmed Abd al-Moneim from his course "Basa'ir Qur'aniyya" (Quranic Insights), specifically covering Surah At-Takwir.
