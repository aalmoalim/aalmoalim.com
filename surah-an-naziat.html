Everything You Need to Know About Surah An-Nazi'at (Those Who Pull Out)

Introduction: Surah An-Nazi'at continues the powerful themes introduced in Surah An-Naba. While An-Naba focused on "The Great News" and the Day of Judgment, An-Nazi'at begins with the moment of death itself - the extraction of the soul. This surah emphasizes the reality of death, the power of Allah over all creation, and the ultimate division of humanity into two groups: those who transgress and those who fear their Lord.

The Opening: The Angels Who Extract Souls

وَالنَّازِعَاتِ غَرْقًا
"By those [angels] who extract [souls] with violence"
[An-Nazi'at 79:1]

The surah opens with a powerful oath by Allah, swearing by different types of angels. The first scene is terrifying: angels extracting the souls of disbelievers with great difficulty and force.

The Opening Scene: Unlike Surah An-Naba which began with people questioning, this surah begins with the moment of death itself. The first sound you "hear" when opening this surah is the sound of a soul being violently extracted - the death rattle of someone dying a difficult death.

The word نَزَع (naza'a) means to pull out something that is firmly attached, requiring force and resistance. It's not a gentle removal, but a forceful extraction. The word غَرْقًا (gharqan) means to the utmost extent - the angels pull with all their might.

This describes the death of the disbeliever whose soul clings desperately to this world. The soul has become so attached to worldly pleasures that it resists leaving, spreading throughout the body, hiding in every limb, every nerve, every part of the body. The angels must extract it forcefully from every part.

The Angels Who Extract Gently

وَالنَّاشِطَاتِ نَشْطًا
"And those [angels] who remove [souls] gently"
[An-Nazi'at 79:2]

In stark contrast, the second type of angels extract souls gently and easily. The word نَشَط (nashat) means to untie or release something that was bound. It's like untying a knot - quick and easy.

The Contrast:

  • An-Nazi'at (Those who extract with violence): The death of the disbeliever - difficult, painful, resisted
  • An-Nashitat (Those who extract gently): The death of the believer - easy, smooth, like a drop of water from a water-skin

The believer's soul exits easily because:

  • The believer felt imprisoned in this world - "The world is a prison for the believer" (Hadith)
  • The believer longed for the meeting with Allah
  • The believer's soul was disciplined and trained to obey
  • The believer's soul was purified and refined

When the angel says "Come out" to the believer's soul, it exits immediately, like water pouring from an overturned vessel. There's no resistance because the soul was already prepared and trained.

The Angels Who Swim and Race

وَالسَّابِحَاتِ سَبْحًا
"And those [angels] who swim [through space]"
[An-Nazi'at 79:3]
فَالسَّابِقَاتِ سَبْقًا
"Then those who race ahead"
[An-Nazi'at 79:4]
فَالْمُدَبِّرَاتِ أَمْرًا
"Then those who arrange [each] matter"
[An-Nazi'at 79:5]

After extracting the soul, the angels carry it through the heavens. They:

  1. Swim (yasbahun): Move smoothly through space without obstacles
  2. Race (yasbiqun): Compete with each other to reach Allah quickly
  3. Arrange matters (yudabbirun): Execute Allah's commands with precision

These angels race with the souls of believers, and the speed of their race depends on the quality of the believer's deeds. The foremost in good deeds will be brought forward first - "The foremost, the foremost - those are the ones brought near [to Allah]" (Al-Waqi'ah 56:10-11).

Lesson for Believers: When Allah commands something, the believer should follow the example of the angels: first swim (move smoothly without obstacles), then race (compete in good deeds), then execute with precision and planning.

The Day of Trembling

يَوْمَ تَرْجُفُ الرَّاجِفَةُ
"On the Day the first blast shivers"
[An-Nazi'at 79:6]
تَتْبَعُهَا الرَّادِفَةُ
"Followed by the second blast"
[An-Nazi'at 79:7]

After describing the angels and their work, Allah now describes the Day of Resurrection. Scholars differ on what الرَّاجِفَة (ar-rājifah) and الرَّادِفَة (ar-rādifah) refer to:

  • Most common view: The first blast (rājifah) causes all to die, the second blast (rādifah) causes all to be resurrected
  • Alternative view: The first refers to the earth shaking, the second to the heavens shaking

This is a day that should never be forgotten - a day of absolute power and control by Allah over all creation.

Hearts Will Tremble

قُلُوبٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ وَاجِفَةٌ
"Hearts that Day will tremble"
[An-Nazi'at 79:8]
أَبْصَارُهَا خَاشِعَةٌ
"Their eyes humbled"
[An-Nazi'at 79:9]

The word وَاجِفَة (wājifah) means trembling with fear and anxiety, like a horse's heart beating rapidly when whipped to run. On that Day, hearts will be in a state of extreme fear and confusion.

Notice that Allah says أَبْصَارُهَا (absāruhā - their eyes) referring to the eyes of the hearts, meaning the entire body will be in a state of humility and submission. When the heart trembles, the whole body trembles.

The Three Stages of Khashyah (Fear/Awe): The surah mentions three stages that were rejected by the disbelievers:

  1. "I guide you to your Lord, so you would fear" - Rejected
  2. "Indeed in that is a lesson for whoever would fear" - Rejected
  3. "You are only a warner to whoever fears it" - They will now experience the reality

The Mockery of the Disbelievers

يَقُولُونَ أَئِنَّا لَمَرْدُودُونَ فِي الْحَافِرَةِ
"They say, 'Are we indeed to be restored to [our] former state?'"
[An-Nazi'at 79:10]
أَئِذَا كُنَّا عِظَامًا نَّخِرَةً
"Even when we are decayed bones?"
[An-Nazi'at 79:11]

The disbelievers mock the idea of resurrection. They say: "Are we really going to be returned to our original state, even when we've become decayed bones?"

The word نَّخِرَةً (nakhiratan) means decayed, hollow, crumbled - bones that have completely disintegrated. They emphasize this to make resurrection seem impossible.

Propaganda Technique: The disbelievers would bring decayed bones, blow on them to show how hollow and crumbled they were, and mock: "Look! The messenger claims these will come back to life!" This was their media campaign to mislead people.

قَالُوا تِلْكَ إِذًا كَرَّةٌ خَاسِرَةٌ
"They say, 'That, then, would be a losing return'"
[An-Nazi'at 79:12]

Even in their mockery, they acknowledge the truth: "If this really happens, we would be losers!" They know deep down that if resurrection is real, they have lost because they did no good deeds. But they say this mockingly, not believing it will actually happen.

Allah's Response: A Single Command

فَإِنَّمَا هِيَ زَجْرَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ
"But it will be only one shout"
[An-Nazi'at 79:13]
فَإِذَا هُم بِالسَّاهِرَةِ
"And suddenly they will be [alert] upon the earth's surface"
[An-Nazi'at 79:14]

Allah responds: What they consider impossible will happen with a single command - one blast of the Trumpet, and they will be standing on the earth's surface, resurrected.

The word السَّاهِرَةِ (as-sāhirah) refers to the gathering place on the Day of Judgment. It's called "sāhirah" (awake/vigilant) because:

  • There will be no sleep there - sleep has ended forever
  • There is no place to hide or take refuge
  • Everyone will be fully alert and aware

The Story of Pharaoh: A Warning Example

هَلْ أَتَاكَ حَدِيثُ مُوسَىٰ
"Has there reached you the story of Moses?"
[An-Nazi'at 79:15]

Allah presents the story of Pharaoh as a warning example for the disbelievers of Makkah. Pharaoh represents the ultimate transgressor - someone who claimed divinity and rejected all limits.

إِذْ نَادَاهُ رَبُّهُ بِالْوَادِ الْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًى
"When his Lord called him in the sacred valley of Tuwa"
[An-Nazi'at 79:16]

Allah called Moses from above the seven heavens to go to a tyrant. This shows Allah's mercy - even before punishing Pharaoh, Allah sent a messenger to guide him, hoping he might repent.

اذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ إِنَّهُ طَغَىٰ
"Go to Pharaoh. Indeed, he has transgressed"
[An-Nazi'at 79:17]

Important Lesson for Da'wah: The duty of calling to Islam includes addressing tyrants and oppressors. The message must reach even the most powerful and arrogant. This is an obligation - the word of truth must reach everyone, regardless of their status.

The Offer of Purification

فَقُلْ هَل لَّكَ إِلَىٰ أَن تَزَكَّىٰ
"And say to him, 'Would you [be willing to] purify yourself?'"
[An-Nazi'at 79:18]
وَأَهْدِيَكَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَتَخْشَىٰ
"And let me guide you to your Lord so you would fear [Him]?'"
[An-Nazi'at 79:19]

Moses was instructed to offer Pharaoh purification and guidance. The word تَزَكَّىٰ (tazakkā) means to purify and grow. It has two meanings:

  1. Purification: Removing impurities and sins from the soul
  2. Growth: The soul grows and increases in closeness to Allah

The Relationship Between Tazkiyah and Khashyah:

  • First stage - Tazkiyah: Purification of the soul from sins and impurities
  • Second stage - Khashyah: Fear and awe of Allah that comes after purification

One cannot truly fear Allah until the soul is first purified. The purified soul can then properly recognize Allah's greatness and develop genuine fear and awe.

The contrast is powerful: Pharaoh had chosen طَغَىٰ (taghā - to transgress, to exceed limits), which is also a form of "increase" but in the wrong direction. Moses offers تَزَكَّىٰ (tazakkā - purification and growth), which is increase in the right direction - growth with purity, not transgression.

Pharaoh's Rejection

فَأَرَاهُ الْآيَةَ الْكُبْرَىٰ
"So he showed him the greatest sign"
[An-Nazi'at 79:20]
فَكَذَّبَ وَعَصَىٰ
"But [Pharaoh] denied and disobeyed"
[An-Nazi'at 79:21]
ثُمَّ أَدْبَرَ يَسْعَىٰ
"Then he turned back, striving [against the message]"
[An-Nazi'at 79:22]

Pharaoh saw the greatest sign (Moses' staff turning into a snake), but he didn't give himself a chance to think or reflect. He immediately:

  1. Denied (kadhdhaba): Rejected the truth
  2. Disobeyed (asā): Actively resisted and fought against it
  3. Turned away striving (adbara yas'ā): Ran away to gather his forces

The word يَسْعَىٰ (yas'ā) means to strive or hurry - not quite running (which would show fear) but not walking calmly either. He was in a state of panic, trying to maintain his composure while rushing to protect his kingdom.

فَحَشَرَ فَنَادَىٰ
"So he assembled [his people] and called out"
[An-Nazi'at 79:23]
فَقَالَ أَنَا رَبُّكُمُ الْأَعْلَىٰ
"And said, 'I am your most exalted lord'"
[An-Nazi'at 79:24]

Pharaoh gathered all his forces and declared: "I am your most exalted lord!" This was the ultimate transgression - claiming divinity. This statement became the reason for his immediate punishment.

The Disease of Transgression (Taghā): This is the most dangerous disease of the soul - when the soul becomes so arrogant that it exceeds all limits and even claims what belongs only to Allah. This is what Pharaoh represents - the soul that refuses to be disciplined and purified.

فَأَخَذَهُ اللَّهُ نَكَالَ الْآخِرَةِ وَالْأُولَىٰ
"So Allah seized him in exemplary punishment for the last [transgression] and the first"
[An-Nazi'at 79:25]
إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَعِبْرَةً لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ
"Indeed in that is a warning for whoever would fear [Allah]"
[An-Nazi'at 79:26]

Allah seized Pharaoh with exemplary punishment - punishment that serves as a warning to others. The word نَكَال (nakāl) means exemplary punishment that deters others from committing the same crime.

Allah punished him for both his first transgression and his last (claiming divinity). When someone commits a major sin and then adds an even greater sin to it, Allah may delay punishment until they commit the greater sin, then punish them for both.

Allah's Absolute Power Over Creation

أَأَنتُمْ أَشَدُّ خَلْقًا أَمِ السَّمَاءُ ۚ بَنَاهَا
"Are you a more difficult creation or is the heaven? He constructed it"
[An-Nazi'at 79:27]

Allah challenges the disbelievers: "Are you harder to create than the heaven?" This is a rhetorical question - of course the heavens are more magnificent and complex. If Allah can create the heavens, creating humans is easy for Him.

رَفَعَ سَمْكَهَا فَسَوَّاهَا
"He raised its ceiling and proportioned it"
[An-Nazi'at 79:28]
وَأَغْطَشَ لَيْلَهَا وَأَخْرَجَ ضُحَاهَا
"And He darkened its night and extracted its brightness"
[An-Nazi'at 79:29]

Allah describes His absolute control: He raised the heaven, made it level, darkened the night, and brought forth the day. Every command is executed immediately - "He said... and it was."

وَالْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ ذَٰلِكَ دَحَاهَا
"And the earth after that He spread"
[An-Nazi'at 79:30]
أَخْرَجَ مِنْهَا مَاءَهَا وَمَرْعَاهَا
"He extracted from it its water and its pasture"
[An-Nazi'at 79:31]
وَالْجِبَالَ أَرْسَاهَا
"And the mountains He set firmly"
[An-Nazi'at 79:32]

Allah spread the earth, brought forth water and vegetation from solid rock, and firmly set the mountains. All of this demonstrates absolute power and control.

مَتَاعًا لَّكُمْ وَلِأَنْعَامِكُمْ
"As provision for you and your grazing livestock"
[An-Nazi'at 79:33]

The Difference Between Humans and Animals: Both humans and animals benefit from these cosmic signs. The difference is that humans should reflect on these signs, remember Allah, and be grateful. Animals simply consume without reflection. If humans don't reflect and remember Allah, they become like animals - "They are like livestock. Rather, they are more astray in [their] way" (Al-Furqan 25:44).

The Overwhelming Calamity

فَإِذَا جَاءَتِ الطَّامَّةُ الْكُبْرَىٰ
"But when there comes the greatest Overwhelming Calamity"
[An-Nazi'at 79:34]

The word الطَّامَّة (at-tāmmah) means a calamity that covers everything. The الْكُبْرَىٰ (al-kubrā - the greatest) means there is nothing greater than it.

Different Names for the Day of Judgment:

  • As-Sākhkhah (The Deafening Blast): Affects the ears - deafening
  • Al-Ghāshiyah (The Overwhelming): Affects the eyes - overwhelming vision
  • Al-Qāri'ah (The Striking): Affects the hearts - striking fear
  • At-Tāmmah al-Kubrā (The Greatest Overwhelming Calamity): Affects the mind - covers all thought

This calamity will cover the mind so completely that people will forget everything except one thing...

يَوْمَ يَتَذَكَّرُ الْإِنسَانُ مَا سَعَىٰ
"On the Day a man will remember what he strove for"
[An-Nazi'at 79:35]

On that Day, a person will remember in detail everything they did. The word يَتَذَكَّر (yatadhakkar) indicates detailed remembrance, not just general recall. They will remember every moment, every intention, every action.

The Connection: Earlier in the surah, Pharaoh "turned away striving" (أَدْبَرَ يَسْعَىٰ). On the Day of Judgment, all those who strove against the truth will remember their striving and see its consequences.

وَبُرِّزَتِ الْجَحِيمُ لِمَن يَرَىٰ
"And Hellfire will be exposed for [all] who see"
[An-Nazi'at 79:36]

Despite all the media campaigns to hide and deny the reality of Hellfire, it will be exposed for all to see. All the efforts to distract people from thinking about the Hereafter will be in vain.

The Two Paths: The Ultimate Division

فَأَمَّا مَن طَغَىٰ
"So as for he who transgressed"
[An-Nazi'at 79:37]
وَآثَرَ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا
"And preferred the life of this world"
[An-Nazi'at 79:38]
فَإِنَّ الْجَحِيمَ هِيَ الْمَأْوَىٰ
"Then indeed, Hellfire will be [his] refuge"
[An-Nazi'at 79:39]

The first group: Those who transgressed and preferred this worldly life. The word آثَر (āthara) means to choose something that will remain, thinking it will last. They chose the lower life (الدُّنْيَا - ad-dunyā means "the lower/closer"), thinking it would be permanent.

The word الْمَأْوَىٰ (al-ma'wā) means a place of refuge - a place you go to when you're tired, afraid, or need rest. But Hellfire will be the opposite - a place of torment, not rest.

وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ وَنَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَىٰ
"But as for he who feared the position of his Lord and prevented the soul from [unlawful] inclination"
[An-Nazi'at 79:40]
فَإِنَّ الْجَنَّةَ هِيَ الْمَأْوَىٰ
"Then indeed, Paradise will be [his] refuge"
[An-Nazi'at 79:41]

The second group: Those who feared standing before their Lord and prevented their souls from following base desires.

The Central Theme: Disciplining the Soul

This surah focuses on the soul throughout:

  1. Beginning: The soul being extracted (violently or gently)
  2. Middle: The soul being offered purification (tazkiyah)
  3. End: The soul being disciplined (preventing it from following desires)

The key is: Whoever disciplines their soul in this world will have an easy death. Whoever indulges their soul will have a difficult death.

Preventing the Soul from Following Desires

The phrase وَنَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَىٰ (wa nahā an-nafsa 'anil-hawā) is crucial. It doesn't mean to eliminate desires from the soul - that's impossible. It means to say "No" to the soul when it wants something unlawful.

Understanding "Hawā" (Desire): The word هَوَىٰ (hawā) has two meanings in Arabic:

  • Emptiness/Void: The soul becomes empty of good
  • Falling: The soul falls into sin

When the soul is empty of good, it falls into evil. The solution is not to eliminate desires, but to discipline them - to say "No" when the soul wants something wrong.

The contrast is clear:

  • Those who transgress: Their souls are not disciplined. When the soul wants something, they say "Yes." The soul becomes spoiled and dominant. At death, the soul resists leaving, spreading throughout the body, requiring violent extraction.
  • Those who fear Allah: They constantly say "No" to their souls. The soul becomes disciplined and obedient. At death, the soul exits easily, like a drop of water from a water-skin.

The Relationship Between Disciplining the Soul and Ease of Death:

  • The more you discipline your soul in this world, the easier your death will be
  • The more you indulge your soul, the more difficult your death will be
  • A disciplined soul exits smoothly (an-nashitat)
  • An undisciplined soul must be violently extracted (an-nazi'at)

Fearing the Standing Before Allah

The phrase خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ (khāfa maqāma rabbih) means to fear standing before Allah on the Day of Judgment - standing alone, naked, accountable for every deed. This fear helps a person discipline their soul.

This is the moment that tyrants and oppressors hate - the moment when they will stand as equals with everyone else, when all their power and status will be meaningless. This is why they reject the concept of accountability.

Questions About the Hour

يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ السَّاعَةِ أَيَّانَ مُرْسَاهَا
"They ask you, [O Muhammad], about the Hour: when is its arrival?"
[An-Nazi'at 79:42]

After all the warnings and descriptions, the disbelievers still ask mockingly: "When will the Hour come?" This is not a genuine question seeking knowledge, but mockery and dismissal.

فِيمَ أَنتَ مِن ذِكْرَاهَا
"In what [position] are you that you should mention it?"
[An-Nazi'at 79:43]
إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ مُنتَهَاهَا
"To your Lord is its finality"
[An-Nazi'at 79:44]

Allah tells the Prophet (peace be upon him): "You have no knowledge of when it will occur. That knowledge belongs only to Allah." This is a warning to da'wah workers: Don't get distracted by questions meant to divert you from your main message.

إِنَّمَا أَنتَ مُنذِرُ مَن يَخْشَاهَا
"You are only a warner to those who fear it"
[An-Nazi'at 79:45]

Important Lesson for Da'wah: Not every question from disbelievers should be answered. Some questions are meant to distract and waste time. The role of the da'iyah (caller to Islam) is to warn those who have fear in their hearts - those who are receptive. Don't waste time with those who are only interested in mockery and argumentation.

The Final Scene: As If They Had Stayed Only an Evening or Morning

كَأَنَّهُمْ يَوْمَ يَرَوْنَهَا لَمْ يَلْبَثُوا إِلَّا عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَاهَا
"It will be, on the Day they see it, as though they had not remained [in the world] except for an afternoon or a morning thereof"
[An-Nazi'at 79:46]

This is a powerful contrast. The disbelievers were asking "When will it come?" as if it were far away, extending their worldly life in their minds. But when they actually see the Day of Judgment, they will realize that their entire worldly life felt like just an afternoon or morning - so brief, so insignificant.

The Contrast:

  • Before: "When will it come?" - treating it as if it were far away, extending life in their imagination
  • After: "We only stayed an afternoon or morning" - realizing how brief life actually was

This is the Quran's way of addressing denial - it speaks about the denied reality as if it has already happened, making it more impactful and real.

Key Lessons and Insights

1. The Reality of Death

This surah emphasizes that death is more impactful than the Hereafter because it's what people actually experience. The surah begins with death to make the message more powerful and immediate.

2. The Two Types of Death

The surah clearly shows two types of death:

  • Violent extraction (an-nazi'at): For those whose souls are undisciplined and attached to this world
  • Gentle release (an-nashitat): For those whose souls are disciplined and prepared for the meeting with Allah

3. The Disease of Transgression (Taghā)

The surah identifies transgression as the most dangerous disease of the soul. It's when the soul exceeds all limits and refuses to be disciplined. Pharaoh is the ultimate example of this disease.

4. The Importance of Disciplining the Soul

The central theme is disciplining the soul by:

  • Constantly saying "No" to unlawful desires
  • Remembering the standing before Allah
  • Purifying the soul (tazkiyah)
  • Developing fear of Allah (khashyah)

5. The Two Paths

The surah clearly divides humanity into two groups:

  • Those who transgress and prefer this world: Destination - Hellfire
  • Those who fear Allah and discipline their souls: Destination - Paradise

6. Lessons for Da'wah

  • Don't get distracted by mockery and argumentative questions
  • Focus on warning those who have fear in their hearts
  • The message must reach even tyrants and oppressors
  • Present the reality as certain, not as a possibility

Conclusion

Surah An-Nazi'at continues the powerful themes of Juz Amma, focusing specifically on the moment of death and the ultimate division of humanity. It emphasizes the importance of disciplining the soul in this world, as this determines not only one's destination in the Hereafter, but even the manner of one's death.

The surah teaches us that the soul must be trained and disciplined. Those who constantly say "No" to their souls when they want something wrong will have an easy death and a good ending. Those who always say "Yes" will have a difficult death and a bad ending.

It presents Pharaoh as a warning example - someone who had the opportunity for purification and guidance but chose transgression instead, leading to his destruction. The surah calls us to choose the opposite path: fear of Allah and discipline of the soul.
