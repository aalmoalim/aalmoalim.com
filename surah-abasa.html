Everything You Need to Know About Surah Abasa (He Frowned)

Introduction: Surah Abasa is a Makkan surah that contains a gentle admonition to the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) regarding an incident with Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum, a blind companion. However, the surah's significance goes far beyond this single incident - it establishes crucial principles for da'wah (calling to Islam) that apply to all callers to faith. The surah teaches about priorities in da'wah, treating all people equally regardless of their social status, and the importance of not being distracted from the core mission.

Important Note: This surah contains an admonition to the Prophet (peace be upon him), but this should be understood in context. The Prophet was human and could make minor mistakes in matters not related to revelation. This incident was corrected immediately by Allah, showing that even the best of creation can benefit from guidance. The Prophet's status and honor remain intact, and this incident actually demonstrates Allah's care in perfecting the da'wah methodology.

The Opening: The Admonition

عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ
"He frowned and turned away"
[Abasa 80:1]
أَن جَاءَهُ الْأَعْمَىٰ
"Because there came to him the blind man"
[Abasa 80:2]

The surah opens with a description of facial expression - the Prophet (peace be upon him) frowned and turned away when the blind man (Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum) came to him. This is significant because it shows that even facial expressions matter in da'wah.

The Incident: The Prophet (peace be upon him) was engaged in calling the leaders and nobles of Quraysh to Islam. At that moment, Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum, a blind companion, came seeking guidance. The Prophet, focused on the potential conversion of the Quraysh leaders (hoping their acceptance would help spread Islam), may have shown some displeasure in his facial expression when the blind man interrupted. This was immediately corrected by Allah through this revelation.

The Importance of Facial Expressions in Da'wah

The surah begins with facial expressions (عَبَسَ - he frowned), teaching us that:

  • Facial expressions communicate messages to people
  • A da'iyah (caller to Islam) must be aware of their facial expressions
  • Even a frown can hurt someone's feelings and push them away
  • The Prophet (peace be upon him) was known for his cheerful countenance - this incident was an exception that Allah corrected

Even though the blind man couldn't see the frown, the fact that it happened is significant. It shows that a da'iyah must maintain a pleasant expression at all times, as it affects how people perceive the message.

Turning Away to What Is More Important

The word تَوَلَّىٰ (tawallā) means to turn away to something considered more important. The Prophet turned from the blind man to focus on the Quraysh leaders. This raises a crucial question: How should priorities be determined in da'wah?

Critical Lesson: Priorities in da'wah should not be based on:

  • Personal preferences
  • Material considerations
  • Social status
  • Group affiliations
  • Personal relationships
  • Worldly benefits

Priorities must be based on divine criteria established by Allah in the Quran and Sunnah.

What Do You Know? Perhaps He Will Be Purified

وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُ يَزَّكَّىٰ
"And what would make you know, [O Muhammad], that perhaps he might be purified"
[Abasa 80:3]

Allah asks the Prophet: "What do you know?" This is a fundamental principle for all da'iyahs: You cannot judge a person's potential based on initial impressions.

Key Principle: A da'iyah should never make initial judgments about who will benefit from the message. You don't know:

  • What good is hidden within a person
  • What evil is hidden within a person
  • How the Quranic verses will affect them
  • What role they might play in spreading the religion

Your role is to expose people to the Quranic verses. What happens after that is in Allah's hands.

Two Possible Outcomes

أَوْ يَذَّكَّرُ فَتَنفَعَهُ الذِّكْرَىٰ
"Or be reminded and the remembrance would benefit him?"
[Abasa 80:4]

When you expose someone to the Quran, one of two things will happen:

  1. He will be purified (yazakkā): The highest outcome - the verses penetrate the heart, the soul is purified, and the person grows in faith
  2. He will be reminded (yadhakkar): The information enters the mind and may benefit him later, even if it doesn't immediately change his heart

The Stages of Impact:

  1. Recitation (Tilawah): You recite the verses to them
  2. Purification (Tazkiyah): The verses purify their souls
  3. Teaching (Ta'lim): They learn the Book and wisdom with a pure heart

Even if purification doesn't happen immediately, the reminder may benefit them in the future when circumstances change or when they face trials.

The Two Types of People

أَمَّا مَنِ اسْتَغْنَىٰ
"As for he who thinks himself without need"
[Abasa 80:5]
فَأَنتَ لَهُ تَصَدَّىٰ
"To him you give attention"
[Abasa 80:6]

The first type: Those who think themselves self-sufficient (اسْتَغْنَىٰ - istaghnā). They believe they don't need the message, don't need guidance, don't need religion. To such people, you give excessive attention (تَصَدَّىٰ - tasaddā), meaning you exhaust yourself trying to reach them.

وَمَا عَلَيْكَ أَلَّا يَزَّكَّىٰ
"And not upon you [is any blame] if he will not be purified"
[Abasa 80:7]

Allah reassures: You are not responsible if they don't accept. This provides psychological balance for da'iyahs - you are not required to make everyone accept. Your duty is to convey the message, not to force acceptance.

وَأَمَّا مَن جَاءَكَ يَسْعَىٰ
"But as for he who came to you striving [for knowledge]"
[Abasa 80:8]
وَهُوَ يَخْشَىٰ
"While he fears [Allah]"
[Abasa 80:9]
فَأَنتَ عَنْهُ تَلَهَّىٰ
"From him you are distracted"
[Abasa 80:10]

The second type: Those who come to you striving (يَسْعَىٰ - yas'ā), making effort, and fearing Allah. These are the ones you should focus on, but in this incident, attention was diverted from them.

The Divine Criterion: When there's a conflict in priorities, choose based on:

  • Those who come seeking (al-muqbil): Priority over those who turn away (al-mu'rid)
  • Those who strive and fear Allah: Priority over those who think themselves self-sufficient

This is NOT about rich vs. poor. It's about seeking vs. turning away, humility vs. arrogance.

The Word "Talahhā" (You Are Distracted)

The word تَلَهَّىٰ (talahhā) is significant. It means to be distracted or to engage in something that diverts you from what's more important. When a da'iyah focuses on those who turn away and neglects those who come seeking, this is called "talahhā" - being distracted from priorities.

Types of Distraction (Talahhā) for Da'iyahs:

  • Focusing on the self-sufficient while neglecting those who seek
  • Engaging in arguments and debates meant to distract
  • Responding to false accusations and slander instead of focusing on teaching
  • Getting involved in matters not relevant to the current needs of the da'wah
  • Doing good deeds that are less important while neglecting more important ones

No! Indeed, It Is a Reminder

كَلَّا إِنَّهَا تَذْكِرَةٌ
"No! Indeed, it is a reminder"
[Abasa 80:11]

The word كَلَّا (kallā) is a strong negation - "No!" This corrects the approach. Your function is to remind, not to force acceptance. The Quran is a reminder (تَذْكِرَة - tadhkirah).

فَمَن شَاءَ ذَكَرَهُ
"So whoever wills may remember it"
[Abasa 80:12]

Whoever wants to remember will remember. You cannot force it. Your role is to present the reminder clearly and honorably.

The Honored Pages

فِي صُحُفٍ مُّكَرَّمَةٍ
"In honored pages"
[Abasa 80:13]
مَّرْفُوعَةٍ مُّطَهَّرَةٍ
"Exalted and purified"
[Abasa 80:14]
بِأَيْدِي سَفَرَةٍ
"In the hands of messenger-angels"
[Abasa 80:15]
كِرَامٍ بَرَرَةٍ
"Noble and dutiful"
[Abasa 80:16]

The Quran is described as being in honored, exalted, and purified pages, carried by noble and dutiful messengers. This description also applies to those who carry the Quran on earth - the da'iyahs and scholars.

Qualities of Those Who Carry the Quran (Safarah - Messengers/Ambassadors):

  1. They carry the Quran in their hands: They always have the Quran with them, always refer to it, always move with it
  2. They are Safarah (Ambassadors): They reveal and explain the meanings of the Quran to people
  3. They are Karam (Noble): They are generous, not stingy with knowledge, and they don't let people's bad behavior affect their good character
  4. They are Barr (Dutiful/Righteous): They excel in doing good deeds, even to those who harm them

These qualities must be present in every da'iyah. The Quran must remain honored, exalted, and purified - never lowered or compromised to please people.

The Rejection of the Disbeliever

قُتِلَ الْإِنسَانُ مَا أَكْفَرَهُ
"Cursed is man; how disbelieving is he"
[Abasa 80:17]

Despite all the efforts, some people will still reject. The word قُتِلَ (qutila) is a form of curse or strong condemnation. "How disbelieving is he!" - expressing amazement at the extent of disbelief despite all the evidence.

From What Was He Created?

مِنْ أَيِّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقَهُ
"From what substance did He create him?"
[Abasa 80:18]
مِن نُّطْفَةٍ خَلَقَهُ فَقَدَّرَهُ
"From a sperm-drop He created him and destined for him"
[Abasa 80:19]

Allah reminds the arrogant disbeliever of his humble origin. He was created from a drop of fluid, then Allah determined everything about him - his form, his features, his lifespan, his provisions. He had no control over any of this.

ثُمَّ السَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُ
"Then He eased the way for him"
[Abasa 80:20]

Allah made the way easy for him to be born - he couldn't have exited his mother's womb without Allah's facilitation.

ثُمَّ أَمَاتَهُ فَأَقْبَرَهُ
"Then He causes his death and provides a grave for him"
[Abasa 80:21]
ثُمَّ إِذَا شَاءَ أَنشَرَهُ
"Then when He wills, He will resurrect him"
[Abasa 80:22]

Allah controls his death, his burial, and his resurrection. The disbeliever has no control over any of these matters, yet he arrogantly rejects the One who controls everything.

كَلَّا لَمَّا يَقْضِ مَا أَمَرَهُ
"No! But man has not yet completed what He commanded him"
[Abasa 80:23]

Despite all these favors, man has not fulfilled what Allah commanded him - to be grateful and to worship Him alone.

Let Man Look at His Food

فَلْيَنظُرِ الْإِنسَانُ إِلَىٰ طَعَامِهِ
"Then let mankind look at his food"
[Abasa 80:24]

If man wants to fulfill what Allah commanded him, let him look at and reflect upon his food - how it was created, how it reaches him, the variety and abundance of provisions.

أَنَّا صَبَبْنَا الْمَاءَ صَبًّا
"How We poured down water in torrents"
[Abasa 80:25]
ثُمَّ شَقَقْنَا الْأَرْضَ شَقًّا
"Then We broke open the earth, splitting [it with sprouts]"
[Abasa 80:26]
فَأَنبَتْنَا فِيهَا حَبًّا
"And caused to grow within it grain"
[Abasa 80:27]
وَعِنَبًا وَقَضْبًا
"And grapes and herbage"
[Abasa 80:28]
وَزَيْتُونًا وَنَخْلًا
"And olive and palm trees"
[Abasa 80:29]
وَحَدَائِقَ غُلْبًا
"And gardens of dense shrubbery"
[Abasa 80:30]
وَفَاكِهَةً وَأَبًّا
"And fruit and grass"
[Abasa 80:31]
مَّتَاعًا لَّكُمْ وَلِأَنْعَامِكُمْ
"[As] enjoyment for you and your grazing livestock"
[Abasa 80:32]

Allah describes the abundance and variety of provisions - water poured in torrents, earth split open, various grains, fruits, and gardens. All of this is provided for humans and their livestock. The difference between humans and animals is that humans should reflect on these signs and be grateful, while animals simply consume without reflection.

The Deafening Blast

فَإِذَا جَاءَتِ الصَّاخَّةُ
"But when there comes the Deafening Blast"
[Abasa 80:33]

The word الصَّاخَّةُ (as-sākhkhah) means the deafening blast - a sound so loud it deafens the ears. This is one of the names of the Day of Judgment, chosen here because the disbeliever refused to listen. On that Day, he will be forced to hear.

يَوْمَ يَفِرُّ الْمَرْءُ مِنْ أَخِيهِ
"On the Day a man will flee from his brother"
[Abasa 80:34]
وَأُمِّهِ وَأَبِيهِ
"And his mother and his father"
[Abasa 80:35]
وَصَاحِبَتِهِ وَبَنِيهِ
"And his wife and his children"
[Abasa 80:36]

On that Day, a person will flee from those closest to him - starting from the most distant relationship (brother) to the closest (children). Each person will be concerned only with their own affair.

Contrast with Surah Al-Ma'arij: In Surah Al-Ma'arij, the order is reversed - starting with children (closest) to tribe (most distant). The difference:

  • Here (Abasa): Describes fleeing - starting from distant relationships and moving closer
  • Al-Ma'arij: Describes trying to ransom - starting with the most precious (children) and moving outward
لِكُلِّ امْرِئٍ مِّنْهُمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ شَأْنٌ يُغْنِيهِ
"For every person, that Day, will be a matter adequate for him"
[Abasa 80:37]

Each person will be so preoccupied with their own situation that they cannot help anyone else. Notice the connection: In this world, the disbeliever was preoccupied with wealth and children, neglecting religion. On that Day, he will be preoccupied with his own affair, unable to help his wealth or children.

The Two Groups on the Day of Judgment

وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ مُّسْفِرَةٌ
"[Some] faces, that Day, will be bright"
[Abasa 80:38]
ضَاحِكَةٌ مُّسْتَبْشِرَةٌ
"Laughing, rejoicing at good news"
[Abasa 80:39]

The first group: Bright faces (مُّسْفِرَة - musfirah), laughing, rejoicing. These are the da'iyahs and those who called to Allah. The word مُّسْفِرَة comes from the same root as سَفَرَة (safarah - ambassadors) - those who illuminated the path for people in this world will have illuminated faces in the Hereafter.

وَوُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ عَلَيْهَا غَبَرَةٌ
"And [other] faces, that Day, will have upon them dust"
[Abasa 80:40]
تَرْهَقُهَا قَتَرَةٌ
"Covered with [black] dust"
[Abasa 80:41]
أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْكَفَرَةُ الْفَجَرَةُ
"Those are the disbelievers, the wicked ones"
[Abasa 80:42]

The second group: Faces covered with dust (غَبَرَة - ghabarah) and blackness (قَتَرَة - qatarah). These are the disbelievers and the wicked.

The Symbolism:

  • Dust (Ghabarah): Represents disbelief (kufr) - they covered their fitrah (natural disposition) with the dust of disbelief, preventing them from hearing and seeing the truth
  • Blackness (Qatarah): Represents sinfulness (fujūr) - each sin adds blackness to the face

Just as they combined disbelief and sinfulness in this world, their faces will combine dust and blackness in the Hereafter.

Key Lessons and Insights

1. Priorities in Da'wah

The surah establishes that priorities must be based on divine criteria, not worldly considerations. Those who come seeking should be prioritized over those who turn away, regardless of social or economic status.

2. The Danger of Worldly Criteria

When da'iyahs begin to use worldly criteria (wealth, status, group affiliation) instead of divine criteria, this is the beginning of deviation from the correct path of da'wah.

3. Don't Judge Potential

A da'iyah should never make initial judgments about who will benefit. You don't know what good or evil is hidden within a person, or how the Quranic verses will affect them.

4. The Role of the Da'iyah

The da'iyah's role is to:

  • Recite the verses (tilawah)
  • Present them in an honored, exalted manner
  • Not be responsible for acceptance - that's between the person and Allah

5. Qualities of Those Who Carry the Quran

They must be:

  • Always carrying the Quran (in their hands, in their speech, in their actions)
  • Revealing and explaining its meanings (safarah)
  • Noble and generous (karam)
  • Excelling in good deeds (barr)

6. The Reminder of Human Weakness

The surah reminds the arrogant disbeliever of his humble origin and complete dependence on Allah, showing that arrogance has no basis.

7. The Two Outcomes

On the Day of Judgment, there will be two groups:

  • Bright, laughing, rejoicing faces: The da'iyahs and believers
  • Dust-covered, blackened faces: The disbelievers and wicked

Conclusion

Surah Abasa is crucial for all da'iyahs and those working for Islam. It establishes fundamental principles for da'wah that prevent deviation from the correct path. It teaches that priorities must be based on divine criteria, that all people should be treated with respect regardless of their status, and that the da'iyah's role is to present the message clearly and honorably, leaving the rest to Allah.

The surah also serves as a reminder of human weakness and dependence on Allah, and it describes the ultimate outcomes for those who call to truth versus those who reject it.

Most importantly, it warns against the subtle deviation that can occur when da'iyahs begin to prioritize based on worldly considerations rather than divine guidance - a deviation that can lead the entire da'wah effort astray.
