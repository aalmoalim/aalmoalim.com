The Illness That Permits Breaking the Fast

Imam al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said, reporting from al-Qadi 'Iyad: The majority of scholars are of the view that the illness that permits breaking the fast is that which makes fasting difficult. And some of them permitted it for every sick person. This is the end of al-Qadi's statement.(1)

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, also said: The sick person who is unable to fast due to an illness from which recovery is hoped, fasting is not required upon him in the present, and making up is required upon him, due to what the author mentioned. This is if apparent hardship befalls him by fasting, and it is not a condition that it reaches a state in which fasting is impossible for him. Rather, our companions said: The condition for the permissibility of breaking the fast is that apparent hardship befalls him that is difficult to bear. As for a minor illness by which no apparent hardship befalls him, breaking the fast is not permissible for him without disagreement according to us, contrary to the people of zahir.

Our companions said: Then the illness that permits breaking the fast, if one is able, he may abandon the intention at night. And if one has fever that comes and goes, and during the time of fever one is unable to fast, and when there is no fever one is able, if one is feverish at the time of beginning the fast, he may abandon the intention. Otherwise, he must intend from the night. Then if the illness returns and he needs to break the fast, he breaks it. And Allah, Glorified and Exalted, knows best.(2)

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The illness that permits breaking the fast is the severe one that increases by fasting, or delay in recovery is feared. It was said to Ahmad: When does the sick person break his fast? He said: When he is unable. It was said: Like fever? He said: And what illness is more severe than fever?

And it was reported from some of the predecessors that they permitted breaking the fast with every illness, even from the pain of a finger and a tooth, due to the generality of the verse regarding it. And because the traveler is permitted to break his fast even if he does not need it, so likewise the sick person.

Our view: He is present for the month, and fasting does not harm him, so it is required upon him like the healthy person. And the verse is specified to the traveler and the sick person together, as evidence by that the traveler is not permitted to break his fast in short travel. And the difference between the traveler and the sick person is that in travel, the apparent cause is considered, which is long travel, where it is not possible to consider the wisdom itself. For little hardship does not permit, and much hardship has no limit in itself, so the apparent cause is considered, which is long travel. So the ruling follows the apparent cause in existence and absence. And illness has no limit, for illnesses differ. Some of them harm their possessor by fasting, and some of them have no effect of fasting in them, like the pain of a tooth, a wound in a finger, a boil, a minor ulcer, and scabies and the like. So illness is not suitable as a limit, and it is possible to consider the wisdom, which is what harm is feared from, so it must be considered.

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: If the sick person bears it and fasts despite this, he has done a disliked act, due to what it includes of harming himself, and abandoning the easing of Allah, the Exalted, and accepting His concession. And his fast is valid and suffices for him, because it is an obligation that he abandoned as a concession. So if he bears it, it suffices for him, like the sick person who is permitted to abandon Friday prayer if he attends it, and the one who is permitted to abandon standing if he stands in it.(3)

(1) "Sharh Sahih Muslim" (8/21).

(2) "Al-Majmu'" (7/423), "Hashiyat Qalyubi" (2/81), and "Rawdat al-Talibin" (2/369).

(3) "Al-Mughni" (4/211), "Al-Furu'" (3/20), "Al-Insaf" (3/286), and "Kashaf al-Qina'" (2/310).
