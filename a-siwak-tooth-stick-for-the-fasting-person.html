Siwak (Tooth Stick) for the Fasting Person

There is no disagreement among the jurists of the four schools regarding the permissibility of siwak for the fasting person before noon. They only differed regarding its ruling after noon, on two views:

First View: That Siwak Is Permissible Absolutely

In the beginning of the day and at its end. This is the view of Abu Hanifah, Malik, and Ahmad in one narration chosen by Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim, and it is the view of Al-Nawawi from the Shafi'is.(1)

They used as evidence the following:

1. The hadith of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"Were it not that I would make it difficult for my ummah or for the people, I would have commanded them to use siwak with every prayer."(2)

The aspect of evidence from the hadith: That it includes in its generality every prayer for the fasting person and the one who breaks his fast.(3)

2. The hadith of 'Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, who said:

"The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, when he entered his house, would begin with siwak."(4)

The aspect of evidence from the hadith: That it is general for any time he entered, whether he was fasting or not fasting, before noon or after noon, and in every case.(5)

3. The statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Siwak is a purifier for the mouth and pleasing to the Lord."(6)

The aspect of evidence from the hadith: That in it is encouragement to use siwak without restriction to a specific time, and the time after noon is included in it.

4. The hadith of 'Amir ibn Rabi'ah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said:

"I saw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, countless times using siwak while fasting."(7)

5. What 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Ghanm narrated. He said:

"I asked Mu'adh ibn Jabal, may Allah be pleased with him: Do I use siwak while fasting? He said: Yes. I said: At what time of the day do I use siwak? He said: At any time of the day you wish. If you wish in the morning, and if you wish in the evening. I said: But the people dislike it in the evening. He said: Why? I said: They say: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'The breath of the fasting person is more pleasant to Allah than the fragrance of musk.' So he said: Glory be to Allah, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, commanded them to use siwak when he commanded them, and he knew that there must be bad breath in the mouth of the fasting person, even if he uses siwak. And he was not one who would command them to intentionally make their mouths foul. There is no good in that, rather there is harm in it, except for one who is afflicted with an affliction from which he finds no alternative."(8)

Second View: That Siwak Is Disliked for the Fasting Person After Noon

This is the view of Al-Shafi'i and Ahmad in the well-known view from him.(9)

They used as evidence the following:

1. The hadith of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"By the One in Whose hand is my soul, the breath of the fasting person is more pleasant to Allah, the Exalted, than the fragrance of musk."(10)

The aspect of evidence from the hadith: That the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, has clarified that the breath of the fasting person is more pleasant to Allah than the fragrance of musk, and siwak cuts that, so it must be disliked.(11)

2. The hadith of Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"When you fast, use siwak in the morning and do not use siwak in the evening, for there is no fasting person whose lips dry in the evening except that they will be light between his eyes on the Day of Resurrection."(12)

And because it is an effect of worship that is witnessed to as pleasant, so its removal is disliked, like the blood of the martyr.(13)

And just as the blood of the martyrs is witnessed to as pleasant, and washing the martyr is left for it, even though washing the dead is obligatory, so if the obligatory is left to preserve the remaining of the blood that is witnessed to as pleasant, then leaving siwak, which is not obligatory, to preserve the remaining of the bad breath that is witnessed to as such, is more appropriate.(14)

They answered regarding the evidence of the proponents of the first view: That they are general, specified, and what is intended by them is other than the fasting person at the end of the day.(15)

The proponents of the first view answered regarding the evidence of the proponents of the second view as follows:

The claim that siwak cuts the breath of the fasting person: Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, refuted it from six aspects:

First aspect: That rinsing the mouth is more effective than siwak in cutting the bad breath of the mouth, and there is consensus on its being legislated for the fasting person.

Second aspect: That the pleasure of Allah is greater than His finding pleasant the bad breath of the fasting person.

Third aspect: That His love for siwak is greater than His love for the bad breath of the fasting person.

Fourth aspect: That siwak does not prevent the pleasantness of the bad breath that siwak removes, at Allah on the Day of Resurrection.

Fifth aspect: That the bad breath does not disappear with siwak, because its cause is present, which is the emptiness of the stomach from food.

Sixth aspect: That the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught his ummah what is recommended and what is disliked for them in fasting, and he did not make siwak from the disliked.(16)

The hadith of Khabbab, may Allah be pleased with him, is a weak hadith that is not used as evidence, as was mentioned in the authentication.

Their statement: That it is an effect of worship that is witnessed to as pleasant, so its removal is disliked, like the blood of the martyr.

It was answered: That the effect of worship that is appropriate for it is concealment, contrary to the martyr, for the purpose of the Lawgiver from the remaining of the blood of the martyr is so that it may witness for him against his opponent on the Day of Resurrection. And also, the blood of the martyr has come the text with not removing it, where he is raised on what he was killed on, the color is the color of blood and the smell is the smell of musk, contrary to removing the smell of the mouth, for there is no text on not removing its effect.(17)
