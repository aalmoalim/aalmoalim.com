That He Pass Beyond the City and What Is Connected to It, Buildings, Courtyards, and Tents

Ibn Qudamah said: Breaking the fast is not permitted for him until he leaves the houses behind his back, meaning that he passes beyond them and exits from between their buildings.

Al-Hasan said: He may break his fast in his house if he wishes on the day he intends to depart, and the like was narrated from 'Ata'.

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr said: Al-Hasan's statement is a deviant statement, and breaking the fast is not for anyone while resident in view or report. And the opposite of it was narrated from al-Hasan.

Muhammad ibn Ka'b narrated: "I came to Anas ibn Malik in Ramadan and he intended to travel, and his mount had been prepared for him and he had put on his travel clothes, then he called for food and ate. So I said to him: Is it Sunnah? He said: It is Sunnah, then he mounted."(1) Al-Tirmidhi said: This is a good hadith.

Our view: The statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"So whoever among you witnesses the month, let him fast it"
[Al-Baqarah 2:185]

And this one is present, and he is not described as being a traveler until he exits from the city. And as long as he is in the city, he has the rulings of the residents. Therefore, he does not shorten prayer. And as for Anas, it may be that he had already gone out from the city, exiting from it, so Muhammad ibn Ka'b came to him in that residence.(2)

Al-Tirmidhi, may Allah have mercy on him, said after mentioning this hadith: Some of the people of knowledge have gone with this hadith and said: The traveler may break his fast in his house before departing, and he does not have the right to shorten prayer until he exits from the wall of the city or the village. This is the view of Ishaq ibn Ibrahim al-Hanzali.(3)

Imam al-Baghawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Al-Hasan said: If a resident wakes up with the intention of travel on his day, it is permissible for him to break his fast in his house. And this is the view of Ishaq. And it is narrated from Anas ibn Malik that he intended to travel, and his mount had been prepared for him, and he had put on his travel clothes, then he called for food and ate. So it was said to him: Is it Sunnah? He said: It is Sunnah, then he mounted. And the majority of the people of knowledge are of the view that if dawn appears before he departs, he must fast that day.

And they have unanimously agreed that he is not permitted to shorten prayer as long as he has not exited from the city.(4)

Imam Ibn al-Mundhir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Chapter on the time in which the traveler may break his fast when departing:

They differed regarding the time in which the one departing to travel may break his fast. A group said: He breaks his fast from his day if he departs as a traveler. This is the view of 'Amr ibn Shurahbil and al-Sha'bi.

Ahmad said: He breaks his fast if he goes out beyond the houses.

Ishaq said: No, rather when he places his feet in the saddle.

Al-Hasan al-Basri said: He may break his fast if he wishes in his house on the day he intends to depart.

Abu Bakr said: Ahmad's statement is correct, because they say: Whoever wakes up fasting and healthy, then becomes ill, breaks his fast for the rest of his day. And likewise, if one wakes up while resident, then departs to travel, he may likewise break his fast.

A group said: He does not break his fast on that day. This is the view of al-Zuhri, Makhul, Yahya al-Ansari, Malik, al-Awza'i, al-Shafi'i, Abu Thawr, and the people of opinion.(5)

They differed regarding if one eats in this case, eating before leaving his city: Is expiation required upon him or not?

Malik said: No. And Ashhab said: He is interpreting. And others said: He expiates.

Ibn Juzayy said: If one breaks his fast before departing, there are three views regarding the requirement of expiation upon him. The third distinguishes between if he travels so it is dropped, or not, so it is required.(6)

(1) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Al-Tirmidhi (799).

(2) "Al-Mughni" (4/142).

(3) "Sunan Al-Tirmidhi" (3/163).

(4) "Sharh al-Sunnah" (6/313).

(5) "Al-Ishraf" (3/144). See: "Tafsir al-Qurtubi" (2/278).

(6) "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (82).
