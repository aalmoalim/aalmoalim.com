If One Intends to Break the Fast During the Day

The jurists differed regarding if a fasting person intends to break the fast during the day: does he break his fast by that or not?

The Hanafis, Shafi'is in the madhab, and Ibn Hamid from the Hanbalis held that he does not break his fast by that, just as if one intended to speak in his prayer and did not speak.

Al-Bayjuri, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Rejecting the intention at night harms, and it does not harm during the day.(1)

The Malikis, Hanbalis in the madhab, and some Shafi'is held that he breaks his fast by that, because intention is a condition in all of it. So if he cuts it off during it, the remainder remains without intention, so it becomes invalid. And if part becomes invalid, the whole becomes invalid, because one part does not separate from another.(2)

It came in "Al-Mudawwanah al-Kubra": Regarding one who woke up in Ramadan intending to break the fast but did not eat until the sun set.

(I said): If a man woke up and his intention was to break the fast in Ramadan, but he did not eat or drink until the sun disappeared or most of the day passed, is making up and expiation obligatory upon him? He said: Yes. (I said): And this is the view of Malik? He said: Yes. (I said): And if he woke up intending to break the fast in Ramadan, then intended to fast before the sun rose? (Ibn al-Qasim said): Making up and expiation are obligatory upon him. (I said): What do you think if one intended to break the fast in Ramadan for his entire day except that he did not eat or drink? (He said): Malik said something about that, I do not know. He said expiation and making up, or making up and no expiation is upon him. And what I prefer is that expiation be in it with making up. (I said): What do you think if a man woke up intending to break the fast in Ramadan intentionally, except that he did not eat or drink, then it occurred to him to return to fasting after he had intended to break the fast? (He said): It reached me from Malik that he said: Making up and expiation are obligatory upon him. He said: But I did not hear it from him. (Ibn al-Qasim said): And making up and expiation are obligatory upon him.(3)

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: This is if one determines with the intention of leaving immediately. So if one hesitated in leaving it, or made leaving conditional upon the entry of Zayd, for example, then the madhab view - and the majority have affirmed it - is that it does not become invalid, one view.(4)

The Hanbalis in "the madhab" said: Whoever intends to break the fast, his fast becomes invalid. And likewise, if one hesitated in breaking the fast, his fast becomes invalid. Or if one intended "if I find food I will eat, otherwise if I do not find food I will complete," his fast becomes invalid, due to his hesitation in the intention, just as prayer becomes invalid by breaking the intention, since maintaining its ruling until the completion of every act of worship is a condition.

And the fast of the day in which one intended to break the fast becomes valid as voluntary fasting, not Ramadan. This was explicitly stated due to the validity of the intention of voluntary fasting during the day, even after noon. And likewise, if one intended to break the fast in voluntary fasting then intended to fast, it is valid, due to the validity of the intention of voluntary fasting from the day, even after noon.(5)

(1) "Al-Bahr al-Ra'iq" (2/139), "Al-Durr al-Mukhtar" (2/123), "Maraqi al-Falah" (361), "Hashiyat al-Bayjuri" (1/300), "Al-Majmu'" (7/492), "Al-Mughni" (4/174), "Al-Insaf" (3/297), "Al-Mubdi'" (2/154), and "Al-Ifsah" (1/388).

(2) "Al-Majmu'" (7/491), "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (80), and the rest of the previous sources.

(3) "Al-Mudawwanah al-Kubra" (1/220).

(4) "Al-Majmu'" (7/492).

(5) "Al-Mughni" (4/174), "Al-Insaf" (3/297), "Al-Mubdi'" (2/154), and "Matlab Awwal al-Nuha" (2/188).
