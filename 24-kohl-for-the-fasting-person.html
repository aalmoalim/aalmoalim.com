Kohl for the Fasting Person

The jurists differed regarding if the fasting person uses kohl that reaches his throat, either due to its moisture, like al-ashyaf (1), or due to its sharpness: like "scented powder" (2).

Abu Hanifah and al-Shafi'i said: He does not break his fast.

Due to what 'A'ishah, may Allah be pleased with her, narrated: She said:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used kohl while he was fasting."(3)

And due to what Abu Dawud narrated that Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him: "Used to use kohl while he was fasting."(4)

And because the eye is not a passage, so the fast does not become invalid by what reaches through it. And it is the same whether one finds its taste in his throat or not, because what is found in his throat is its effect entering from the pores, and what breaks the fast is what enters from the passages, like the entrance and exit, not from the pores, which are the gaps of the body, due to agreement that one who immerses in water finds its cold in his stomach and does not break his fast.

Imam Malik and Ahmad said: If one finds its taste in his throat or knows it reached it, he breaks his fast, otherwise he does not, because he delivered to his throat what is prohibited from consuming, so he broke his fast, just as if he delivered it from his nose.(5)

(1) Al-ashyaf: They are medications for the eye and the like. "Tartib al-Qamus" (2/776).

(2) Al-dharur: It is what is sprinkled in the eye from dry medication. It is said: "I sprinkled it" when one treats it with it. "Al-Nihayah fi Gharib al-Hadith" (2/157).

(3) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Ibn Majah (1678).

(4) Hasan hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2378).

(5) "Mukhtasar al-Quduri" (62), "Sharh Fath al-Qadir" (2/346), "Al-Mudawwanah" (1/177), "Al-Majmu'" (7/577), "Al-Mughni" (4/157, 158), and "Al-Ifsah" (1/400).
