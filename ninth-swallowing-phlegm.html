Ninth: Swallowing Phlegm

Phlegm (al-nukhamah) is: al-nukha'ah, and it is what a person brings out from his throat, from the exit of the letter kha' with a dot.

Al-Fayyumi said: Ibn al-Athir restricted it thus, and al-Mutarrizi said thus, and added: What comes out from the nasal cavity when clearing the throat.(1)

The view of the Hanafis and the relied-upon view of the Malikis is that phlegm, whether it is mucus descending from the head, or phlegm ascending from the interior, by coughing or clearing the throat - as long as the phlegm is not excessive - does not break the fast absolutely.

In the explicit statements of the Malikis: That phlegm does not break the fast absolutely, even if it reaches the tip of the tongue, due to its difficulty, contrary to Khalil, who saw invalidation if it is possible to expel it, by it passing the throat, then returning it and swallowing it, and that making up is required upon him.

In one narration from Ahmad, that swallowing phlegm does not break the fast, because it is habitual in the mouth, not coming from outside, so it resembles saliva.(2)

According to the Shafi'is, this detail:

If one extracts phlegm from the interior and expels it, there is no harm in that on the more correct view, because the need for it is something that recurs. And in one view: One breaks one's fast by it, like vomiting.

If it ascends by itself, or by his coughing, and he expels it, he does not break his fast decisively. And if he swallows it after it reaches the exterior of the mouth, he breaks his fast decisively.(3)

If it occurs in the exterior of the mouth, it is required to cut its path to the throat and expel it. If one leaves it with the ability to do that, and it reaches the stomach, one breaks one's fast on the more correct view, due to one's negligence. And in one view: One does not break one's fast, because one did not do anything. Rather, one refrained from action. And if one swallows it after it reaches the exterior of the mouth, one breaks one's fast decisively.

The Hanbalis explicitly stated that it is forbidden for the fasting person to swallow phlegm if it occurs in his mouth, and one breaks one's fast by it if one swallows it, whether it was in one's stomach or in one's chest, after it reaches one's mouth, because it is from other than the mouth, so it resembles vomit. And because it is possible to guard against it, so it resembles blood.(4)

Due to this difference, Ibn al-Shihnah alerted that one should expel phlegm, so that one's fast is not invalidated according to the view of Imam al-Shafi'i, and so that one's fast is valid by agreement, due to one's ability to expel it.(5)

(1) "Al-Misbah al-Munir" under the root (nukhm).

(2) "Hashiyat al-Qalyubi" (2/55), "Al-Durr al-Mukhtar" (2/101, 111), "Jawahir al-Iklil" (1/149), "Al-Sharh al-Kabir" by al-Dardir (1/525), and "Al-Mughni" (4/160).

(3) "Al-Majmu'" (7/524), "Rawdat al-Talibin" (2/360), and "Hashiyat al-Qalyubi" (2/55).

(4) "Kashaf al-Qina'" (2/329), "Al-Rawd al-Murbi'" (1/143), "Al-Mughni" (4/160), and "Al-Insaf" (2/325, 326).

(5) "Maraqi al-Falah" (362).
