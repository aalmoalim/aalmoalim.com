Determination

The majority of jurists - the Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis in the school - have explicitly stated that determination is required in the intention for fasting, to cut off hesitation. So even if one intended on the night of doubt to fast the next day, if it is from Ramadan then it is obligatory, otherwise it is voluntary, his fast is not valid and does not suffice him if it appears that it is from Ramadan, due to the lack of determination in his intention. And he does not become a fasting person due to the lack of determination, so it becomes like if one intended that if he finds food tomorrow he will break his fast, and if he does not find it he will fast.

If he said that on the thirtieth night of Ramadan, his fast is valid if it appears to be from it, because it is built upon a foundation whose removal has not been established, and his hesitation does not invalidate it, because it is the ruling of his fast with determination, unlike if he said it on the thirtieth night of Sha'ban, because there is no foundation with him to build upon, rather the foundation is the continuation of Sha'ban.(1)

Imam Ahmad, in a narration chosen by Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah, held that whoever intended "if tomorrow is Ramadan then it is obligatory, otherwise it is voluntary" is valid and suffices him for Ramadan.(2)

This is also the view of the Hanafis. Al-Marghinani, may Allah have mercy on him, said: If one intended for Ramadan if tomorrow is from it, and for voluntary fasting if it is from Sha'ban, it is disliked, because he is intending for the obligation from one aspect. Then if it appears that it is from Ramadan, it suffices him for it. And if it appears that it is from Sha'ban, it is valid for his voluntary fast, because it is fulfilled by the foundation of the intention. And if he invalidates it, it is obligatory that he not make it up, due to the entry of cancellation in his resolve from one aspect.(3)

(1) "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (p. 80), "Sharh Mukhtasar Khalil" (2/238), "Ashal al-Madarik" (1/417), "Bulghat al-Salik" (1/444), "Rawdat al-Talibin" (2/353), "Al-Majmu'" (7/490) and what follows, "Sharh al-Mahalli 'ala al-Minhaj" (2/53, 54), "Asna al-Matalib" (1/413), "Al-Mughni" (4/130, 131), "Al-Insaf" (3/295, 296), "Al-Qawa'id al-Fiqhiyyah" (1/133), "Kashshaf al-Qina'" (2/315), and "Matlab Awwal al-Nuha" (2/186).

(2) "Al-Majmu'" (7/490) and related sources.

(3) "Al-Hidayah" (1/120) and "Al-'Inayah Sharh al-Hidayah" (3/266).
