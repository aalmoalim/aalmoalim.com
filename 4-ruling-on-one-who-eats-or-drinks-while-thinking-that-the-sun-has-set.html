Eating or Drinking Due to Mistaken Timing

The four imams have agreed that whoever eats or drinks while thinking that the sun has set or that dawn has not appeared, then the matter appears contrary to that after his breaking the fast, making up is obligatory upon him, because he ate voluntarily while remembering the fast, so he broke his fast, just as if one ate on the day of doubt. And because he was ignorant of the time of fasting, so he was not excused by it, like one ignorant of the beginning of Ramadan. And because it is possible to avoid it, so it resembles the intentional eating.

And due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"Until the white thread becomes distinct to you from the black thread of dawn, then complete the fast until night"
[Al-Baqarah 2:187]

And this one ate during the day. And due to what Al-Bukhari narrated from Hisham ibn 'Urwah from Fatimah bint al-Mundhir from Asma' bint Abi Bakr al-Siddiq, may Allah be pleased with her, who said:

"We broke our fast at the time of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, on a cloudy day, then the sun appeared." It was said to Hisham: "Were they commanded to make it up?" He said: "Making up is necessary."(1)

And due to what Malik narrated in "Al-Muwatta'" from Zayd ibn Aslam from his brother Khalid that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him:

"Broke his fast one day in Ramadan on a cloudy day, and he saw that it was evening and the sun had set. Then a man came to him and said: O Commander of the Believers, the sun has appeared. So 'Umar said: 'The matter is easy, and we have made an effort.'"(2)

Al-Bayhaqi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Malik and al-Shafi'i said: The meaning of "The matter is easy" is making up a day in its place.(3)

Abu 'Umar Ibn 'Abd al-Barr, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The evidence for the correctness of whoever said "he makes up the day" is their consensus that if the new moon of Ramadan was obscured and they broke their fast, then proof was established by seeing the new moon that making up is obligatory upon them after completing their fasting of their day.(4)

Ibn Battal, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And Ma'mar said: I heard Hisham say: "I do not know, did they make it up or not?"

The majority of scholars say making up is required in this issue, and that has been narrated from 'Umar ibn al-Khattab from the narration of the people of Hijaz and the people of Iraq. As for the narration of the people of Hijaz, Ibn Jurayj narrated from Zayd ibn Aslam from his father who said: "The people broke their fast in the month of Ramadan on a cloudy day, then the sun appeared. So 'Umar said: 'The matter is easy, and we have made an effort. We will make up a day.'" This is how Ibn Jurayj said from Zayd ibn Aslam from his father, and it is connected.

And Malik's narration in "Al-Muwatta'" from Zayd ibn Aslam from his brother that 'Umar... and it is mursal, because Khalid ibn Aslam, the brother of Zayd, did not meet 'Umar. As for the narration of the people of Iraq, al-Thawri narrated from Jabalah ibn Suhaym ibn Hanzalah from his father that he witnessed 'Umar, and he mentioned the story and said: "O people, whoever broke the fast, making up a day is easy." And it came in another narration from 'Umar that he said: "There is no making up upon him."

Ma'mar narrated from al-A'mash from Zayd ibn Aslam who said: "The people broke their fast in the time of 'Umar, then the sun appeared, and that was difficult for the people. They said: 'Shall we make up this day?' So 'Umar said: 'Why should we make it up? By Allah, we did not commit sin.'"

The first narration is more correct. And making up has been narrated from Ibn 'Abbas and Mu'awiyah, and it is the view of 'Ata', Mujahid, al-Zuhri, Malik, al-Thawri, Abu Hanifah, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, and Abu Thawr. And al-Hasan al-Basri said: There is no making up upon him, like the forgetful person. This is the view of Ishaq and the people of zahir.

The evidence of those who made making up obligatory is the consensus of the scholars that if the new moon of Ramadan was obscured and they broke their fast, then proof was established by seeing the new moon that making up is obligatory upon them after completing their fasting of their day. And al-Muhallab said: And from their evidence is also the statement of Allah, the Exalted: "Then complete the fast until night" [Al-Baqarah 2:187]. And whoever breaks the fast then the sun appears, he did not complete the fast until night as Allah, Glorified and Exalted, commanded him, so making up is obligatory upon him from other days, by the text of the Book of Allah, Glorified and Exalted.

Ibn al-Qassar said: What was narrated from 'Umar that he said: "We will not make it up, and by Allah, we did not commit sin" may mean that he abandoned making up if he did not know, and breaking the fast occurred on doubt. And the narration from him with the affirmation of making up is if breaking the fast occurred during the day without doubt.(5)

Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The people differed: is making up obligatory in this case? The majority said: It is obligatory. And Ishaq ibn Rahawayh and the people of zahir held that there is no making up upon them, and their ruling is the ruling of one who eats forgetfully. And that was reported from al-Hasan and Mujahid, and it was disagreed upon regarding 'Umar. Zayd ibn Wahb narrated: I was sitting in the mosque of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, in Ramadan in the time of 'Umar, then we were brought a cup containing drink from the house of Hafsah, and we drank while thinking that it was night, then the clouds cleared, and the sun was appearing. He said: "So the people began to say: 'Let us make up a day in its place.' 'Umar heard that and said: 'By Allah, we will not make it up, and we did not commit sin.'" Al-Bayhaqi and others narrated it.

And Malik narrated in "Al-Muwatta'" from Zayd ibn Aslam that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab broke his fast one day in Ramadan on a cloudy day and saw that it was evening, and the sun had set. Then a man came to him and said to him: O Commander of the Believers, the sun has appeared. So 'Umar said: "The matter is easy, and we have made an effort." Malik said: He means by his statement "The matter is easy": making up, in what we see, and Allah, Glorified and Exalted, knows best.

And likewise al-Shafi'i said: And this does not contradict the previous report.

His statement "and we have made an effort" indicates the absence of making up, and his statement "The matter is easy" is only making light of what they did, and making the matter easy.

However, al-Athram and al-Bayhaqi narrated from 'Umar, and in it: "Whoever broke the fast, let him fast a day in its place." Al-Bayhaqi gave precedence to this narration over the narration of Zayd ibn Wahb and considered it an error, and said: The narrations have agreed on making up. He said: And Ya'qub ibn Sufyan al-Farisi criticized Zayd ibn Wahb for this narration that contradicts the previous narrations. He said: And Zayd is trustworthy, except that error from him is not safe.

In what he said there is consideration: The narration did not agree from 'Umar on making up. Rather, it came from the narration of 'Ali ibn Hanzalah from his father, and his father was a companion of 'Umar, so he mentioned the story and said in it: "Whoever broke the fast, let him fast a day in its place."

Al-Bayhaqi gave precedence to this narration over the narration of Zayd ibn Wahb and considered it an error, and said: The narrations have agreed on making up. He said: And Ya'qub ibn Sufyan al-Farisi criticized Zayd ibn Wahb for this narration that contradicts the previous narrations. He said: And Zayd is trustworthy, except that error from him is not safe. In what he said there is consideration: The narration did not agree from 'Umar on making up. Rather, it came from the narration of 'Ali ibn Hanzalah from his father, and his father was a companion of 'Umar, so he mentioned the story and said in it: "Whoever broke the fast, let him fast a day in its place." And I did not see the command for making up explicitly except in this narration. As for Malik's narration, it does not mention making up or its absence. So the narration of Hanzalah and the narration of Zayd ibn Wahb contradict each other, and the narration of Zayd ibn Wahb is superior to it by the amount of excellence between Hanzalah and him. And al-Bayhaqi narrated with a chain that has consideration from Suhayb that he commanded his companions to make up in a story that occurred to them like this. So if the reports from 'Umar were considered contradictory, analogy would require the dropping of making up, because ignorance of the continuation of the day is like forgetting the fast itself. And if one ate forgetfully of his fast, making up would not be obligatory upon him. And the Shari'ah did not distinguish between the ignorant and the forgetful, for each of them has done what he believes is permissible, and erred in his action. And they are equal in most rulings and in the lifting of sins, so what makes the distinction between them in this place obligatory? And the companions of al-Shafi'i and others made the ignorant person who errs more excused than the forgetful person in multiple places. And it may be said: In the case of fasting, he is more excused than him, for he is commanded to hasten iftar as a recommendation, so he hastened to perform what he was commanded and the Lawgiver recommended for him. So how does his fast become invalid? And the invalidation of the forgetful person's fast is more appropriate than him, because his action is not permitted for him in it. Rather, at most it is pardoned, so he is less than the erring ignorant person in excuse.

In summary, they were not distinguished in Hajj, nor in the invalidators of prayer, like carrying impurity and other than that. And what was said of the distinction between them, that the forgetful person is not legally responsible, and the ignorant person is legally responsible - if what is intended by it is legal responsibility for making up, it is not correct, because this is what is disputed. And if what is intended by it is that the action of the forgetful person does not stand as a cause for sin, and the legal address does not include it, so likewise the action of the erring person. And if what is intended by it is that the erring person is remembering his fast and proceeding to break it, so his action is included under legal responsibility, unlike the forgetful person - it is also not correct, because he believes the exit of the time of fasting and that he is commanded to break the fast, so he is proceeding to do what he believes is permissible. And his error in the continuation of the day is like the forgetfulness of the eater in the day, so the two actions are equal. So how does legal responsibility attach to one of them and not the other?

The best thing by which the two issues are distinguished is that the erring person was able to complete his fast by delaying iftar until he is certain of sunset, unlike the forgetful person, for the action is not attributed to him, and he was not able to avoid it. And this, even if it is a distinction in appearance, it is not effective in the obligation of making up, just as it was not effective in sin by agreement. And if it were attributed to negligence, sin would attach to it. So when they agreed that sin is lifted from him, it indicates that his action is not attributed in it to negligence, especially since he is commanded to hasten to break the fast. And the cause that called him to break the fast is not attributed to him in both cases - it is forgetfulness in the issue of the forgetful person, and the appearance of darkness and the obscuring of the day in the case of the erring person. So this one, Allah, Glorified and Exalted, fed him and gave him drink by forgetfulness, and this one, Allah fed him and gave him drink by the obscuring of the day. And for this reason Suhayb said: "It is Allah's feeding." But this is more appropriate, for it is Allah's feeding by permission and permissibility, and the feeding of the forgetful person is his feeding by pardon and lifting of hardship. So this is what the evidence requires.(6)

(4) "Al-Istidhkar" (3/344).

(5) "Sharh Sahih al-Bukhari" (4/105, 106).

(6) "Hashiyat Ibn al-Qayyim 'ala Sunan Abi Dawud" (6/346, 348).
