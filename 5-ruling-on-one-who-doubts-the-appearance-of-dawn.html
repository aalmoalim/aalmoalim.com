Ruling on One Who Doubts the Appearance of Dawn and Eats, Drinks, or Has Intercourse

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis held that whoever eats, drinks, or has intercourse while doubting the appearance of dawn and the matter is not clear, there is no making up upon him. He may eat, drink, and have intercourse until he is certain of the appearance of dawn, due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And eat and drink until the white thread becomes distinct to you from the black thread of dawn, then complete the fast until night"
[Al-Baqarah 2:187]

It extended eating to the limit of clarity, and one may be in doubt before clarity. So if making up were required upon him, eating would be forbidden for him.

And because it is authentically reported from Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, that he said:

"Eat as long as you are in doubt until it becomes clear to you."

And in another wording:

"Eat as long as you doubt until you do not doubt."(1)

And because the principle is the continuation of night. The time of doubt is from it, as long as one does not know with certainty of its departure, unlike the setting of the sun. That is, if one ate while doubting the setting of the sun and it did not become clear, making up is required upon him, because the principle is the continuation of day.

Imam Malik held that whoever doubts the appearance of dawn and eats, making up is required upon him, because the principle is the continuation of fasting in his obligation, so it does not fall away by doubt. And because he ate while doubting whether it is day or night, so making up is required upon him, just as if he ate while doubting the setting of the sun.(2)

(1) Narrated by 'Abd al-Razzaq (4/172), Ibn Abi Shaybah (2/287, 288), and Al-Bayhaqi (4/221). Al-Nawawi said in "Al-Majmu'" (7/504): The chain is authentic.

(2) "Al-Istidhkar" (3/343), "Al-Majmu'" (7/506, 507), "Al-Mughni" (4/198), and "Mukhtasar Ikhtilaf al-'Ulama'" (2/14).
