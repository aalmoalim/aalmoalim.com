Nasal Drops

Nasal drops (al-isti'at): A form derived from "sa'ut" (nasal medication), like "rasul" (messenger): a medication that is applied in the nose.(1)

And nasal drops (al-isti'at) and (al-is'aat) according to the jurists: delivering something to the brain from the nose.(2)

The jurists differed regarding if one uses nasal drops with oil or water or other than them, and it reaches his brain:

Abu Hanifah, al-Shafi'i, and Ahmad said: He breaks his fast by that, even if it does not reach his throat, due to the hadith of Laqit ibn Sabrah, in which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"Exaggerate in sniffing, except if you are fasting."(3)

So it indicates that if something reaches the brain, his fast becomes invalid. And because the brain is one of the two cavities, so the fast becomes invalid by what reaches it, like the stomach. And that it contains the meaning of breaking the fast, for what reaches the brain contains its benefit and nourishment, so it breaks the fast.

Imam Malik, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Whenever it reaches his brain, and it does not reach his throat, it does not break his fast, because as long as it does not reach the throat, it does not require breaking the fast.(4)

(1) "Al-Misbah al-Munir" under the root (sa'at), and "Radd al-Muhtar 'ala al-Durr al-Mukhtar" (2/102).

(2) "Hashiyat al-Qalyubi 'ala Sharh al-Minhaj" (2/56).

(3) Sahih hadith: Previously mentioned.

(4) "Radd al-Muhtar" (2/102), "Sharh Fath al-Qadir" (2/355), "Al-Ishraf" (204), "Al-Majmu'" (7/515, 516), "Al-Mughni" (4/156), "Al-Mubdi'" (3/22), "Mataleb Uli al-Nuha" (2/191), "Al-Ifsah" (1/398), and "Mawahib al-Jalil" (2/425).
