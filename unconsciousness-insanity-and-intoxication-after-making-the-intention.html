Unconsciousness, Insanity, and Intoxication After Making the Intention

The jurists differed regarding if one intended to fast from the night, then unconsciousness, insanity, or intoxication occurred upon him before the appearance of dawn:

If one regains consciousness in any part of the day and had intended from the night then became unconscious for the remainder, the majority of jurists held that his fast is valid.

Al-Wazir Ibn Hubayrah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: They have agreed that whoever is found to have regained consciousness in part of the day, then became unconscious for the remainder, his fast is valid.(1)

However, the Malikis said: The unconscious person is either unconscious before dawn or after it. If he became unconscious before dawn and regained consciousness after it by a long time, it does not suffice him, without disagreement. And if he regained consciousness after it by a short time, it does not suffice him according to the well-known view.

If he became unconscious after dawn, if he regained consciousness after it by a short period, it suffices him. And if he regained consciousness after noon or at it, it does not suffice him.

The intoxicated person with something lawful is like the unconscious person in the mentioned detail. And whoever became intoxicated with something unlawful at night and continued in his intoxication, making up is obligatory upon him, all the more so due to his causing it, and it is not permissible for him to use what breaks the fast for the remainder of his day. And the sleeper who intends at the beginning of the month then sleeps the entire month, his fast is valid and his liability is cleared, and the intoxicated person with something lawful is not like him.

The ruling of the insane person is the ruling of the unconscious person.(2)

However, they differed regarding if one became unconscious and did not regain consciousness except after sunset: is his fast valid or not?

The Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis held that his fast is not valid, because fasting is abstention with intention, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Allah, the Exalted, said: 'Every deed of the son of Adam is for him except fasting, for it is for Me and I will reward for it. He only abandons his food and drink for My sake.'"(3)

So He attributed abandoning food and drink to him. If one is unconscious, abstention is not attributed to him, so it does not suffice him. And because intention is one of the two pillars of fasting, it does not suffice alone, just as abstention alone does not suffice.

The Hanafis held the validity of his fast, because his intention has become valid, and the removal of awareness after that does not prevent fasting, like sleep.

They differed regarding if one regained consciousness during the day and had not intended from the night: is his fast valid or not?

The Hanafis held that if one regained consciousness before noon and intended to fast, it suffices him, because fasting Ramadan is valid with an intention before noon. And if one regained consciousness after noon, his fast is not valid.(4)

The Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis held that his fast is not valid.(5)

If the Insane Person Regains Consciousness After the Month Has Passed

The jurists differed regarding if the insane person regained consciousness after the month has passed: is making up obligatory upon him or not?

The Malikis, Shafi'is in the old view, and Ahmad in a narration held that making up what passed is obligatory upon him, even if years passed upon him, because insanity does not prevent the obligation of fasting, rather it prevents its performance. So if he regains consciousness, making it up is obligatory upon him. And due to the generality of the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"So whoever among you witnesses the month, let him fast it"
[Al-Baqarah 2:187]

And this one witnessed the month while insane, so making up a number of other days is obligatory upon him. And because insanity does not contradict the obligation of fasting, because it is a meaning that removes reason during life, so it does not prevent the obligation of fasting, like the state of unconsciousness, sleep, and intoxication.

The Hanafis, Shafi'is in the madhab, and Hanbalis in the madhab held that making up what passed is not obligatory upon him, because it is a fast that was missed in a state in which legal obligation fell due to deficiency, so making it up is not obligatory, just as if it was missed in the state of childhood and disbelief.

The Hanafis said: This is regarding if insanity was all-encompassing, such that one became insane before the entry of the month of Ramadan and regained consciousness after its passing, so there is no making up upon him.(6)

If the Insane Person Regains Consciousness During the Month

They also differed regarding if the insane person regained consciousness during the month.

The Hanafis, Malikis, and Ahmad in a narration held that fasting what remains is obligatory upon him and he makes up what passed, due to the existence of the cause and legal capacity in the liability. The intended meaning by "cause" is witnessing part of the month, and that is the interpretation of the verse: "So whoever among you witnesses part of the month, let him fast the entire month," because the pronoun refers to what is mentioned, not what is implied. And the insane person whose insanity did not encompass the entire month has witnessed part of the month, so he fasts all of it.

And because insanity does not contradict fasting, as evidenced by the fact that if one becomes insane during fasting, it does not become invalid. So if it is found in part of the month, making up is obligatory, like unconsciousness.

The Shafi'is and Hanbalis in the madhab held that only fasting what he regained consciousness in is obligatory upon him, and making up what he missed in the state of insanity is not obligatory upon him, because it is a fast that was missed in a state in which legal obligation falls due to deficiency, so making it up is not obligatory, just as if it was missed in the state of childhood and disbelief.

This disagreement is regarding the insane person. As for the unconscious person, making up is obligatory upon him without disagreement among the jurists of the four madhabs.

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Making up is obligatory upon the unconscious person without disagreement that we know of, because its duration is usually not prolonged and guardianship is not established over its possessor, so legal obligation is not removed by it. And making up acts of worship is like sleep.(7)
