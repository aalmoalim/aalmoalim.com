Kissing for the Fasting Person

The four imams have agreed that kissing is disliked for one who does not feel secure from it that it will arouse his desire.(1)

The basis for that is what 'Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, narrated. She said:

"The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would kiss and have intimate contact while fasting, and he was the most in control of you of his desire."(2)

Then they differed regarding one who does not fear that: Is it disliked for him or not?

Imam Abu Hanifah, Al-Shafi'i, and Ahmad in one narration held that it is not disliked for him, due to the previously mentioned hadith of 'Aishah, and due to what Muslim narrated from 'Amr ibn Abi Salamah: That he asked the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: Does the fasting person kiss? So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him: "Ask this one, Umm Salamah." So she informed him that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, did that. So he said: O Messenger of Allah, Allah has forgiven you what has passed of your sin and what will come. So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him: "By Allah, I indeed am the most God-fearing of you of Allah and the most fearful of you of Him."(3)

From 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I became excited one day, so I kissed while fasting. So I came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: I did a great thing today. I kissed while fasting. So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "What do you think if you rinsed your mouth with water while fasting?" I said: There is no harm in that. So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Then why?"(4) And other than that from the evidence.

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Kissing is disliked for one whose desire is aroused while fasting, and it is not disliked for other than him. However, it is better to leave it. And there is no difference between the old man and the young man in that. The consideration is the arousal of desire and fear of ejaculation. If it arouses the desire of a young man or a strong old man, it is disliked. And if it does not arouse it for an old man or a weak young man, it is not disliked. And it is better to leave it. And it is the same whether one kisses the cheek or the mouth or other than them. And likewise intimate contact with the hand and embracing have the ruling of kissing. Then the dislike for one whose desire is aroused is a dislike of prohibition according to the author and his shaykh, Qadi Abu al-Tayyib, al-'Abidari, and others.

And others said: A dislike of recommendation, as long as one does not ejaculate, and al-Mutawalli authenticated it.

Al-Rafi'i and others said: The more correct is dislike of prohibition. And if one kisses and does not ejaculate, one's fast is not invalidated without disagreement according to us, whether we say dislike of prohibition or we say dislike of recommendation.(5)

Imam Malik and Ahmad in one narration held that it is disliked for him, due to the hadith of 'Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, who said:

"The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would kiss and have intimate contact while fasting, and he was the most in control of you of his desire."(6)

And because kissing and intimate contact are from the causes of intercourse, so one does not feel secure that it will arouse one's desire, so it calls to increase and excess, and that leads to the invalidation of fasting. And because it is an act of worship in which intercourse is forbidden, so it is permissible that prohibition is attached to kissing, like Hajj.(7)

(1) "Al-Ifsah" (1/410).

(2) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1826) and Muslim (1106).

(3) Narrated by Muslim (1108).

(4) Sahih hadith: Previously mentioned.

(5) "Al-Majmu'" (7/593, 594).

(6) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1826) and Muslim (1106).

(7) "Al-Dhakhirah" (1/504), "Al-Ishraf" (205), "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/447), "Al-Bada'i'" (2/664), "Sharh Fath al-Qadir" (2/331), "Al-Mughni" (4/168), "Al-Insaf" (3/328), and "Al-Ifsah" (1/410).
