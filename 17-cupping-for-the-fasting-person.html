Cupping for the Fasting Person

The jurists differed regarding the fasting person if he has cupping done: does he break his fast by that or not?

The majority of jurists (the Hanafis, Malikis, and Shafi'is) held that cupping does not break the fast of the fasting person, due to what Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, narrated: "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had cupping done while he was fasting."(1)

And due to the hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, who said:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, gave permission for cupping for the fasting person."(2)

And from Thabit that he said to Anas ibn Malik: "Did you dislike cupping for the fasting person at the time of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him?" He said: "No, except due to weakness."(3)

And because it is blood coming out from the body, so it resembles bloodletting.

They said: And the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "The cupper and the one cupped break their fast"(4) is interpreted as metaphorical, meaning that their matter will lead to breaking the fast. And what supports this is what Abu Dawud narrated from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla from a man from the companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: "Forbade cupping and continuous fasting, and did not prohibit them, preserving his companions..."(5)

So it indicates that he only disliked that for one who would be weakened by it. And the hadith of Thabit mentioned previously confirms it, that he said to Anas: "Did you dislike cupping for the fasting person at the time of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him?" He said: "No, except due to weakness."(6)

The Hanbalis held that cupping breaks the fast of the cupper and the one cupped, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "The cupper and the one cupped break their fast."(7)

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Breaking the fast by cupping is in accordance with the principles and analogy, from the category of breaking the fast by menstrual blood, intentional vomiting, and masturbation. And if it is so, then by whatever means one intends to extract blood, he breaks his fast, just as by whatever means one extracts vomit, he breaks his fast, whether he pulls the vomit by inserting his hand, or by smelling what makes him vomit, or by placing his hand under his stomach and extracting the vomit. So those are ways to extract vomit, and these are ways to extract blood. And for this reason, the exit of blood by this and that is equal in (the chapter of purity). So it becomes clear by this the completeness of the Shari'ah, its balance, and its proportionality, and that what came from the texts and their meanings, some of them confirms and agrees with others:

"And if it were from other than Allah, they would have found therein much contradiction"
[Al-Nisa' 4:82]

As for the cupper, he draws air into the cup by sucking it, and the air draws what is in it, so perhaps something from the blood rises with the air and enters his throat without him realizing. And wisdom, if it is hidden or widespread, the ruling is attached to the apparent cause, just as the sleeper from whom wind exits and he does not know is commanded to perform ablution. So likewise the cupper, something from the blood enters with his saliva into his stomach without him knowing. And blood is from the greatest invalidators, for it is forbidden in itself due to what is in it of the excess of desire and departure from balance. And the fasting person is commanded to cut off its source, so blood increases blood, so it is from the category of the prohibited, so the cupper breaks his fast for this, just as the ablution of the sleeper is invalidated even if he is not certain of the exit of wind from him, because it exits and he does not know. And likewise the cupper, blood may enter his throat without him knowing. And as for the one who makes the incision, he is not a cupper, and this meaning is absent in him, so the one who makes the incision does not break his fast. And likewise, if a cupper is imagined who does not suck the cup but sucks something else, or takes the blood by another way, he does not break his fast.

And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, his speech came regarding the known, customary cupper. And if the wording is general, even if his intent was a specific person, all of the type share in the ruling due to the legal custom that what is established for one of the Ummah is established for all. So this is more comprehensive. So it is not established by its wording what appears in wording and meaning that it did not enter, with its distance from the Shari'ah, reason, and Allah, Glorified and Exalted, knows best.(8)

Ibn Rushd, may Allah have mercy on him, said: As for cupping, there are three schools in it. A group said: It breaks the fast, and abstaining from it is obligatory. This is the view of Ahmad, Dawud, al-Awza'i, and Ishaq ibn Rahawayh. A group said: It is disliked for the fasting person and does not break the fast. This is the view of Malik, al-Shafi'i, and al-Thawri. A group said: It is neither disliked nor does it break the fast. This is the view of Abu Hanifah and his companions.

The reason for their difference is the contradiction of the reports that came regarding that. That is, two hadiths came regarding that. One of them is what was narrated from the way of Thawban and from the way of Rafi' ibn Khadij that he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "The cupper and the one cupped break their fast."

And this hadith of Thawban was considered authentic by Ahmad.

And the second hadith is the hadith of 'Ikrimah from Ibn 'Abbas that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: "Had cupping done while he was fasting."

And this hadith of Ibn 'Abbas is authentic. So the scholars went in these two hadiths with three schools:

One of them: The school of preference.

The second: The school of reconciliation.

The third: The school of dropping when there is contradiction and returning to the original permissibility if one does not know the abrogating from the abrogated.

Whoever went with the school of preference said by the hadith of Thawban, and that is because this requires a ruling, and the hadith of Ibn 'Abbas lifts it. And the one that requires is preferred according to many scholars over the one that lifts, because a ruling, if it is established by a way that requires action, does not lift except by a way that requires action by lifting it. And the hadith of Thawban has required action by it, and the hadith of Ibn 'Abbas may be abrogating and may be abrogated, and that is doubt, and doubt does not require action nor does it lift knowledge that requires action. And this is according to the way of whoever does not see doubt as effective in knowledge.

Whoever sought to reconcile between them, the hadith of prohibition was interpreted as dislike, and the hadith of cupping was interpreted as lifting the prohibition.

Whoever dropped them due to contradiction said by the permissibility of cupping for the fasting person.(9)
