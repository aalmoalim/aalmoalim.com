Food Between the Teeth

Ibn al-Mundhir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The scholars have unanimously agreed that there is nothing upon the fasting person regarding what he swallows that flows with the saliva from what is between his teeth that he is unable to remove. He said:

If he is able to remove it but swallows it intentionally, Abu Hanifah said: He does not break his fast. And the rest of the scholars said: He breaks his fast. And this is what I say.(1)

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: If food remains in the gaps of his teeth, he should clean it at night and purify his mouth. If he wakes up fasting and there is something in the gaps of his teeth and he swallows it intentionally, he breaks his fast without disagreement according to us. This is the view of Malik, Abu Yusuf, and Ahmad.

Abu Hanifah said: He does not break his fast.

Our evidence: He swallowed what he could avoid, and his need does not call to it, so his fast becomes invalid, just as if he took it out to his hand then swallowed it.

If he swallows something very small, like a sesame seed or mustard seed or the like, he breaks his fast without disagreement according to us. This is the view of the majority of scholars. Al-Mutawalli said: He breaks his fast according to us, and he does not break his fast according to Abu Hanifah, as he said regarding what remains in the gaps of the teeth.(2)

Al-Sarakhsi the Hanafi, may Allah have mercy on him, said in "Al-Mabsut": If there is something in his teeth and he swallows it, it does not harm him, because what remains between the teeth is in the ruling of being subordinate to his saliva, so for this reason fasting does not become invalid.

This is if it is less than a chickpea, for that usually remains between the teeth.(3)

(1) "Al-Majmu'" (7/522, 529) and "Al-Ijma'" (33).

(2) "Al-Majmu'" (7/522) and "Al-Mughni" (4/167).

(3) "Al-Mabsut" (1/195).
