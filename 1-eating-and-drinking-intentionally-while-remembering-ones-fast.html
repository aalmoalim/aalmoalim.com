Eating and Drinking Intentionally

The people of knowledge have unanimously agreed that whoever intentionally eats and drinks while he is healthy and resident on a day from the month of Ramadan, it is forbidden for him, and making up is obligatory upon him, due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And eat and drink until the white thread becomes distinct to you from the black thread of dawn, then complete the fast until night"
[Al-Baqarah 2:187]

He extended eating and drinking until the appearance of dawn, then commanded fasting from them.

And due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"By the One in Whose hand is my soul, the breath of the fasting person is more pleasant to Allah, the Exalted, than the fragrance of musk. He abandons his food, drink, and desire for My sake."(1)

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: One breaks the fast by eating and drinking by consensus, and by the indication of the Book and the Sunnah.

The scholars have unanimously agreed on breaking the fast by eating and drinking what is nourishing. As for what is not nourishing, the majority of the people of knowledge hold that breaking the fast occurs by it, due to the indication of the Book and the Sunnah on the prohibition of eating and drinking in general.(2)

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The Ummah has unanimously agreed on the prohibition of food and drink upon the fasting person, and it is the purpose of fasting. Its evidence is the noble verse and consensus. Among those who transmitted consensus on it is Ibn al-Mundhir.(3)

He also said: If the fasting person swallows what is not eaten normally, such as a dirham, dinar, dust, pebble, grass, fire, iron, thread, or other than that, he breaks his fast without disagreement according to us. This is the view of Abu Hanifah, Malik, Ahmad, Dawud, and the majority of scholars from the predecessors and successors.

Our companions reported from Abu Talhah al-Ansari, the Companion, may Allah be pleased with him, and al-Hasan ibn Salih and some companions of Malik that he does not break his fast by that.

They reported from Abu Talhah that he used to consume ice while fasting and swallow it, saying: It is not food or drink. Our companions argued with what the author mentioned and what al-Bayhaqi narrated with a good or sound chain from Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them, who said: "Ablution is only from what comes out, not from what enters. And breaking the fast is only from what enters, not from what comes out."(4)

(1) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1795).

(2) "Al-Mughni" (4/154) and "Al-Ifsah" (1/395).

(3) "Al-Majmu'" (7/515).

(4) "Al-Majmu'" (7/522).
