Intention in Voluntary Fasting

The majority of jurists - the Hanafis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis - held that all voluntary fasting is valid with an intention from the day before noon, and it is not required from the night, on the condition that one has not done what breaks the fast before the intention, due to the hadith of 'A'ishah, may Allah be pleased with her, who said:

"The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, entered upon me one day and said: 'Do you have anything?' We said: 'No.' He said: 'Then I am fasting.'"(1)

And because voluntary fasting is lighter than the obligation, and the evidence for it is that it is permissible to abandon standing in voluntary [prayer] with ability, and it is not permissible in the obligation, so likewise with fasting.(2) However, they differed regarding if one intends after noon.

The Hanbalis in the school and the Shafi'is in a view held the permissibility of intention in voluntary fasting before noon and after it, due to the previously mentioned hadith of 'A'ishah, and the hadith of fasting 'Ashura', and that it is the view of Mu'adh, Hudhayfah, and Ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with them, and no one is known to have disagreed with them. And because he intended in a part of the day, so it resembles if he intended at its beginning. And because all of the night is a time for the intention of the obligation, so likewise all of the day is a time for the intention of voluntary fasting.(3)

Based on this, is he judged to have fasted from the beginning of the day, so he is rewarded from the appearance of dawn, or is he judged [to have fasted] only from the time of intention, and the reward of what is before it is not counted for him?

The majority of Shafi'is and Abu al-Khattab from the Hanbalis held that he is judged to have done that from the beginning of the day and is rewarded from the appearance of dawn, because fasting is not divided in the day, as evidenced by the fact that if one eats in part of it, fasting the remainder does not suffice him. So if it is found in part of the day, it indicates that he is fasting from its beginning. And because if one catches part of the rak'ah or part of the congregation, he is considered to have caught all of it.

The Hanbalis in the school and Abu Ishaq al-Marwazi from the Shafi'is held that he is judged to have the legal fast that is rewarded from the time of intention, because what is before the intention he did not intend to fast, so he is not fasting in it, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "Actions are only by intentions." And because fasting is a pure act of worship that does not exist without intention, like all other pure acts of worship.(4)

The Hanafis and the Shafi'is in the more correct view and some Hanbalis held that intention for voluntary fasting after noon is not permissible, because the intention did not accompany most of the act of worship, so it resembles if one intended with sunset. And it differs from the first half, because the intention there accompanied most of the act of worship, and most of something may stand in place of the whole. For this reason, if one catches most of the rak'ah with the imam, he is made to have caught the rak'ah, and if one catches less than most, he is not made to have caught it.(5)

The Malikis, al-Muzani, and Abu Yahya al-Balkhi from the Shafi'is held that in voluntary fasting, an intention made at night is required, due to the generality of the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "Whoever does not make the intention to fast from the night has no fast."(6) And because fasting is an act of worship, and intention is a condition for its validity, so its voluntary and obligatory must be equal, like prayer. And because intention after dawn is like intention after noon.(7)

(1) Narrated by Muslim (1154).

(2) "Al-Hidayah wa Shuruhuha" (2/241), "Al-Bada'i'" (2/607), "Al-Majmu'" (7/485, 486, 499), "Al-Mughni" (4/132), and "Al-Ifsah" (1/385).

(3) "Al-Majmu'" (7/485) and "Al-Mughni" (4/134).

(4) "Al-Mughni" (4/134, 135), "Al-Majmu'" (7/485, 486), and "Al-Ikhtiyarat al-Fiqhiyyah" (1/160).

(5) "Al-Bada'i'" (2/606) and the two previous sources.

(6) Sahih hadith: Previously cited.

(7) "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/450), "Bidayat al-Mujtahid" (1/404), "Jawahir al-Iklil" (1/148), "Sharh al-Khirshi" (2/246), "Al-Ifsah" (1/385), "Al-Majmu'" (7/486), and "Al-Mughni" (4/132).
