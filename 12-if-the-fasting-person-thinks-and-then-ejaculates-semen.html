Ejaculation from Thought or Sight

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis held that whoever thinks and then ejaculates, his fast is valid.

Due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Allah has pardoned for my Ummah what they speak to themselves about, as long as they do not act or speak."(1)

And because there is no text regarding breaking the fast by it, nor consensus, and it is not possible to compare it to direct contact or repeated looking, because it is less than them in arousing desire and leading to ejaculation. And it differs from them in prohibition if it is related to a non-mahram woman, or dislike if it is with a wife, so it remains on dislike.

Imam al-Nawawi reported from the author of "Al-Hawi" (al-Mawardi) his statement: As for if one thinks with his heart without looking and takes pleasure and ejaculates, there is no making up upon him and no expiation by consensus. He said: And if one repeats looking and ejaculates, he sins, even if making up is not required.(2)

The Malikis, Abu Hafs al-Barmaki, and it is the choice of Ibn 'Aqil from the Hanbalis held that whoever thinks and ejaculates, his fast is invalidated and making up is required upon him, because thought is brought to mind, so it enters under choice, as evidence by the sinfulness of its possessor in persisting in it in innovation or disbelief. And Allah, Glorified and Exalted, praised those who reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, forbade reflecting on the essence of Allah, and commanded reflecting on His blessings. And if it were not within one's ability, that would not be related to it, like wet dreams.(3)

From Looking

The summary of the Maliki school is that if one ejaculates by mere thought or looking without persisting in them, there is definitely no expiation, and making up is required upon him, except if that is overwhelming and difficult for him, then there is also no making up due to hardship. And if one persists in them until ejaculating, if his habit is to ejaculate by them when persisting, then expiation is definitely required. And if his habit is not to ejaculate by them when persisting, and he went against his habit and ejaculated, there are two views, and the more apparent is that expiation is dropped.(4)

(1) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (4968). And it was narrated with the wording: "My Ummah has been pardoned for error and forgetfulness and what they speak to themselves about, as long as they do not act or speak."

(2) "Al-Majmu'" (7/532). See: "Sharh Fath al-Qadir" (2/329, 330), "Al-Mughni" (4/169), "Al-Insaf" (3/307), and "Sharh Muntaha al-Iradat" (2/194).

(3) "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/457), "Al-Mughni" (4/169), "Al-Insaf" (3/307), and "Sharh Muntaha al-Iradat" (2/194).

(4) "Al-Taj wa al-Iklil" (4/40), "Sharh Mukhtasar Khalil" (2/253), "Al-Sharh al-Kabir ma'a Hashiyat al-Dusuqi" (2/159), "Tahbir al-Mukhtasar" (1/657), "Sharh al-Zarqani" (2/369), and "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/457).
