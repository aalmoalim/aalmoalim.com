Intercourse When Dawn Appears

The jurists of the four schools have agreed that if dawn appears upon a person while he is having intercourse and he continues in intercourse with knowledge of the appearance of dawn, his fast becomes invalid and making up is obligatory upon him by agreement.

However, the jurists differed in two places:

First Place: If Dawn Appears Upon Him While He Is Having Intercourse and He Withdraws Immediately

The Hanafis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis in one view, which is the choice of Ibn Taymiyyah and al-Mardawi, said: His fast is valid, due to what Al-Bayhaqi narrated from Nafi' from Ibn 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both:

"If the call to prayer was made, and the man was with his wife, that did not prevent him from fasting. If he wanted to fast, he would get up, perform ritual bath, and complete his fast."(1)

And because withdrawal is abandoning intercourse, and what is attached to doing something is not attached to abandoning it, just as if one swore not to wear this garment while it is on him, and he began to remove it, he does not break his oath. Or if one swore not to enter a house while he is in it, and he left it, so likewise here.

The Malikis said: His fast becomes invalid and only making up is required upon him, because it is the occurrence of intercourse in a portion of the day, so it resembles if he continues.

In one view of the Hanbalis: Making up and expiation are required if he withdraws immediately with the first appearance of dawn. This is the view of Ibn Hamid and the judge, and Ibn 'Aqil supported it, because withdrawal is intercourse that one takes pleasure in, so what is attached to continuation is attached to it, like penetration.(2)

Second Place: If He Continues in Intercourse, Is Expiation Required Upon Him or Not?

The majority of jurists from the Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis held that expiation is required upon him, because he abandoned the fast of Ramadan by intercourse, and he sinned by it due to the sanctity of fasting, so expiation is required by it, just as if he had intercourse after the appearance of dawn.

Al-Qadi 'Abd al-Wahhab, may Allah have mercy on him, said: If dawn appears while he is penetrating... if he continues, our companions said: Expiation is required upon him.

And this is derived according to me from the view of Ibn al-Qasim: When one eats forgetfully, then eats after it intentionally, if he was interpreting, there is no expiation upon him. And if he was on the basis of boldness and intending violation, expiation is required upon him.(3)

The Hanafis held that if he continues in intercourse and does not withdraw immediately, making up is required upon him without expiation, because his intercourse did not coincide with a valid fast, so it did not require expiation, because expiation is only required by invalidating the fast, and invalidating the fast occurs after its existence. And his remaining in intercourse prevents the existence of the fast, so if its existence is prevented, invalidation becomes impossible, so expiation is not required. And the obligation of making up is due to the absence of his fast today, not due to invalidating it after its existence.(4)

The Woman Who Is Had Intercourse With During the Day of Ramadan

The jurists have agreed that the woman who is had intercourse with during the day of Ramadan while consenting has invalidated her fast, and making up is required upon her.

However, they differed regarding the obligation of expiation upon her:

Abu Hanifah, Malik, al-Shafi'i in one view, and Ahmad in the more apparent of the two narrations from him said: Expiation is also required upon her, like the man, because the cause of expiation is the transgression of invalidation, not intercourse itself, and she shared in it. And it cannot be borne on her behalf, because it is an act of worship or a punishment, and bearing it on behalf of another does not suffice. And because she violated the fast of Ramadan by intercourse, so expiation is required upon her, like the man.

And because intercourse is a cause that requires expiation, and it must be that they share in what is required by it, if they share in it, like killing. And because all rulings related to intercourse regarding the one who has intercourse are ruled the same regarding the one who is had intercourse with, from the obligation of ritual bath, breaking the fast, hadd, being muhsan, and making up, so likewise the existence of expiation.

Imam al-Shafi'i in his school and Imam Ahmad in one narration held that there is no expiation upon her, because the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, commanded the one who had intercourse in Ramadan to free a slave, and he did not command anything regarding the woman, despite his knowledge of its occurrence from her. And because it is a right of property that is related to intercourse from among its kind, so it is upon the man, like the dowry.

Imam al-Shafi'i, may Allah have mercy on him, said in "Al-Umm": And if he expiates, it suffices for him and for his wife. And likewise in Hajj and 'Umrah. And this is how the Sunnah proceeded. Do you not see that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not say: "The woman expiates," and that he did not say in the report regarding the one who had intercourse in Hajj: "The woman expiates"?

If someone says: Then why is hadd required upon her for intercourse, and expiation is not required upon her?

It is said: Hadd does not resemble expiation. Do you not see that hadd differs for the free person and the slave, and the previously married woman and the virgin?

So our school and what we claim is that when the reports distinguish between things, they should be distinguished as they distinguished.(5)
