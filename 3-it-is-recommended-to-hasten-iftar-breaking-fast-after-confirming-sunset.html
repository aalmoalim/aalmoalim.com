Hastening Iftar After Sunset

In the two Sahihs from Sahl ibn Sa'd, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"The people will continue to be in good as long as they hasten the iftar."(1)

Muslim narrated from Abu 'Atiyyah who said: Masruq and I entered upon 'A'ishah and we said: O Mother of the Believers, two men from the companions of Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, one of them hastens the iftar and hastens the prayer, and the other delays the iftar and delays the prayer. She said: "Which one hastens the iftar and hastens the prayer?" We said: 'Abdullah, meaning Ibn Mas'ud. She said: "That is how the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to do." Abu Kurayb added: And the other was Abu Musa.(2)

Al-Nasa'i narrated it from Abu 'Atiyyah with the wording: He said: I said to 'A'ishah: Among us are two men from the companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, one of them hastens the iftar and delays suhur, and the other delays the iftar and hastens suhur. She said: "Which one hastens the iftar and delays suhur?" I said: 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud. She said: "This is how the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to do."(3)

And because in hastening it there is opposition to the disbelievers. Abu Dawud narrated from Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said:

"The religion will continue to be manifest as long as the people hasten the iftar, because the Jews and Christians delay it."

Ibn Majah narrated it with the wording:

"The people will continue to be in good as long as they hasten the iftar. Hasten the iftar, for indeed the Jews delay it."(4)

And because the place of fasting is the day, so there is no meaning in delaying iftar.

(1) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1957) and Muslim (1098).

(2) Narrated by Muslim (1099).

(3) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Al-Nasa'i (2158, 2159).

(4) Hasan sahih: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2353) and Ibn Majah (1698).
