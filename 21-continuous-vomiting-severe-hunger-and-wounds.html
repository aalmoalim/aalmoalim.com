Continuous Vomiting, Severe Hunger, and Wounds

Al-amah (wound in the head): An injury in the head.

Al-ja'ihah (wound in the abdomen): An injury in the abdomen.

The meaning of this - as al-Kasani, may Allah have mercy on him, says: What reaches the cavity from other than the natural orifices.(1)

If the fasting person treats the wound in the head, the wound in the abdomen, or a wound, the jurists differed regarding it:

The majority of jurists from the Hanafis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis held that if he treats it and it reaches the inside of his brain, his fast is invalid and making up is required upon him, because fasting is abstaining from everything that reaches the cavity, and this is what he abstained from. And because it reaches his cavity by his choice, so it resembles if he ate. And because if the fast becomes invalid by what reaches the cavity that is not eating, like nasal drops and enema, so likewise if medicine enters by way of treating the wound in the abdomen or the wound in the head.

Imam al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: If one treats his wound and the medicine reaches his cavity or brain, he breaks his fast according to us, whether the medicine was wet or dry.(2)

As for if one doubts the reaching of the medicine to the cavity, according to the Hanafis there is some detail and difference: If the medicine was wet, according to Abu Hanifah, the apparent is reaching, due to the existence of a passage to the cavity, and it is the cause, so the ruling is based on the apparent, which is reaching usually.

And the two companions (Abu Yusuf and Muhammad) said: He does not break his fast due to the absence of knowledge of it, so he does not break his fast by doubt. So they consider the natural orifices, because reaching the cavity from the natural orifices is certain, and from other than them is doubtful, so we do not rule invalidation with doubt.

As for if the medicine was dry, there is no breaking the fast by agreement, because it did not reach the cavity nor the brain.

However, al-Babarti, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Most of our shaykhs are of the view that consideration is by reaching, so even if one knows that the dry medicine reached his cavity, his fast does not become invalid according to him. However, he mentioned wet and dry based on custom, and if one does not know with certainty, it becomes invalid according to Abu Hanifah, considering custom, not according to the two companions.(3)

The Malikis and Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah, may Allah have mercy on him, held that there is no breaking the fast by treating wounds, because it is not a passage to the cavity, so the occurrence of something in it does not require breaking the fast, like the inside of the mouth and nose.

Al-Dardir said, explaining the absence of breaking the fast by placing oil on the wound in the abdomen and the wound in the abdomen that reaches the cavity: It does not reach the place of food and drink, otherwise one would die immediately.(4)

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The more apparent is that one does not break his fast by any of that, for fasting is from the religion of the Muslims that the common and the elite need to know. So if these matters were from what Allah, Glorified and Exalted, and His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, prohibited in fasting, and the fast becomes invalid by them, this would be something that the Messenger must clarify. And if he mentioned that, the companions would know it and convey it to the Ummah, just as they conveyed the rest of his Shari'ah. So when no one from the people of knowledge transmitted from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, regarding that an authentic hadith, nor weak, nor connected, nor disconnected, it is known that nothing of that was mentioned.

And those who said: These matters break the fast, like enema and treating the wound in the head and the wound in the abdomen, did not have evidence from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Rather, they mentioned that by what they saw from analogy. And the strongest of what they used as evidence is his statement, peace and blessings be upon him: "Exaggerate in sniffing, except if you are fasting." They said: So that indicates that what reaches the brain breaks the fast of the fasting person if it was by his action. And by analogy, everything that reaches his cavity by his action, from enema and other than it, whether that was in the place of food and nourishment or other than it from filling his cavity.

And if their basis is these analogies and the like, it is not permissible to invalidate the fast by such analogies for reasons - then he mentioned them, may Allah have mercy on him.(5)

(1) "Al-Bada'i'" (2/627).

(2) "Al-Majmu'" (7/516).

(3) "Sharh al-Hidayah ma'a al-'Inayah" by al-Babarti with "Fath al-Qadir" (2/266, 267).

(4) "Al-Sharh al-Kabir" (1/533), "Al-Mudawwanah" (1/198), "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (80), "Al-Insaf" (3/299), "Maraqi al-Falah" (368), "Fath al-Qadir" (2/267), "Kashaf al-Qina'" (318), "Al-Mughni" (4/156), "Al-Ifsah" (1/392), "Al-Ishraf" (204), and "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/463).

(5) "Majmu' al-Fatawa" (25/234).
