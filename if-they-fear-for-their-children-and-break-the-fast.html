If They Fear for Their Children and Break the Fast

The jurists differed regarding if the pregnant woman or the nursing woman breaks her fast due to fear for their two children: Is ransom required upon them or not?

The Shafi'is in their school and the Hanbalis held that making up and ransom are required upon them, which is feeding a poor person for each day, due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And upon those who are able to bear it, a ransom of feeding a poor person"
[Al-Baqarah 2:184]

And they are included in the generality of the verse.

And due to what Al-Bayhaqi narrated from Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said:

"Permission was given to the old man and the old woman in that, and they are able to fast, to break their fast if they wish and feed a poor person each day, and there is no making up upon them. Then that was abrogated in this verse: 'So whoever among you witnesses the month, let him fast it.' And it remained for the old man and the old woman if they are unable to fast, and the pregnant woman and the nursing woman if they fear, they break their fast and feed a poor person each day."(1)

The Hanafis said: There is no ransom upon them, because it is breaking the fast due to an excuse. For the pregnant woman and the nursing woman are commanded to preserve the child, and that is intended, and it is not achieved without breaking the fast. And when there is fear, they are commanded to break the fast. And the command to break the fast with expiation, which is built on requirement for breaking the fast, do not combine.

And due to what Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said:

"Allah has lifted from the traveler fasting and half of prayer, and from the pregnant woman and the nursing woman fasting - or fasting - and by Allah, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said both of them, one of them or both."(2)

And he did not command expiation.

The Malikis held that the pregnant woman has no ransom upon her, because she breaks her fast due to an excuse, like the menstruating woman. And because expiation by breaking the fast is only required on the basis of violation. So if there is no violation, it is not required, considering the pregnant woman and the sick person.

As for the nursing woman, according to them there are two views:

One of them: Ransom is required upon her, because the excuse is not present in her. Rather, it is for the sake of other than her, so her matter is weaker than the pregnant woman and the sick person.

The second: There is no ransom upon her, by analogy to the sick person, due to the commonality of permissibility, and due to what was mentioned previously regarding the pregnant woman.(3)

(1) Narrated by Al-Bayhaqi (4/230), Al-Tabari (2752, 2753), and Ibn al-Jarud in "Al-Muntaqa" (381). Al-Albani authenticated it in "Al-Irwa'" (912) and said: Abu Dawud narrated it (2318) from Ibn 'Abbas, except that he abbreviated it with a harmful abbreviation. Its wording: "It was a concession for the old man and the old woman, and they are able to fast, to break their fast and feed in place of each day a poor person, and the pregnant woman and the nursing woman if they fear." Abu Dawud said: He means for their children. "They break their fast and feed." So the narration gives permission to the old man and the woman to break their fast, and they are able to fast. And the reality is that this is abrogated.

(2) Sahih hadith: Previously mentioned.

(3) "Bada'i' al-Sana'i'" (2/636, 637), "Sharh Fath al-Qadir" (2/355, 356), "Al-Ikhtiyar" (1/135), "Mukhtasar al-Quduri" (63), "Al-Mabsut" (3/99, 100), "Mukhtasar al-Tahawi" (54), "Al-Mudawwanah" (1/186), "Al-Ishraf" (204), "Al-Sharh al-Saghir" (1/466), "Al-Dhakhirah" (2/515), "Bidayat al-Mujtahid" (1/412), "Al-Umm" (2/103, 104), "Al-Majmu'" (7/442, 445), "Al-Mughni" (4/202), "Al-Insaf" (3/290), and "Al-Ifsah" (1/398, 399).
