Making the Intention at Night

The jurists have agreed that what is established in the liability from fasting, such as making up Ramadan, making up vows, and expiations, is not valid to fast except with an intention from the night.(1)

They differed regarding fasting Ramadan and specified vows: is it valid to be with an intention from the day, or is it required that the intention be at night?

The majority of jurists - the Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis - held that making the intention at night in fasting Ramadan and other obligatory fasts is a condition for the validity of the fast, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in the hadith of Hafsah:

"Whoever does not resolve to fast before dawn has no fast."

In another wording:

"Whoever does not make the intention to fast from the night has no fast."(2)

And because it is an obligatory fast that requires intention from the night, like making up and vows. And because intention is a condition in the legal fast, it is required that the fast not suffice whenever part of the day passes without it. And because it is an act of worship that is performed and made up, it is required that one complete the time of intention in performance and making up, like prayer.

Making the intention at night (tabyit) is: making the intention occur in the night, from sunset to the appearance of dawn. So intention before sunset does not suffice according to all, nor after dawn, because intention is resolve, and resolve for what has passed is impossible rationally.

If it coincides with sunset or dawn, or there is doubt, it is not valid, as is the case with making the intention at night.

In a view of the Malikis, it is valid if it coincides with dawn, as in the takbir of ihram, because the foundation in intention is coincidence with what is intended.(3)

It does not suffice after dawn, and it suffices with the appearance of dawn if that coincides, and this is the view of 'Abd al-Wahhab, and al-Lakhmi and Ibn Rushd from the Malikis affirmed it. Even though Ibn 'Abd al-Hakam narrated that it does not suffice with dawn, the statements of al-Qarafi, Ibn Bashir, and Ibn al-Hajib indicate that coincidence with dawn is the foundation, but precedence over it was permitted due to the difficulty in coinciding with it.(4)

The correct view according to the Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis is: it is not required in making the intention at night that it be in the latter half of the night. Rather, it is valid in any part of the night, due to its generality in the hadith: "There is no fast for whoever does not make the intention to fast from the night" without detail. And because he intended from the night, so his fast is valid, as if he intended in the latter half.

And because specifying the intention to the latter half of the night would lead to missing the fast, because it is the time of sleep and many people do not wake up in it and do not remember the fast. The Lawgiver only permitted advancing the intention before its beginning due to the difficulty of considering it at that time, so He does not specify it to a place where the difficulty is not removed by specifying it to it. And because specifying it to the latter half is arbitrary without evidence.

The correct view is also: it does not harm if one does after the intention what contradicts fasting, such as eating, drinking, and intercourse, as long as it is in the night, because he has not engaged in the act of worship.

The correct view is also: it is not required to renew the intention if one sleeps after it then wakes up before dawn.(5)

The Hanafis held that making the intention at night is not required in Ramadan, nor in specified vows, nor in voluntary fasting. So if one does not intend at night until morning, the intention suffices him between it and noon - half the day - so he intends before noon so that the majority is intended, so it has the ruling of the whole. If he intends after that, it is not valid, due to the majority being empty of intention, giving preference to the majority.

However, it is better to fast with a specified intention made at night to avoid disagreement.(6)

The Hanafis argued for what they held regarding the non-requirement of making the intention at night and its validity even until noon by the following:

  1. What Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated: The people woke up on the day of doubt, then a Bedouin came and testified to seeing the new moon. So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Do you testify that there is no god but Allah and that I am the Messenger of Allah?" He said: Yes. So he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Allah is greater. It suffices the Muslims that one of them [testifies], so he fasted and commanded fasting, and commanded a caller to call out: 'Whoever has eaten, let him not eat for the rest of his day, and whoever has not eaten, let him fast.'" So he commanded fasting, and that entails the ability for legal fasting. If intention from the night were required, he would not have been able to do it, so it indicates the non-requirement of it.(7)
  2. From Salamah ibn al-Akwa', may Allah be pleased with him, who said: "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, commanded a man from Aslam to call out to the people: 'Whoever has eaten, let him fast the rest of his day, and whoever has not eaten, let him fast, for today is the day of 'Ashura'."(8)

The fast of 'Ashura' was obligatory, then it was abrogated by the obligation of Ramadan.(9)

(3) "Sharh Mukhtasar Khalil" (2/246), "Hashiyat al-Dusuqi" (1/520), and "Bulghat al-Salik" (1/449).

(4) "Hashiyat al-Dusuqi" (1/519, 520).

(6) "Al-Ikhtiyar" (1/136) and "Al-Bada'i'" (2/606).

(7) "Al-Ikhtiyar" (1/135).
