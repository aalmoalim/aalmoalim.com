Making Up Ramadan May Be Done at Leisure

The majority of jurists from the Malikis in the well-known view, the Shafi'is, and the Hanbalis in their school held that making up Ramadan may be done at leisure. However, they restricted that to if the time of making it up has not passed, by the coming of another Ramadan, due to the statement of 'Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her:

"Fasting would be required upon me from Ramadan, and I would not be able to make it up except in Sha'ban, and that was due to the place of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him."(1)

And it is not permissible according to them to delay making up Ramadan to another Ramadan without an excuse. Rather, making it up before the coming of Ramadan of the following year is required upon him, because 'Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, did not delay it to that. And if it were possible for her, she would have delayed it. And because fasting is a repeated act of worship, so delaying the first from the second is not permissible, like the obligatory prayers. If one delays it to another Ramadan without an excuse, one sins, and fasting the present Ramadan is required upon him, and making up the missed Ramadan is required upon him after that. And feeding a poor person for each day from the missed one is required upon him by the mere entry of the second Ramadan.

They used as evidence what Al-Daraqutni narrated in his Sunan(2) from Ibn 'Abbas and Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said regarding one who has fasting required upon him and did not fast it until another Ramadan reached him: "He feeds for the first." And Al-Daraqutni said: Its chain is Sahih. And the wording of the narration of Abu Hurayrah: "Whoever was ill in Ramadan, then recovered and did not fast until another Ramadan reached him, he said: He fasts the one that reached him and feeds for the first, for each day a mudd of wheat for each poor person. When he finishes this, he fasts the one he was negligent in."(3)

And it was narrated with this meaning from Ibn 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both. Al-Daraqutni also narrated it.(4)

And this narration from Ibn 'Abbas, Abu Hurayrah, and Ibn 'Umar did not come from other than them from the companions contradicting it. And because it is a timed act of worship. So if one delays it without an excuse until the time of another act of worship of its kind enters, one is negligent and disobedient, like prayer. And because it is an act of worship in which money enters into its compensation. So if one delays it with negligence until its time passes, expiation is required upon him, like Hajj.

As for if one has making up Ramadan or some of it required upon him, and one was excused in delaying the making up, by the continuation of one's illness or travel and the like, it is permissible for one to delay as long as one's excuse remains, even if years remain, and feeding is not required upon one by this delay, even if Ramadans are repeated. Rather, only making up is required upon one, because delaying the performance of Ramadan is permissible with this excuse, so delaying the making up is more appropriate in permissibility.(5)

Al-Hattab, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Note: The well-known view is that making up Ramadan is not required immediately.

And it was said in "Al-Dhakhirah": It is permissible to delay it to Sha'ban, and it is forbidden after it.

And it was said: Making up is required immediately.

Al-Rajraji and others reported the two views.

Ibn Bashir said: There is no disagreement that it is not required immediately.

Ibn al-Hajib followed him in reporting the agreement, and likewise Ibn Naji in the commentary of "Al-Mudawwanah".

Ibn 'Arafah summarized in it three views: The first: That it is immediately. The second: That it is at leisure, for the remaining of its amount before the following one, with the condition of safety. So if one recovered after Ramadan for the amount of time of making up, and did not make it up in it, then illness or travel befell one, and that continued to the following Ramadan, making up is required upon one.

The third: That it is at leisure until the amount of what is required upon one of days remains from Sha'ban absolutely. And Ibn al-Hajib was objected to in his reporting of the agreement.

And it was said in "Al-Ikmal" in the commentary of the statement of 'Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her: "Fasting would be required upon me from Ramadan, and I would not be able to make it up except in Sha'ban, due to being busy with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him." In it is evidence that making up Ramadan is not required immediately, contrary to al-Dawudi in requiring it from the second of Shawwal, and that one sins whenever one does not complete it. So if it is not immediately, its time is extended, restricted to the remainder of the year, as long as another Ramadan does not enter. However, hastening is recommended.(6)

Ibn Rushd, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The apparent of what is in the book of fasting from "Al-Mudawwanah" is that making up Ramadan is immediately, because he said regarding one who broke his fast in Ramadan in travel or illness, then arrived and stayed a month, or recovered a month, then died, and bequeathed that feeding be done for him, that is in his third, initially. And likewise on his school.

Ibn al-Hajib, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Making up Ramadan is not required immediately by agreement. If one delays it to another Ramadan without an excuse, then feeding is required by agreement.(7)

Al-Mardawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: His statement: And consecutiveness in making up Ramadan is recommended, and it is not required. This is the school, and the companions are upon it. And he explicitly stated it and mentioned it. And the judge mentioned it in "Al-Khilaf" in that zakat is required immediately if we say that making up Ramadan is immediately. And he used as evidence his explicit statement in expiation. And it will come in the following chapter: Is voluntary fasting before making up Ramadan valid for them or not?

The Hanafis, the Malikis in one view, al-Muzani from the Shafi'is, and the Hanbalis in one view held that making up Ramadan is at leisure absolutely without restriction. So if another Ramadan comes, and one did not make up the missed one, one gives precedence to the fast of performance over making up, so even if one intends fasting for making up, it does not occur except for performance. And there is no feeding required upon one by delaying to it, due to the absolute text, which is His statement, the Exalted: "a number of other days" without restriction. So the requirement of making up is at leisure, so nothing is required upon one by the delay other than that one is abandoning what is more appropriate of hastening. And because it is an obligatory fast, so expiation is not required in delaying it, just as if one delayed performance and vow. And no raised hadith was established that explicitly states the requirement of feeding.(8)

Imam Al-Kasani, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The discussion regarding the manner of the requirement of making up, that it is immediately or at leisure, is like the discussion regarding the manner of requirement in an absolute command without time originally, like the command regarding expiations and absolute vows and the like. And that is at leisure according to the generality of our shaykhs. And the meaning of at leisure according to them is that it is required in absolute time, not a specific time, and the choice of specification is to the one charged. So in any time in which that time is legislated, that time is specified for requirement. And if it is not legislated, requirement becomes restricted upon one at the end of one's life, in a time in which one is able to perform before one's death.

Al-Karkhi reported from our companions that it is immediately, and the correct is the first. And according to the generality of the companions of hadith, an absolute command requires requirement immediately, as is known in the principles of jurisprudence.

Al-Quduri reported from al-Karkhi that he used to say regarding making up Ramadan: It is timed to what is between two Ramadans. And this is not sound. Rather, the school according to our companions is that the requirement of making up is not timed, due to what we mentioned, that the command for making up is absolute from specifying some times without others, so it proceeds on its absoluteness. And for this our companions said: It is not disliked for one who has making up Ramadan required upon him to do voluntary acts. And if requirement were immediately, voluntary acts would be disliked for him before making up, because that would be delaying the obligatory from its restricted time, and that is disliked. And on this our companions said: If one delays making up Ramadan until another Ramadan enters, there is no feeding required upon one.

And Al-Shafi'i said: Feeding is required upon one. As if he said requirement is immediately, with the concession of delay to another Ramadan. And this is not sound, due to what we mentioned, that there is no indication in the command of specifying the time, so specification would be arbitrary on the evidence. And the statement of feeding is invalid, because it is required as a substitute for fasting when there is inability to achieve it, an inability with which ability is not hoped for usually, as in the case of the decrepit old man. And inability was not found, because one is able to make up, so there is no meaning to requiring feeding.(9)
