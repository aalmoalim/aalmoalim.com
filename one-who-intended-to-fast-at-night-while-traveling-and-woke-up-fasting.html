One Who Intended to Fast at Night While Traveling and Woke Up Fasting

The jurists differed regarding if one intends to fast at night while traveling, and wakes up fasting, without breaking one's resolve before dawn: Is breaking the fast permissible for him or not?

The Hanafis, Malikis, and Shafi'is in one view held that breaking the fast is not permissible for him on that day. And if he breaks his fast, expiation is not required upon him due to doubt.

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Malik used to require making up and expiation upon him. And it was narrated from him that there is no expiation upon him, and this is the view of most of his companions except 'Abd al-Malik, for he said: Whoever breaks his fast by intercourse expiates, because he does not strengthen by that for his travel, and he has no excuse. And on that are the schools of the rest of the jurists of Hijaz and Iraq, that there is no expiation upon him.

Abu 'Umar said: The evidence for the dropping of expiation is clear from the aspect of consideration, because he is interpreting, not violating the sanctity of his fast according to himself. And he is a traveler who has entered into the generality of the permissibility of breaking the fast.(1)

Ibn Juzayy, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Whoever is traveling, and wakes up with the intention of fasting, breaking the fast is not permissible for him except with an excuse, like eating to meet the enemy. And Mutarrif permitted it without an excuse. And on the well-known view: If he breaks his fast, there are three views regarding the requirement of expiation upon him. The third distinguishes between if he breaks his fast by intercourse, so it is required, or by other than it, so it is not required.(2)

However, what is in the commentaries of Khalil, and in "Hashiyat al-Dusuqi": That if one makes the intention to fast at night while traveling, and wakes up fasting in it, then breaks his fast, expiation is required upon him, whether he breaks his fast interpreting or not.

And this is what is explicitly stated in "Al-Mudawwanah", for it came in it: Fasting in travel: (Ibn al-Qasim said) Malik said: Fasting in Ramadan in travel is more beloved to me for whoever is strong for it. (He said) So I said to Malik: What if a man wakes up in travel fasting in Ramadan, then breaks his fast intentionally without an ailment, what is upon him? (He said) Making up with expiation, like one who breaks his fast while resident. (He said): And I asked Malik about this more than once and more than one year, and every time he said to me: Expiation is required upon him. And that is because I saw him or he said to me: He only had the choice to break his fast or to fast. So if he fasts, he does not have the right to exit from it except with an excuse from Allah. So if he breaks his fast intentionally, expiation is required upon him with making up. (He said): So I said to Malik: What if a man wakes up in residence in Ramadan fasting, then travels and breaks his fast on that day? (He said): Only making up a day is required upon him, and I do not like that he break his fast. So if he breaks his fast, only making up a day is required upon him.

(I said): What is the difference between this one who fasted in travel then broke his fast, and this one who fasted in residence then traveled on that day and broke it according to Malik? (He said): Malik said to us, or it was explained to us from him: Because the resident was from the people of fasting, then he went out traveling, so he became from the people of breaking the fast. So from here expiation was dropped from him. And because the traveler was given a choice to break his fast and to fast. So when he chose fasting and abandoned the concession, he became from the people of fasting. So if he breaks his fast, what is upon the people of fasting is required upon him: expiation.

And al-Makhzumi, Ibn Kinanah, and Ashhab said regarding one who fasts in travel in Ramadan then breaks his fast: Making up is required upon him, and expiation is not required upon him, except that Ashhab said: If he interprets that breaking the fast is permitted for him, breaking the fast is permitted for him, because Allah, Glorified and Exalted, has lifted fasting from him. (Ashhab said): And if one wakes up fasting in travel, then enters upon his family during the day and breaks his fast, making up and expiation are required upon him, and no one is excused in this. (And he said) Al-Makhzumi and Ibn Kinanah said regarding one who wakes up in residence fasting, then goes out to travel and breaks his fast on that day: Making up and expiation are required upon him, because fasting was required upon him while resident.(3)

The Shafi'is in their school, the Hanbalis, Ibn 'Abd al-Barr, and Mutarrif from the Malikis held that if one wakes up fasting in travel, then wants to break his fast, breaking the fast is permissible for him without an excuse, because the excuse is present - which is travel - or due to the continuation of the excuse.(4)

And from what they used as evidence is the hadith of Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, went out to Makkah in Ramadan and fasted until he reached al-Kadid, then broke his fast, and the people broke their fast."(5)

And the hadith of Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him:

"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, went out to Makkah in the year of the conquest and fasted until he reached Kuray' al-Ghamim, and the people fasted with him. Then it was said to him: The people have found fasting difficult, and the people are looking at what you have done. So he called for a cup of water after 'Asr and drank, and the people were looking at him. So some of them broke their fast and some of them fasted. Then it reached him that some people had fasted. So he said: 'Those are the disobedient, those are the disobedient.'"(6)

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: And this is an explicit text that does not turn to whoever contradicts it.(7)

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr, may Allah have mercy on him, said: All of these reports clarify for you that the fasting person may break his fast in his travel after entering into fasting, choosing it in Ramadan.(8)

The Hanbalis added that breaking the fast is permissible for him with whatever he wishes, from intercourse and other than it, like eating and drinking, because whoever is permitted to eat is permitted to have intercourse, like one who did not intend. And there is no expiation upon him by intercourse, due to the occurrence of breaking the fast by intention before intercourse, so intercourse occurs after it.(9)

(1) "Al-Tamhid" (22/51), "Radd al-Mukhtar" (2/122, 123), and "Maraqi al-Falah" (369, 374).

(2) "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (82).

(3) "Al-Mudawwanah al-Kubra" (1/201, 202), "Hashiyat al-Dusuqi ma'a al-Sharh al-Kabir" (1/535), "Jawahir al-Iklil" (1/153), "Manh al-Jalil" (1/410), and "Sharh al-Zarqani" (2/213).

(4) "Rawdat al-Talibin" (2/369), "Al-Majmu'" (7/428), "Al-Najm al-Wahhaj" (3/330), "Sharh al-Muhalla 'ala al-Minhaj" (2/64), "Al-Wajiz" (1/103), "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah" (82), "Al-Mughni" (4/143), and "Al-Tamhid" (22/53).

(5) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1842) and Muslim (1113). Al-Kadid: water between 'Usfan and Qudayd.

(6) Sahih hadith: Previously mentioned. Kuray' al-Ghamim: a place in the region of Hijaz between Makkah and Madinah, and it is a valley in front of 'Usfan by eight miles. This Kuray' is a black mountain at the edge of the harrah that extends to it. "Mu'jam al-Buldan" (4/443).

(7) "Al-Mughni" (4/144).

(8) "Al-Tamhid" (22/53).

(9) "Kashaf al-Qina'" (2/312) and "Al-Mughni" (4/144).
