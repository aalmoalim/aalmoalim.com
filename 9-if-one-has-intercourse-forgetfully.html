Intercourse Forgetfully

The jurists differed regarding the ruling for one who has intercourse forgetfully of his fast:

The Hanafis and Shafi'is held that his fast is not invalidated by that, and expiation and making up are not required upon him, due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Whoever eats or drinks forgetfully, let him not break his fast, for it is only provision that Allah provided for him."(1)

And in another narration:

"Whoever forgets while he is fasting and eats or drinks, let him complete his fast, for Allah fed him and gave him drink."(2)

They said: He explicitly mentioned eating and drinking, and we compared to them everything that invalidates the fast, from intercourse and other than it.

The Malikis in their school and Imam Ahmad in one narration held that his fast is invalidated by that, and making up is required upon him, and expiation is not required upon him, due to the statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"And eat and drink until the white thread becomes distinct to you from the black thread of dawn, then complete the fast until night"
[Al-Baqarah 2:187]

And this one did not complete it, and abstaining has become forbidden, so it resembles the intentional act. And because every action for which fasting is not valid regarding something of its kind intentionally in a way that is not valid with forgetfulness, like abandoning the intention.

And the major expiation is not required upon him, because it follows sin, as evidence by its absence with its absence, due to his statement, peace and blessings be upon him: "Error and forgetfulness have been lifted from my Ummah."(3)

The Hanbalis in the apparent school and Imam Malik in a narration narrated from him by Ma'n ibn 'Isa and al-Harawi, and it is the view of Ibn al-Majishun from the Malikis, held that his fast is invalidated and making up and expiation are required upon him, because the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, commanded the one who had intercourse with his wife in Ramadan to expiate, and he did not ask him about intention. And if the situation differed, he would have asked and inquired in detail. And because it is necessary to base the ruling on what the wording of the questioner includes, which is having intercourse with the woman during fasting. And because the question is like the answer in meaning, so it is as if the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: Whoever has intercourse with his wife in Ramadan, let him free a slave.

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: If it is said: In the hadith there is what indicates intention, which is his statement: "I am ruined." And it was narrated: "I am burned."

We say: It is possible that he informed about his being ruined in what he believed regarding intercourse with forgetfulness, from invalidating the fast and his fear of other than that. And because fasting is an act of worship that forbids intercourse, so intention and forgetfulness are equal in it, like Hajj. And because invalidating the fast and the obligation of expiation are two rulings related to intercourse that doubt does not drop, so intention and forgetfulness are equal in them, like the rest of its rulings.(4)

Ibn Rushd, may Allah have mercy on him, said: As for the second issue, which is if one has intercourse forgetfully of his fast, al-Shafi'i and Abu Hanifah say: There is no making up upon him and no expiation. And Malik said: Making up is required upon him without expiation. And Ahmad and the people of zahir said: Making up and expiation are required upon him. The reason for their difference regarding making up for the forgetful person is the contradiction of the apparent meaning of the report in that and analogy.

As for analogy, it is comparing the forgetful person in fasting to the forgetful person in prayer. Whoever compared him to the forgetful person in prayer made making up obligatory upon him, like its obligation by text for the forgetful person in prayer.

As for the report that contradicts this analogy by its apparent meaning, it is what Al-Bukhari and Muslim narrated from Abu Hurayrah, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"Whoever forgets while he is fasting and eats or drinks, let him complete his fast, for Allah fed him and gave him drink."

And this report is testified to by the generality of his statement, peace and blessings be upon him: "Error and forgetfulness and what they are compelled to do have been lifted from my Ummah."

From this category is their difference regarding one who thought that the sun had set and broke his fast, then the sun appeared after that. Is making up required upon him or not? That is because this one is erring, and the erring person and the forgetful person have the same ruling. So however we say, the effect of forgetfulness in dropping making up is clear, and Allah, Glorified and Exalted, knows best.

That is, if we say: The principle is that making up is not required until evidence indicates that, it must be that forgetfulness does not require making up in fasting, since there is no evidence here for that, unlike the matter in prayer.

And if we say: The principle is requiring making up until evidence indicates lifting it from the forgetful person, evidence has indicated in the hadith of Abu Hurayrah lifting it from the forgetful person. However, except if someone says: The evidence that made an exception for the forgetful person in fasting from the forgetful person in the rest of acts of worship for which hardship was lifted from the one who abandons them by text is comparing fasting to prayer. But requiring making up by analogy in it is weak, and making up according to the majority is required by a new command. As for whoever made making up and expiation obligatory upon the one who has intercourse forgetfully, it is weak, for the effect of forgetfulness in dropping punishments is clear in the Shari'ah, and expiation is from the types of punishments. And what led them to that is their taking the general description transmitted in the hadith, I mean that it did not mention in it that he did that intentionally or forgetfully. But whoever made expiation obligatory upon one who kills game forgetfully did not preserve his principle in this, even though the text only came regarding the intentional act. And it was necessary for the people of zahir to take what is agreed upon, which is requiring expiation upon the intentional act, until evidence indicates requiring it upon the forgetful person, or to take the generality of his statement, peace and blessings be upon him: "Error and forgetfulness have been lifted from my Ummah," until evidence indicates specification. But both groups did not adhere to their principle, and there is no evidence in the general what was transmitted from the hadith of the Bedouin. And whoever said from the people of principles: That abandoning detail in different situations from the Lawgiver is like generality in statements, it is weak, for the Lawgiver never ruled except on what is detailed, and generality is only in our regard.(5)

(1) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Al-Tirmidhi (721) and Al-Daraqutni (2/180).

(2) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (1933) and Muslim (809).

(3) Sahih hadith: Its chain was previously mentioned.

(4) "Al-Mughni" (4/187, 179), "Al-Majmu'" (7/533, 536), "Al-Hidayah" (1/122), "Sharh Fath al-Qadir" (2/327, 328), "Mukhtasar al-Quduri" (62), "Al-Mudawwanah al-Kubra" (1/208), "Sharh Muslim" (8/35), "Rawdat al-Talibin" (3/143), "Al-Ikhtiyar" (1/141), and "Al-Qawanin al-Fiqhiyyah".

(5) "Bidayat al-Mujtahid" (1/221, 222).
