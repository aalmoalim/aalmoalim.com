Tenth: Backbiting, Tale-Bearing, Cursing, and Lying

Ibn Hubayrah said: They have agreed - meaning the four imams - that backbiting and lying are disliked for the fasting person, and they do not break his fast, and his fast is valid in ruling,(1) due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"If it is a day of fasting for one of you, let him not engage in obscene speech on that day, nor raise his voice. If someone abuses him or fights him, let him say: 'I am a fasting person...'" Narrated by Al-Bukhari and Muslim from the hadith of Abu Hurayrah.

And due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Whoever does not abandon false speech and acting upon it, then Allah has no need that he abandon his food and drink." Narrated by Al-Bukhari.

Ibn Qudamah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: It is required upon the fasting person to purify his fast from lying, backbiting, and cursing.

Ahmad said: It is appropriate for the fasting person to watch over his fast from his tongue, and not to argue, and to preserve his fast. They used to, when they fasted, sit in the mosques and say: We preserve our fast. And not to backbite anyone, nor to do any action that wounds his fast.(2)

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: If one backbites during one's fast, one disobeys and one's fast is not invalidated according to us. And with this Malik, Abu Hanifah, Ahmad, and all the scholars said, except al-Awza'i, who said: Fasting is invalidated by backbiting, and making up is required. And he used as evidence the hadith of Abu Hurayrah mentioned, and his hadith also that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever does not abandon false speech and acting upon it, then Allah has no need that he abandon his food and drink." Narrated by Al-Bukhari. And from him also, he said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "How many a fasting person has nothing from his fasting except hunger, and how many a person who stands has nothing from his standing except staying awake." Narrated by Al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah in their two Sunans, and Al-Hakim narrated it in "Al-Mustadrak". He said: It is Sahih according to the condition of Al-Bukhari. And from him also, he said: "The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'Fasting is not from eating and drinking. Rather, fasting is from idle talk and obscene speech.'" Narrated by Al-Bayhaqi, and Al-Hakim narrated it and said: It is Sahih according to the condition of Muslim. And with the other hadith: "Five things break the fast of the fasting person: backbiting, tale-bearing, lying, kissing, and the false oath."

Our companions answered regarding these hadiths except the last one, that what is intended is that the perfection of fasting and its desired virtue are only by preserving it from idle talk and bad speech, not that fasting is invalidated by it. (As for) the last hadith: "Five things break the fast of the fasting person," it is a baseless hadith that is not used as evidence. Al-Mawardi, al-Mutawalli, and others answered it, that what is intended is the invalidation of reward, not fasting itself.(3)

It has been mentioned in "Fath al-Qadir" that consensus has occurred that what is intended is the loss of reward, not fasting itself.(4)

(1) "Al-Ifsah" (1/388).

(2) "Al-Mughni" (4/244).

(3) "Al-Majmu'" (7/597).

(4) "Fath al-Qadir" (2/378), "Durar al-Hukkam" (2/477), and "Al-'Inayah" (3/356).
