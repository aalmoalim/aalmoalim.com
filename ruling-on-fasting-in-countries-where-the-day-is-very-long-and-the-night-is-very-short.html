Ruling on Fasting in Countries Where the Day is Very Long and the Night is Very Short

It is known that Allah, Glorified and Exalted, has commanded abstention from things that break the fast from the appearance of dawn until night.

However, in some places such as the Scandinavian countries in northern Europe, they have night for months and day for months. How do the people of those lands fast?

Similarly, if the day is very long and the night is very short such that they are unable to break their fast, how do these people fast? This issue was not known to the early jurists because this did not exist in the lands that were conquered at that time. The first of the jurists to speak about this, as far as I have found, is Imam al-Suyuti, may Allah have mercy on him, from the Shafi'i jurists, and he reported it from scholars who preceded him. Then Imam Ibn 'Abidin from the Hanafi jurists followed him in this.

The form of the issue for them was regarding the ruling on fasting in countries where dawn appears immediately after sunset such that they are unable to break their fast, or if they prayed Maghrib, dawn appeared and they were unable to break their fast.

Imam al-Suyuti, may Allah have mercy on him, answered questions in one hundred verses of poetry, and among them are these verses:

"For those among whom the sun of day does not set except for the duration of prayer, and dawn appears immediately,
And fasting has arrived, so if they pray, they miss from the evening meal what would give them strength for two obligations.
Should they eat to fulfill the obligation of their Maghrib, and what is their ruling regarding the evening meal? Answer me."

He answered:

"As for the fifty-ninth and sixtieth questions: The answer is that al-Burhan al-Fazari issued a fatwa that 'Isha prayer is obligatory in this situation.

His contemporaries issued a fatwa that it is not obligatory upon them, due to the absence of the cause of obligation in their case, which is the time.

The first is supported by the hadith that came regarding the days of the Dajjal, where it said in it: 'Estimate it according to its measure.' Al-Zarkashi said in 'Al-Khadim': And based on this, the ruling for them in Ramadan is that they eat at night until the time of the appearance of dawn in the nearest country to them, then they abstain and break their fast during the day, similarly before sunset if it sets for others, just as Muslims eat and fast during the days of the Dajjal."(1)

Imam Ibn 'Abidin, may Allah have mercy on him, said: I have not seen anyone among us who addressed the ruling on their fasting when dawn appears for them as the sun sets or after it by a time in which the fasting person is unable to eat what would maintain his body, and it is not possible to say that continuous fasting is obligatory upon them because it would lead to destruction.

If we say that fasting is obligatory, it necessitates saying that estimation is required:

Is their night estimated according to the nearest country to them, as the Shafi'is said here as well, or is it estimated for them by what allows eating and drinking, or is only making up (qada') obligatory upon them without performance (ada'), each is possible, so let it be considered.

It is not possible to say here that there is no obligation at all, like 'Isha for those who say so regarding it, because the reason for the absence of obligation in it, according to those who say so, is the absence of the cause, and in fasting the cause has been found, which is witnessing a part of the month and the appearance of dawn each day. This is what appears to me, and Allah, the Exalted, knows best."(2)

This ruling and the preceding discussion is regarding countries where the appearance of dawn occurs with the setting of the sun, and there are three possibilities:

First: Their night is estimated according to the nearest country to them, as the Shafi'is said, and this is a view of the Hanafis.

Second: It is estimated for them by what allows eating and drinking, and this is a view of the Hanafis.

Third: Only making up (qada') is obligatory upon them without performance (ada'), and this is a view of the Hanafis.

Ibn 'Abidin, may Allah have mercy on him, said: Each is possible, so let it be considered.(3)

As for countries where there is night, even if only for an hour or two, as is the case in Norway for example, where they fast for twenty-one hours, so there are three hours of night, this is outside of what the Hanafi and Shafi'i jurists issued fatwas about, and according to their principles, fasting is obligatory upon them, and it is not estimated according to the nearest country to them.(4)

As for countries where the day is half the year and the night is half the year, estimation is required in this, as in the hadith of the Dajjal that Muslim and others narrated from al-Nawwas ibn Sam'an, may Allah be pleased with him, who said:

"We said: 'O Messenger of Allah, how long will he remain on earth?' He said: 'Forty days: a day like a year, a day like a month, a day like a week, and the rest of his days are like your days.' We said: 'O Messenger of Allah, for that day which is like a year, will the prayer of one day suffice us in it?' He said: 'No, estimate it according to its measure.'"(5)

Imam al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "As for their statement: 'O Messenger of Allah, for that day which is like a year, will the prayer of one day suffice us in it?' He said: 'No, estimate it according to its measure.'" Al-Qadi and others said: This is a specific ruling for that day that the Lawgiver legislated for us. They said: Were it not for this hadith and we were left to our ijtihad, we would have limited it to the five prayers at the known times in other days. The meaning of "estimate it according to its measure"

is that if after the appearance of dawn there passes the amount that is between it and Zuhr each day, then pray Zuhr. Then if after it there passes the amount that is between it and 'Asr, then pray 'Asr. Then if after this there passes the amount that is between it and Maghrib, then pray Maghrib. Similarly 'Isha, then Fajr, then Zuhr, then 'Asr, then Maghrib, and so on until that day ends, and there have occurred in it the prayers of a year, all obligatory prayers performed in their times. As for the second which is like a month, and the third which is like a week, the analogy of the first day is that it should be estimated for them like the first day according to what we mentioned, and Allah, Glorified and Exalted, knows best.(6)

Also, Imam al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, mentioned a similar issue to this regarding a prisoner and the like who does not know night from day, what should he do? He said, may Allah have mercy on him:

Branch: If a prisoner and the like does not know night or day, but darkness continues for him constantly, this is an important issue that few have mentioned. Imam Abu Bakr al-Marwazi from our companions reported three views of the companions regarding it:

First: He fasts and makes it up, because it is a rare excuse.

Second: He does not fast, because certainty in intention is not achieved with ignorance of the time.

Third: He makes an effort to determine (taharri) and fasts, and does not make it up, like the day of clouds in prayer.

I say: The most correct is that he is required to make an effort to determine and fast, and there is no making up upon him. This is if error does not become apparent to him afterward. If it becomes clear that he encountered night, making up is obligatory upon him without disagreement, and Allah, the Exalted, knows best.(7)

(1) "Al-Hawi li al-Fatawa" (2/284, 304).

(2) "Hashiyat Ibn 'Abidin" (1/366).

(3) "Hashiyat Ibn 'Abidin" (1/366).

(4) This is what came in the decision of the Islamic Fiqh Council that fasting must be observed at dawn and at sunset as long as there is a valid entry of dawn and a valid setting of the sun in the day and night.

As for the Egyptian Dar al-Ifta, it issues fatwas relying on the fasting of the people of Makkah or Madinah, so it is estimated according to them, and some contemporary scholars issue fatwas that fasting is estimated for them according to the nearest Islamic country to them.

(6) "Sharh Sahih Muslim" (18/66).

(7) "Al-Majmu'" (6/291).
