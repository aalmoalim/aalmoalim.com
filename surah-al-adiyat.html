Everything You Need to Know About Surah Al-Adiyat (The Assaulters)

Introduction: Surah Al-Adiyat presents a powerful contrast between two types of people: those who rush forward in jihad (struggle) for the sake of Allah, and those who are miserly and ungrateful. The surah begins with a vivid cinematic scene of horses racing in battle - starting with sound, then light, then the full visual scene. It then contrasts the generous mujahid (warrior) who overcomes all obstacles with the miserly person who hoards wealth and is ungrateful to Allah. The surah emphasizes that the greatest obstacle to serving Allah is not external barriers, but internal ones - particularly the love of wealth.

The Opening: A Cinematic Scene of Battle

وَالْعَادِيَاتِ ضَبْحًا
"By the racers, panting"
[Al-Adiyat 100:1]

The surah opens with a powerful oath by Allah, swearing by horses racing in battle. The scene begins with sound - the panting (ضَبْحًا - dabhan) of horses running at full speed. This sound only appears when horses run at extreme speed, when they cannot even stop to breathe properly. The word "adiat" (racers) refers to horses that run and charge in battle for the sake of Allah.

فَالْمُورِيَاتِ قَدْحًا
"And the producers of sparks [when] striking"
[Al-Adiyat 100:2]

Then light appears - sparks (قَدْحًا - qadhan) produced when the horses' hooves strike against stones in the darkness of night. These sparks are visible only in darkness, indicating that the horses have been running throughout the night.

فَالْمُغِيرَاتِ صُبْحًا
"And the raiders at dawn"
[Al-Adiyat 100:3]

Finally, dawn breaks and the full scene is revealed - the raiders (الْمُغِيرَاتِ - al-mughirat) charging at dawn. The Prophet (peace be upon him) used to wait for dawn before launching attacks, and if he didn't hear the call to prayer, he would attack.

فَأَثَرْنَ بِهِ نَقْعًا
"Stirring up thereby [clouds of] dust"
[Al-Adiyat 100:4]

They stir up clouds of dust (نَقْعًا - naq'an) in the enemy's territory, indicating that they have penetrated deep into enemy lines, not just skirmishing on the edges.

فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا
"Arriving thereby in the center collectively"
[Al-Adiyat 100:5]

They penetrate into the very center of the enemy forces (جَمْعًا - jam'an), showing their courage and determination. They don't fight on the edges - they go straight to the heart of the enemy.

The Cinematic Opening: The surah uses a brilliant literary technique - starting with sound (panting), then light (sparks), then the full visual scene (dawn), then action (dust and penetration). This creates a vivid, immersive experience that draws the reader into the scene of battle.

The Contrast: The Ungrateful Human

إِنَّ الْإِنْسَانَ لِرَبِّهِ لَكَنُودٌ
"Indeed, mankind, to his Lord, is ungrateful"
[Al-Adiyat 100:6]

After describing the heroic mujahid, the surah suddenly shifts to describe the opposite - the ungrateful human (لَكَنُودٌ - kanud). The word "kanud" means ungrateful, miserly, and unwilling to give. This person:

  • Does not thank Allah for His blessings
  • Does not use his wealth for the sake of Allah
  • Hoards everything for himself
  • Is like barren land that produces nothing no matter how much water you give it
وَإِنَّهُ عَلَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ لَشَهِيدٌ
"And indeed, he is to that a witness"
[Al-Adiyat 100:7]

And indeed, he is a witness to this - he knows in his heart that he is ungrateful, but he suppresses this knowledge and continues in his miserliness.

وَإِنَّهُ لِحُبِّ الْخَيْرِ لَشَدِيدٌ
"And indeed, he is, in love of wealth, intense"
[Al-Adiyat 100:8]

And indeed, he is intense in his love of wealth (الْخَيْرِ - al-khayr, meaning wealth). The word "shadid" (intense) can also mean "bound" or "chained" - as if he is chained by his love of wealth, unable to move or give.

The Greatest Obstacle: The surah identifies the greatest obstacle to serving Allah - not external barriers like darkness, stones, or enemies, but internal ones - particularly the love of wealth that chains a person and prevents them from giving or sacrificing.

The Reminder: The Day of Judgment

أَفَلَا يَعْلَمُ إِذَا بُعْثِرَ مَا فِي الْقُبُورِ
"Then does he not know when the contents of the graves are scattered"
[Al-Adiyat 100:9]

Does he not know that when the contents of the graves are scattered - when all the dead are resurrected - his wealth will be of no use to him?

وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ
"And that within the breasts is obtained"
[Al-Adiyat 100:10]

And that what is within the breasts (all secrets, intentions, and hidden thoughts) will be extracted and revealed - nothing will remain hidden.

إِنَّ رَبَّهُم بِهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَّخَبِيرٌ
"Indeed, their Lord with them, that Day, is [fully] Aware"
[Al-Adiyat 100:11]

Indeed, their Lord is fully aware of them on that Day - aware of their deeds, intentions, and everything they did or failed to do.

Key Lessons and Insights

1. The Two Models

The surah presents two models: the mujahid who overcomes all obstacles (darkness, stones, enemies) to serve Allah, and the miserly person who is chained by love of wealth and cannot give even a small amount.

2. Obstacles Are Meant to Be Overcome

The mujahid faces many obstacles (darkness, stones, exhaustion, enemies) but continues forward. Obstacles are not reasons to stop - they are meant to be overcome.

3. The Greatest Obstacle Is Internal

The greatest obstacle is not external (darkness, enemies, etc.) but internal - the love of wealth that chains a person and prevents them from giving or sacrificing.

4. Continuous Movement

The surah emphasizes continuous movement - the horses don't stop, they keep running from one battle to the next. The believer should always be in motion, moving from one good deed to another.

5. The Day of Accountability

Everything will be revealed on the Day of Judgment - all deeds, intentions, and secrets. Allah is fully aware of everything.

Conclusion

Surah Al-Adiyat emphasizes that:

  • We should be like the mujahid - continuously moving forward, overcoming obstacles, and serving Allah
  • We should not be like the miserly person - chained by love of wealth, unable to give or sacrifice
  • Obstacles are meant to be overcome, not reasons to stop
  • The greatest obstacle is internal - the love of wealth
  • Everything will be accounted for on the Day of Judgment

The surah calls believers to break free from the chains of miserliness and love of wealth, and to be like the mujahid who continuously moves forward in service of Allah, overcoming all obstacles.
