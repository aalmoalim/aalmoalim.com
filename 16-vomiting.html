Vomiting

The people of knowledge have unanimously agreed that if vomiting overcomes the fasting person (1), his fast is valid and there is nothing upon him:

Ibn al-Mundhir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: They have unanimously agreed that there is nothing upon the fasting person if vomiting overcomes him.(2)

Imam Ibn Battal, may Allah have mercy on him, said: The jurists have unanimously agreed that whoever is overcome by vomiting, there is no making up upon him,(3) due to the statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

"Whoever is overcome by vomiting, there is no making up upon him, and whoever vomits intentionally, let him make it up."(4)

As for one who vomits intentionally, Ibn al-Mundhir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: They have unanimously agreed on invalidating the fast of one who vomits intentionally,(5) due to the previous hadith, and due to what Fadalah ibn 'Ubayd narrated: "The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, called for a drink, so some of us said to him: Did you not wake up fasting, O Messenger of Allah? He said: Yes, but I vomited."(6)

And in another wording from Fadalah ibn 'Ubayd al-Ansari, he reports: "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, came out to us on a day he was fasting, then called for a vessel and drank. So we said: O Messenger of Allah, this is a day you used to fast. He said: Yes, but I vomited."(7)

However, I found Ibn Hubayrah in "Al-Ifsah" reporting a difference regarding what breaks the fast from it. He, may Allah have mercy on him, said: They differed regarding if one vomits intentionally.

Malik and al-Shafi'i said: He breaks his fast.

Abu Hanifah said: He does not break his fast, except if it fills his mouth.

And it was narrated from Ahmad various narrations regarding vomiting that breaks ablution and breaks the fast:

One of them: He does not break his fast except by the excessive amount of it, and this is the well-known view.

The second: By a mouthful.

The third: By what was in half the mouth.

And from him is another fourth narration regarding breaking ablution by vomiting, whether little or much. And it is also regarding breaking the fast, except that vomiting that invalidates the fast, despite the difference in his school regarding its description, his school did not differ in requiring intention in it.(8)

Ibn Battal, may Allah have mercy on him, said: They differed regarding one who vomits intentionally. Malik, al-Layth, al-Thawri, Abu Hanifah, al-Shafi'i, and Ahmad said: Whoever vomits intentionally, making up is required upon him, and this is the view of the majority. This was narrated from 'Ali, Ibn 'Umar, and Abu Hurayrah.

Al-Awza'i and Abu Thawr said: Making up and expiation are required upon him, like the expiation of one who eats intentionally in Ramadan. This is the view of 'Ata'. And they used as evidence the hadith of al-Awza'i from Ya'ish ibn al-Walid ibn Hisham that his father narrated to him, who said: Ma'dan ibn Talhah narrated to us that Abu al-Darda' narrated to him: "The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, vomited and broke his fast." They said: And if vomiting breaks the fast of the fasting person, then upon whoever does it intentionally is what is upon whoever eats and drinks and has intercourse intentionally: making up and expiation. And the jurists interpreted this hadith. They said: The meaning of "vomited" is "vomited intentionally." Al-Tahawi said: And it is possible that his statement "vomited and broke his fast" means: vomited and became weak, so he broke his fast. And this is possible in the language. And Muhammad ibn Ishaq narrated this meaning from Yazid ibn Abi Habib from Abu Marzuq from Fadalah ibn 'Ubayd: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, called for a vessel and drank. So it was said: O Messenger of Allah, this is a day you used to fast. He said: "Yes, I vomited and broke my fast." And this meaning is: But I vomited and became weak from fasting, so I broke my fast. And there is no evidence in these two hadiths that vomiting was what broke his fast. Rather, in them is that he vomited and broke his fast after that.(9)

(1) "Overcomes him" (ذَرَعَهُ): means it preceded and overcame him in coming out. "Al-Nihayah" (2/185).

(2) "Al-Ijma'" (33). See: "Al-Ifsah" (1/391), "Bidayat al-Mujtahid" (1/401), "Al-Majmu'" (7/526, 527), "Al-Istidhkar" (3/347), and "Mukhtasar al-Quduri" (62).

(3) "Sharh Sahih al-Bukhari" (4/80).

(4) Sahih hadith: Narrated by Abu Dawud (2380), Al-Tirmidhi (716), and Ibn Majah (1676).

(5) "Al-Ijma'" (33).

(6) Narrated by Imam Ahmad (23993).

(7) Weak hadith: Narrated by Ibn Majah (1675).

(8) "Al-Ifsah" (1/390). See: "Al-Mughni" (4/172, 173).

(9) "Sharh Sahih al-Bukhari" (4/80, 81).
