Ruling on Fasting the Month of Ramadan

The Ummah has unanimously agreed that fasting the month of Ramadan is an obligatory duty (fard wajib) and a pillar from the pillars of Islam, and that whoever denies and rejects it is a disbeliever. The evidence for its obligation comes from the Book (Quran), the Sunnah, consensus (ijma'), and rational reasoning (ma'qul).

As for the Book (Quran):

The statement of Allah, the Exalted:

"O you who believe! Fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may become righteous. [Fasting for] a limited number of days. So whoever among you is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days. And upon those who are able [to fast, but with hardship] - a ransom [as substitute] of feeding a poor person [each day]. And whoever volunteers good [i.e., excess] - it is better for him. But to fast is best for you, if you only knew. The month of Ramadan [is that] in which was revealed the Quran, a guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion. So whoever sights [the new moon of] the month, let him fast it; and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days. Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship and [wants] for you to complete the period and to glorify Allah for that [to] which He has guided you; and perhaps you will be grateful."
[Al-Baqarah 2:183-185]

His statement: "has been prescribed for you" (kutiba 'alaykum), meaning: it has been made obligatory.

As for the Sunnah:

1 - What was narrated by the two Shaykhs (Al-Bukhari and Muslim) from Ibn 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"Islam is built upon five [pillars]: testifying that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing prayer, giving zakah, performing Hajj, and fasting Ramadan."(1)

(1) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (8) and Muslim (16).

2 - What they also narrated from Talhah ibn 'Ubaydillah: that a Bedouin came to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, with disheveled hair, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, inform me what Allah has made obligatory upon me regarding prayer?" He said: "The five prayers, unless you perform voluntary prayers." He said: "Inform me what Allah has made obligatory upon me regarding fasting?" He said: "The month of Ramadan, unless you perform voluntary fasting." He said: "Inform me what Allah has made obligatory upon me regarding zakah?" He said: So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, informed him of the laws of Islam. He said: "By the One who has honored you, I will not perform any voluntary acts, nor will I decrease anything that Allah has made obligatory upon me." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "He will succeed if he is truthful, or he will enter Paradise if he is truthful."(1)

(1) Narrated by Al-Bukhari (46/1762) and Muslim (11).

As for Consensus (Ijma'):

Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "That fasting Ramadan is a pillar and an obligation is a matter of consensus, and the evidences from the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus all point to this. They have unanimously agreed that no other fasting is obligatory."(2)

Ibn Hubayrah, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "They have unanimously agreed that fasting the month of Ramadan is one of the pillars of Islam and an obligation from its obligations."(3)

Al-Kasani, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "The Ummah has unanimously agreed on the obligation of fasting the month of Ramadan; none denies it except a disbeliever."(4)

(2) "Al-Majmu'" (7/413).

(3) "Al-Ifsah" (1/380) and "Al-Mughni" (4/116).

(4) "Al-Bada'i'" (2/578).

As for Rational Reasoning (Ma'qul):

It is from several aspects:

The first: Fasting is a means to gratitude for blessings; for it is restraining the self from food, drink, and sexual intercourse, and these are among the greatest and highest blessings. Abstaining from them for a considerable period makes one recognize their value, for blessings are unknown to him, so when they are lost, they become known. This compels him to fulfill their right through gratitude, and gratitude for blessings is obligatory both rationally and legally. Allah, the Exalted, alluded to this in His statement in the verse of fasting: "and perhaps you will be grateful" [Al-Baqarah 2:185].

The second: It is a means to righteousness (taqwa); because when one's soul submits to abstaining from what is lawful, hoping for the pleasure of Allah, the Exalted, and fearing His painful punishment, then it is more appropriate that it submit to abstaining from what is unlawful. Thus, fasting becomes a cause for avoiding the prohibitions of Allah, the Exalted, and it is an obligation. This is what is alluded to in His statement at the beginning of the verse on fasting: "that you may become righteous" [Al-Baqarah 2:183].

The third: In fasting there is subduing the nature and breaking the desires; for when the soul is satiated, it desires pleasures, and when it is hungry, it refrains from what it desires. Therefore, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

"O young men! Whoever among you is able to marry, let him marry, for it is more lowering of the gaze and more protective of the private parts. And whoever is not able, then let him fast, for it is a shield for him."(1)

Thus, fasting is a means to abstaining from sins, and it is an obligation.(2)

(1) Narrated by Al-Bukhari in the Book of Marriage, chapter: "Whoever is not able to marry, let him fast" (4771), and Muslim in the Book of Marriage (1400).

(2) "Bada'i' al-Sana'i'" (2/578).

The fourth: Fasting causes mercy and compassion toward the poor; for when the fasting person tastes the pain of hunger at some times, he remembers those who are in this state at all times, and compassion and mercy toward them hasten to him through being good to them, thus attaining what is with Allah, the Exalted, of good reward.

The fifth: In fasting there is conformity with the poor by bearing what they bear at times, and in that is the elevation of one's status with Allah, the Exalted.(1)

(1) "Fath al-Qadir" (2/233).
